# C-144 evidence - asking

## A DCP Identify through the listener (2026-11-24)

Phase 5's first piece. Absorbed from Veredas 1's `active.rs` (Identify-All, a transaction id from
the clock, a response delay of 1) and its server's call of it, changed on the way:
- **Only through a listener already hearing the interface**, from that interface's MAC: the plan's
  "it listens before it asks"; Veredas 1 sent from its one sender.
- **The listener says what became of the request** (`Listener::send_tracked`, an outcome on a
  channel: kept as sent with its index and stamp, or refused with the reason). The first draft
  looked for its request among the kept frames and, under a small bound, found it let go before
  it looked and said it was never sent (a test of twelve frames lost found it).
- **Answers matched to the request**: Identify answers kept from that interface after the
  request, to its MAC under its transaction id; the others there counted apart, frames let go
  before they were read counted lost. Veredas 1 fused every answer and matched none.
- **Bounded**: DCP's delay factors (1 to 6400), a minute's wait at most, the interface's queue.
- The transaction id counts from a start drawn from the clock and the process id, so two scanners
  on one interface do not share one.

Proven against the bench (fixture first) on a bridge in a private network: the namespace allows a
bridge (the module loaded on the host), forwarding DCP's group addresses and keeping LLDP to
itself, so one segment holds two of maquinar's devices of one station name, one announcing itself
and one silent until asked.

Before the first commit, 25 mutants of `ask.rs`: 19 killed by the first tests; five more once
tests were added (a frame this machine sends while it waits, a second interface on the hub, an
answer under another frame id and one of a request's service type, a queue at one a second); one
more once the refusal of an ended worker was timed (immediate, not after the turn). One survived:
the check that the worker ended while the request waited its turn. C-142's first refute round
replaced it with the worker telling the asker that its request was never sent, which `identify`
says as an ended interface; a test of a worker ending behind a queued frame kills its mutant.

## The first refute round, answered (2026-11-25)

The critic (its own copy, probes of concurrent asks, stalled and panicking wires and a streaming
flood, 27 mutants, three of the shared codecs run with the bench rebuilt from the same tree) found
the matching killed by the hub test's distractions and refuted on:
- **A request still waiting its turn was said not sent, then sent anyway**: the turn was counted
  from the queue as read before the request was queued, in another lock, so five askers at once
  undercounted it; and a request whose asker gave up stayed queued. The listener now says, as it
  queues a tracked frame, how many are ahead of it, and lets a waiting frame be withdrawn: past
  its turn a request still waiting is withdrawn (never sent), and one its wire is still taking is
  said so (`Stalled`: it may yet go, and no answer to it is this ask's).
- **A worker that panicked as it handed the request to its wire was said a timeout**: the frame in
  hand was dropped untold. The listener holds a frame being handed over where the worker's end
  tells it too (C-142).
- **The bench half took on trust what the bench and the scanner share** (the MACs read through
  `RawLink::mac`, the addresses through aranha's codec, the requests barely dissected): the test
  sets the three MACs, reads each request's source, destination, id, delay and filter and each
  answer's name, address and mask through the dissectors against what the test asked and the bench
  file states.
- **`lost` was counted and the frames read under two locks**: a frame let go in between was
  neither read nor counted; the listener reads both at one moment (`read_from`), proven by a
  device streaming answers through the read.
- **The spread and the wait were bounded only from below**: the window is said with the answers
  and asserted, and the time taken bounded above too.
- **The answer's stamp was asserted against a clock at 0**: the hub's clock is set.
- **A wait of exactly a minute** is tested taken; the guards the code has beyond the clause's words
  (the PROFINET EtherType, an Identify service, an uncut frame) are named in the clause and tested
  with a Get's answer under the answer frame id, an answer under another EtherType and a cut one.

Of 20 mutants of the answer's paths in `ask.rs` and `listen.rs`, built outside the suite lock and
run under one hold, all are killed (the streamed flood once the stream ran through the read); the
three shared-code mutants run in place with the bench rebuilt from each are reported below.
The three shared-code mutants, run in place with the bench rebuilt from each mutated tree (a MAC
read short on both ends, address and mask swapped on both ends, another Identify group address):
all killed.

## The second refute round, answered (2026-11-26, resumed after the morning's reboot)

The critic (its own copies, a deadline-race probe, a flood with no pause, hand-made frames through
tshark, 19 mutants) found the matching, the refusals and the waits holding, and refuted:
- **A request never sent was said still being taken** (`Stalled`): its worker ended (a panic) as
  its turn ran out while another reader held the listener, so the worker's end emptied the queue
  before the asker's withdrawal, which then failed and went straight to `Stalled` without reading
  the outcome told meanwhile. A failed withdrawal now reads it (sent, refused or never sent, each
  said as such; only nothing told is `Stalled`). Writing the test found the rest of it: the
  listener told the outcome after it let go of its lock, so a withdrawal could fail before the
  telling; every outcome is now told before the lock goes (C-142).
- **The bench half read the frame ids and the filter's shape only through the shared codec**: the
  dissectors read each request's frame id, service and one filter block, each Hello's frame id, and
  each answer's frame id, destination and transaction id against the request this machine sent.
- **"Counted at the moment they are read" was proven by chance** (the hub device paused between
  answers): a wire floods answers with no pause.
- **"Withdrawn and never sent" was checked before a frame left queued would have gone**: the
  stalling wire counts the frames it is handed, read well past that time.

Of eight mutants of the answer, run in place with the bench rebuilt from each mutated tree (no
read after a failed withdrawal, never-sent told after the lock, the lost and the read apart, a
withdrawn frame left queued, the request under the Get/Set frame id, answers under the request's,
an all-selector beside the filter, a send's outcome told after the lock), seven are killed. The
last survives: the window it opens (a sent frame kept, its outcome not yet told) is reached only
when an asker's failed withdrawal falls inside it, where a false "still being taken" cannot be
told from a true one by any test; the code tells every outcome under the lock, so the window is
closed by construction.

## An ordering the listener never promised (2026-11-27)

The orchestrator's run of the whole suite saw `an_identify_is_answered_by_what_it_matches_and_only_
its_answers_count` red once at `ask.rs:182` (green 15 times alone): it asserted the device's name
first witnessed after the request. The device's answer is heard on lan0 and on lan1 (one hub);
lan0's worker hands the request to the wire and only then keeps it as sent, so lan1's worker may
keep its copy of the answer first. A 30 ms pause put between the wire's send and the keep (not
committed) made it fail every time. The clause's answers (lan0's, after the request: one worker
sends and hears) are unchanged; the case now asserts the name first witnessed by a frame of the
device's, kept on lan0 after the request or as its copy on lan1, and held five runs of five under
the same pause. The plan's traps name it.

## The third refute round, answered (2026-11-27)

The critic (its own copy at 975d70c4, seeded and its links repointed; ten probes of its own on
hubs; the pause between the wire's send and the keep, three runs of the whole file, bench
included, green; 19 mutants) found the order across interfaces holding and refuted on the code and
the test:
- **Code: answers the listener never fused.** The ask matched by rules of its own: any odd service
  type byte (its selection bit alone; DCP defines 1 and 5), a group source address, and a frame
  from this machine's other wire's MAC were each said answered, while the listener left each out
  of fusion (damage, a group address, this machine's own). The ask now takes an answer only where
  the listener's decoding (C-140) gives an Identify answer of it, from none of this machine's MACs
  (the listener says them, `Listener::own`): the clause's "the listener has fused every answer"
  holds of every one.
- **Test: ten mutants passed it**: the wait in whole seconds or at a minute and a half, tagged or
  unsupported answers dropped, the window overrun by 80 ms, the second more shrunk behind frames
  queued ahead, answers to a group address counted, one answer kept per MAC, an earlier ask's id
  taken, the request always at factor 1. Each has a case now (`round_3`), the three that measure
  time pinned on the machine with the other cases of the pace.
Its 19 mutants (the one keyed to the old match moved onto the new rule) and two of the answer's
own (the own-MAC check dropped, the ask's old rule put back) replayed against the answer, each
first against the cases that measure no time and, alive, against the timed ones under the
machine's lock (results under `~/.cache/veredas/aranha/mutants/c144r3-answer/`): 20 of 21 killed,
the listener's own sends counted only once a case had the listener send a frame forged as another
device's answer (the own-MAC check had hidden the sends whose source is this machine's). The one
left is the critic's equivalent (A17: a read after a failed withdrawal waiting 100 ms says the
same, later).
The answer's first run of the reachable suite failed `asking_is_refused_where_it_cannot_be_heard_
sent_or_bounded` once: its worker that panics as it hands the request over was told after the
request's turn (1.05 s), so the ask said its wire still taking it. The panic is caught and told
at once; only a worker unscheduled for over a second under the suite's load (and other sessions'
builds) misses the turn. The case times the turn's outcomes (two-second stalls, a refusal
within 900 ms): it is pinned on the machine now, with the pace's other cases.
Pinned, the refusals case failed once more under the reachable suite: its queue of one, filled at
one frame a second, held room for the ask, which was then not refused as a full queue. Filled at
once, the queue could end its loop before the worker took the first frame (within its 20 ms poll),
and the worker take it in the instant before the ask. The first frame is now sent before the
queue is filled: the next turn is a whole second away, and the queue stays full while the ask is
refused. Three runs under the lock passed.
