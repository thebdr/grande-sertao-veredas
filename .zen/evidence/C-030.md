# C-030 evidence - the controller file

## The design (2026-11-06)

The third plain-text file schema (after the channel file, C-017, and the catalog, C-028), on
the same pattern, and the first whose reader takes another file as its context: every message a
controller file names is checked against the catalog, and every list of fields against the names
the message writes, so a rules file that loads can build every message it sends.

The reference, `docs/controller-schema.md`, holds three TOML blocks: a catalog of six messages
(the catalog schema's three and a command, a heartbeat and a status line), the machine's rules
and the unit's rules, the two sides of one conversation. The test loads all three, reads the
prose's numbers and holds them against the loaded rules, walks the eighteen tables' keys (the
reader's own key lists, each key's required or default statement), pins every refusal of the
closing paragraph by its message, quotes every loading sentence beside its pin, and, as the
catalog test does since C-028 round 3, holds a copy of the reference's text, paragraph by
paragraph, so an edit to the reference is an edit to the test.

Two reader changes were made while writing the engine's test: `from` reads an earlier field of
its own list before the context (the reference said fields see the earlier ones, and `from` in
an announcement had nothing else to read), and the reply's fields join the item trimmed (an
outcome reading `item` from a padded reply wrote the padding into its text).

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-06): refuted, seven gaps

The key walk, the header, the fifty refusals and the copy held under the critic's mutations;
seven things did not. A source's place was pinned once each, so `decide` accepted in an outcome
or a cyclic message, and `from` of the destination in a cyclic message that is not per
destination, passed; the bounds were pinned outside only, so off-by-one mutants passed; the
unit's decision was pinned by prose alone (the compiled decision was private, and its rule and
reject were both 0); a reader ignoring a field's decimals passed; padded names loaded in every
place but a message's; `initial` keys were trimmed, so ` 1` and `1` named one destination twice
and the later won silently; a reply key in a repeat's numbered names was refused. Now every
place refuses what it does not take, pinned; the included ends load, pinned; `Fields::decide`
reads a decision back and the unit's is compared with the prose, its reject now 9 (every chute
full gives it, pinned in the engine); a field's decimals reach its draw; a name is exact in
every place (a register, a bit, an `initial` key, a field, a fault, an outcome); a binary
reply's key may be a repeat's numbered name, never its own name or one past its max.

## Reconcile (2026-11-07): the catalog says what a message reads

The near-duplicate check against C-028 found the reply-key rule re-derived in the bench
(`message_reads`, a copy of the catalog's numbered-name parsing) and a text reply's key never
checked. Now the catalog answers (`Message::reads`: a binary field, a payload group, a repeat's
numbered name) and the reader asks it for the reply's key and destination, binary or text; the
reference's `key` and `destination` say so, and state that a disposition's, a rule's, a
command's and a sticky key's field are looked up when the message comes. C-030 cites C-028.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-07): refuted, five gaps

The round-1 fixes held under 31 mutations (places, decimals, both ends of every bound, the
reject, the names, `reads` in both halves, `DecideView` in every part). Five things did not:
an `initial` key `01` or `+1` parsed as 1 beside `1` and the later won silently; a range at 0
decimals holding no whole number (1.5 to 1.7) loaded and drew outside it; a text reply's
destination was never pinned; a command's empty field was refused at load while the clause said
nothing is, and a disposition's, a rule's and a sticky key's empty names loaded; the excluded
ends next to the included ones were unpinned. Now an `initial` key must print as its number,
a whole-number range is required at 0 decimals, an empty field name is refused in all four
places, the text reply's destination and the adjacent excluded ends are pinned, a forced field
no outcome renders is refused (the critic's aside), and the full file's decision is read back
with a rule's value apart from its reject.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-07): refuted on the forced fields without an outcome

The five round-2 fixes held under nineteen mutations and eighteen probes (`01`, `1.0`, `1 `,
`-0`, negative and zero-width ranges, blank names in all four places, both forced-field checks).
A blocking bit's forced fields were checked only when an outcome existed, so a file with none
(the unit's kind, or items with a reply only) loaded a misspelled `set`; and two pins needed a
second case (a later blocking entry, a rule field of blanks). Now a forced field with no
outcome at all is refused ("there is no outcome to force"), both pinned, the rule pin uses
blanks, and, from the critic's aside, a whole range must lie within the 64-bit integers a draw
takes (`1e300` refused at 0 decimals, loading at one).

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-07): refuted on one bound

The no-outcome check held in every order and shape, and every mutation of round 3 was caught.
The 64-bit bound was pinned on one side only and off by one at the top (`i64::MAX as f64` is
2^63, one past the largest integer, so a range at 2^63 loaded and drew 2^63 - 1 outside it). The
top is now excluded; pinned at 2^63 refused, -2^63 loading, -1e300 refused and 3e9 loading.

## Refute round 5 (2026-11-07): holds

Five mutations of the bound (at 32 bits, the low side dropped, each boundary the other way,
the high side dropped) all fail the rules test, each on a named pin; no earlier gap is open.
