# C-202 evidence - the engine, live

## The design (2026-11-22)

`docs/comissionador-plan.md`, "How a run meets the plant", carried out:

- **One door to portal** (`plant::Plant`), its one implementation over a session's `Live` in this
  process (Q313 taken): the catalog from `DuckStore::list_tables` (a table with portal's time
  column, its value columns typed from the engine's own type names), rows of a window from
  `query_rows` (newest first, paged by the store's cap, turned oldest first), the latest value of a
  column from the newest rows back past holes, a write through `Live::opc_handle(..).write_tags`
  (the tag found by its rule's name and its series in the compiled spec, the value coerced by
  `serve::coerce`, a refusal read as "may have reached the plant" by `serve::refused_code` 504),
  a send through `Live::channel_handle(..).send`, the switch read by `Live::writes_armed`. Nothing
  of the OPC UA stack is named in the crate (C-200 refuses the `opc` path): its types are reached
  through the spec's fields.
- **The clock is the store's axis.** A wait gathers every row of the tables its condition reads,
  from the clock on, in stamp order, applies each to the values it holds (initialised from the
  latest at the clock) and evaluates at that row's stamp; the first stamp at which it holds is the
  step's end. An await takes the first row stamped after the clock whose `where` holds, the row
  bound to its name while the condition is judged. Live, the rows are read up to 250 ms before
  the machine's now (the pump flushes every 50 ms), polled every 50 ms; a write moves the clock to
  the moment the plant answered. A replay reads the same way over the window, the window's end
  its horizon (C-203).
- **Outcomes and routes** as the reference says; a retry counted per route (the step and the
  route's place in the file) per run, never reset (Q316); spent, the step fails with the reason,
  routed once more by its `on fail` unless that line was the retry spent.
- **Bounds:** the run's `timeout` bounds every wait without its own; a step the run stops inside
  (the tester's stop, the bound, a replay's window) is recorded with no outcome; a run of more
  than 100 000 steps, or a script or a call of more than ten million statements, ends, so no file
  hangs the engine.
- **Shown values** are the suite's (core's `Value` displays a float by Rust's shortest round trip):
  the worked example's `margin` is `0.050000000000000044`.

## What the build met

- **The worked example's function follows destination 4.** A fixture whose replies name another
  destination makes `ItemRoute` wait out its 60 s bound: no outcome for 4 ever comes. The test of
  the failing path calls it with the destination the replies name.
- **A name nothing assigns is the check's, not the run's.** `report ghost` is refused before any
  run; a report's "no value yet" is for a name the path taken never reached.
- **`/` gives a number.** A function whose output is `int` and computes `x / 2` ends in error at
  the output's type; `x - 4` stays whole.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-23): refuted, seven findings, all closed

1. **The binding missed columns read through an awaited row** (`await announce where a.nosuch ==
   2 -> a`, or `o.dest` in a function): the run was bound, a write before it landed, and the await
   failed in `error`. The binding now follows every `await`'s name (in the steps, a script box's
   names being the test's, a name assigned from it; within each function) to its table and
   refuses a column that table lacks, the rows' own fields aside.
2. **A call inside a call did not keep its caller's bound**: an inner function with no timeout
   awaited until the run's bound (50 s) under an outer one of 300 ms. A call's bound is its own
   timeout within its caller's: the calling function's, or the run's.
3. **A loop of no statement ignored the tester's stop and the run's bound** (`for i in 1 ..
   100000000000 { }`): only statements were counted. Every turn of a loop is counted as a
   statement, the stop looked at every time and the run's bound every 1024. The budget of
   10 000 000 is now per script box or step's call, as stated, where it ran per run.
4. **Durations and moments past their range panicked in a debug build** (and wrapped in a release
   one): their arithmetic is checked, `-` and `abs` at the negative end too, a loop to the largest
   whole number ends there, and `round` of a number past the range and a duration times `1e300`
   are errors, never held at the range's end.
5. **A wait was judged with a stamp's rows half applied**: `a.x` turning 1 as `b.y` turned 0 at
   one stamp passed `a.x == 1 and b.y == 1`. A stamp is judged once every row of it is applied.
6. **A loop of awaits skipped a second row of one stamp**: each await took the first row after
   the clock, so two rows of one stamp gave one. An await at the stamp of the row the last await
   of its table took takes the rows of that stamp after it (the row known by its source, sequence
   and arrival), as the reference now says.
7. **Events not asserted**: the worked example's criteria, values reported and writes are asserted
   as events, each inside its step, equal to the run's.

What held: routes, retries and ends; a read's settle; the gated writes and portal's words.

## A red run beside other tests (2026-11-23)

`a_gated_write_lands_and_a_wait_meets_what_it_caused` asserted that the wait after the write held
100 ms to 2 s after the write's step ended, and failed once at 99 ms in the suite run beside
portal's tests. The bound was not the clause's: the bench sets the speed 100 ms after it takes the
write, a moment the engine never sees; the write's step ends where the answer came back, after it,
and the speed's row is stamped by the next poll (every 50 ms), so the wait's time is the reaction
less the answer's way back plus the poll's phase, under 100 ms when the answer is slow. The test
now asserts on the stamps the clause names, all on the store's axis: the write's step ends at
least the read's settle (250 ms) after it began, the clock having moved to the answer; the wait
starts there and holds at the stamp of the store's first row with the speed 2.0, the rows before
it 1.5 (or at once, had that row been stored before the answer came back); and its detail gives
that difference. No millisecond of the bench's reaction is pinned: that delay is maquinar's.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-24): refuted, seven findings, all closed

What held: round 1's fixes where attacked directly: a call within a call within a call, nested
loops and the stop's latency, `abs` and `-` at the extremes, a wait over two tables at one stamp.

1. **The binding followed a copy of an awaited row only in file order.** A step's script box
   copying `o` to `r` before the step that awaits `o`, reached by the routes, left `r.nosuch`
   unbound: the run was bound, the write answered, and the read failed in error. The copies are
   now followed until nothing changes, in any order; the same in a function, through two copies
   in a loop.
2. **A loop of awaits over a view retook a row.** A view's rows carry no source, sequence or
   arrival, so the identity of the last taken row matched the stamp's first row every time
   (`r3.n = 2`, a count of 7 for 10). The rows of a stamp now stand in one order whatever the
   store's (every field, then the cells), and an await remembers how many rows of the stamp it
   passed and took.
3. **A wait and a read disagreed at a stamp two keys share.** The wait applied a stamp's rows by
   sequence, the store's latest breaks a tie by source and key: `wait kt.x == 1` held where the
   check after it read 0. A stamp where a table has more than one row is now read back through
   the same `latest` a read uses before the wait judges it.
4. **Live, a wait for a duration past its bound returned at once**, the clock at the bound while
   the machine had not reached it: the write after a timed-out call stood a second later than the
   reaction it caused. It now waits until its end or its bound has passed, whichever comes first.
5. **The run's bound passing inside a wait left the clock at the step's start**, so its own
   window replayed ended at the window instead. The clock now moves to the bound first.
6. **A name rebound by an await of another table was refused** though every read was sound: the
   binding took the union of the tables a name may hold. A column is now refused only when no
   table the name may hold has it; which await reached a read is the routes' to say, so a mixed
   case is judged where it runs, as the reference now states.
7. **Whole numbers went through a float**: `round` of a whole number past 2^53 changed it or was
   an error, `int(9.21e18)` was refused, and a duration times `1.0` lost its last millisecond.
   `round` of a whole number is that number, `int` takes any float whose whole part fits 64 bits
   (past it, an error by its name), and a duration times a number is computed exactly from the
   float's mantissa and power of two in 128 bits, rounded once.

Each has its test, and the old engine fails all six tests that hold them (values, copies, keys,
views, the duration's wait, the bound's clock).

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-25): refuted, four findings for C-202, all closed

What held: copies in any order, in a cycle, in loops and branches; the mixed case ending in error
by its table and column; `times` for every float form tried (negative zero, subnormals, the
largest finite, halves on both signs, the extreme durations); `round` and `int` at their edges;
the live duration wait under the tester's stop.

1. **Live, the run's bound was judged on the machine's clock** (C-203's finding too): a read's
   settle put the machine 250 ms ahead of the run's clock, so `timeout 1s` after `wait 800ms` and a
   check aborted live and passed replayed, as did a read under 200 ms and a box of 3 000 000 turns
   under 300 ms. The bound is now the run's clock's in both modes: the plant's time, which a
   replay reproduces. A script box moves no clock, so its budget of 10 000 000 statements and the
   tester's stop end a runaway loop; the reference says so, and the loop test asserts the budget.
2. **A copy through `min` or `max` of one value escaped the binding** (`r = min(o)` then
   `r.nosuch`, the write landing first): `min` and `max` give one of their values unchanged, so the
   binding follows them as copies.
3. **Nothing judged the bound between a step's actions**, and a wait after it moved the clock back
   to the bound, before its own write's answer. The bound is judged before each action, and a bound
   behind the clock never moves it back.
4. **A whole number compared with a float went through the float** (`9007199254740993 ==
   9007199254740992.0` true): the float's whole part is compared as a whole number, then its
   fraction; a numeric text is read as a whole number when it is one.

The old engine fails the four tests that hold them.
