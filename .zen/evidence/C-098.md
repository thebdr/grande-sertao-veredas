# C-098: A family started at its end whose active is held at the first look

## Why (2026-11-25)

C-096 says when a log's start's history is in the store. A start at the end skips the history,
so the flag is set at once; what the start then reads of a held active is new content. Round 3
of C-096's refutes found a family whose active was held at the first look reopening it at its end
on every retry, losing what was written while it was held, and C-096 took on the promise of how
such an active is followed. Rounds 5 and 6 found the flag holding on every path and the gaps all
in that promise: a fixed name's held active retired unopened, the newer active held in turn
retiring, a held generation read past after the patience, then a fixed name rotating again while
a held generation was awaited, and a rotation landing between a round's listing and the held open.
The promise is a family's rotation rule (C-036) for one start, so it has a clause of its own.

Clauses sharing the capability: C-035 (a file held at its first look: the same file read from
where it stood, a replacement whole), C-036 (a family followed through its rotations, the fixed
name's adopt sweep), C-037 (the backfill's replay, whose fixed sweep re-lists after its open),
C-096 (the history flag).

## Choices

- **Known by its identity.** The look records the held active's name, length and identity. A
  member of that identity that is no longer the active retired unopened, whatever its name now;
  a fixed-name active keeps its name, so the name alone cannot tell.
- **Awaited, not skipped.** A held file or generation in this path is awaited while it is there
  and not readable; one not there is gone. A retiree read by C-036's sweep is skipped after its
  patience; here a held active is what the start exists to read from, and skipping would lose it.
- **The sweep after the open.** In a fixed-name family, after a held branch opens the fixed path,
  whatever retired since the round's listing is read before the active's first line (as
  `fixed_adopt` sweeps), the held file among it from where it stood, ending at the cursor's own
  file renamed into a retiree.
- **The start returns at the look**, as a held file's does (C-035), instead of at its five-second
  bound.

## C-096's round 6 findings, answered here (2026-11-25, at 4aff54f8)

1. **A fixed name rotating again while a held generation was awaited lost that generation**: the
   younger generations read were those of the listing taken before the wait, and the active
   opened after it was whatever sat at the fixed path then. The sweep after the open reads what
   retired since (45 of 50 rows before, 50 now).
2. **A rotation between a round's listing and the held open** (seen once by the critic, a race):
   the open landed on the new file while the held one, renamed, was still held, and the new
   file's lines came first. The sweep after the open reads the held file's lines first, awaited.
   Tested in the crate through the open hook (`BEFORE_OPEN`, now also run before a held branch's
   open), the window held open.

Mutants, each red at the assertion meant for it: no sweep after the held open (the new file's 10
lines read while the held one was held, and 45 of 50 rows in the session), the held file swept
whole (its history's 10 lines read), the sweep not awaiting a held retiree (the new file read
first), the cursor's own file, rotated while the sweep awaited, swept as a retiree (its 10 lines
read twice).

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-26, at 8d68e91d): refuted, three defects and one order untested

1. **A held active retired with no active after it, then the next active held in turn and
   retiring while held, lost that active's lines**: once the retired held file was read with no
   active after it, the family was followed as born, and the born sweep gives up on a held
   retiree after its patience (the clause's "held in turn" was kept only when an active followed).
2. **The family's held look took its length from one file and its identity from another** when a
   rotation landed between the two lookups, and the open then sought inside the new file to the
   old one's length (10 lines lost).
3. **A first open that found nothing counted as the look**, so the held look that followed was
   never taken: the start waited its five-second bound and the held active opened at its end,
   every line after the start lost.
4. **The order of several generations after a retired held active** was never asserted.

Traced, answered too: a held active deleted with generations after it opened the active whole,
the generations between marked known and never read.

The held path is one rule now. The names at the look are the history; whatever is born after it
is new. Each round reads what the held file took since the look (found by its identity, from
where it stood), then every member whose name was not there at the look, whole and in order,
then the active (the held file from where it stood, another whole, held in turn if it does not
open), each awaited while held; with no active the family is waited on, never followed as born.
The look is taken only by an open that finds the active there, and bracketed by the identity at
its path before the open: a rotation inside it makes it no look, taken again next round against a
new listing (keeping no identity would leave the renamed held file passing for a new generation,
its history read). Each case is a crate test asserting the lines in order.

Mutants, each red at the assertion meant for it: with no active the family followed as born (the
held-in-turn active's 10 lines lost, 50 to 60), the look unbracketed (the renamed held file read
as new and the new one lost), a not-found open taken for the look (the renamed file's history,
10 to 20, read), the generations after the held file read newest first (out of order), a held
generation not awaited (read past while held), the retired held file not awaited (its 30 lines
lost). The not-found case's test waits for the new held file before writing to it: a mutant
returning the start sooner had died on the test's own write, not on its assertion.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-26, at fb552705): refuted, three defects

1. **The look's names came from a listing taken before the identity bracket**: a rotation
   between the round's listing and the look passed as a clean look, and the active renamed away
   sat under a name the look's set lacked, read as a new generation (its history read). The names
   are listed again after the look; only when they agree is the look taken, else it is taken again
   next round against the new listing. A newest-by-order family whose new member was made before
   the look then finds that member the active, readable, and is followed from its end: what is
   written after the start to the member it retired is a retiree's, which C-036 does not follow.
2. **A held active whose identity the look could not read was lost once it retired under its
   own name** (newest by order): the retired lookup wanted an identity. With none, the member under
   the held name that is no longer the active is the held file, read whole.
3. **On a filesystem that reuses inode numbers (ext4), a new file at the held active's path was
   taken for the held file** and read from the held length (its first lines lost); the tests ran
   on tmpfs, which never reuses. A file's identity on Unix carries its birth time where the system
   gives one (`Identity`, compared by device and inode, and by birth time when both sides have it),
   so a reused inode number is another file; the search's walk confirms a listing's inode against
   it. The same identity serves C-035's held file and C-096's searches. Tested in a folder on ext4
   (under the build's `target`).

Mutants, each red at the assertion meant for it: no second listing after the look (the renamed
active's history, 10 to 20, read), no name to find a held file without an identity (its 0 to 40
never read), the birth time ignored (the new file at the held path read from the old length, its
first 10 lines lost; ext4 reused the inode number in the test's folder, as the critic saw).

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-26, at 11eca6c9): refuted, two defects

1. **A held active renamed in two steps was lost.** Renamed first to a name outside the pattern
   while a new active was made at its path, then into the pattern, the held file was in no member

   its identity is kept, and a member of it met later (the held branch's younger members, the
   fixed open's sweep, the live loop's retiree sweeps) is taken as read. Gone from the folder, or
   a search that cannot tell, its lines are lost and the active is read: a stated limit, tested so
   the active is never waited on. An active held in turn follows the same rule.
2. **Copy-and-truncate of a held active read its history.** The copy had a name not in the look,
   so it was read whole as a generation born after it. While the held file stands at the active's
   path no rotation moved it, so a member born after the look is no generation: its copy or a file
   dropped in. Its bytes would tell which, and a held file shows none; its length can be watched,
   so the most of it seen at its path is kept, and a file shorter than that was cut. Cut (seen
