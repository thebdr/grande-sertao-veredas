# C-131 evidence - the envelope and the MAC address

## The port (2026-11-22)

From Veredas 1's `ether.rs` and `mac.rs`. Changed on the way:
- the 802.1Q tag keeps its priority and drop-eligible bit (Veredas 1 kept the VLAN id only):
  PROFINET's cyclic frames ride at priority 6 with VLAN 0, and the IO controller writes them;
- `build` refuses a payload past 1500 bytes, a priority past 7 and a VLAN id past 4095. Veredas 1
  wrote any length;
- the MAC reader takes exactly three spellings. Veredas 1 deleted every `:`, `-` and `.` and read
  what was left, so it took mixed separators, a group of one digit beside one of three, the dotted
  form with six groups, and a leading `+` (`u8::from_str_radix` accepts a sign: `+1:0e:cf:12:34:56`
  read as `01:0e:cf:12:34:56`);
- `is_local`, the locally administered bit, for the fixtures' range.

## The second reading

`tshark` 4.6.6, extracted from Ubuntu's archive into `~/.local/opt/wireshark` (the packages:
tshark, wireshark-common, libwireshark19, libwireshark-data, libwiretap16, libwsutil17,
libsmi2t64, liblua5.4-0, libnghttp3-9, libopencore-amrnb0, libbcg729-0, libjsoncpp26, libhwloc15,
libtbb12, libtbbmalloc2, libtbbbind-2-5, libfalcosecurity0t64), run by a wrapper that sets its
library, data and plugin folders. `tests/common/mod.rs` writes the frames as a classic pcap and
reads tshark's `-T ek` output (numbers in decimal); a packet tshark calls malformed, or expert
information at warning or error severity anywhere in it, is a complaint the tests require absent.
Where tshark is not found the test fails naming what it tried.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-22): refuted, three gaps

- **The drop bit was never read back alone.** Every parsed tag had the drop bit equal to the
  priority's low bit; a mutant reading bit 13 passed. The drop bit alone and the priority's low bit
  alone are now parsed back.
- **The 1500-byte limit was tested untagged only.** Tagged 1500 is now taken (1518 bytes, read by
  tshark) and 1501 refused.
- **A type field below 0x0600 is an 802.3 length.** `build` wrote one, and 0x8100 untagged, which
  its own `parse` then read as a tag. `build` now refuses both (`NotEtherType`), `parse` keeps the
  field as it travels and `is_length` tells a length (a spanning-tree frame's) from an EtherType;
  the clause's dissector claim names the EtherTypes it covers.
