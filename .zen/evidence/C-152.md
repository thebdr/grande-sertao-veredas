# C-152 evidence - the controller's states

New in Veredas 2: Veredas 1 had no controller beside its demo host. Built on `docs/controlador-plan.md`
and core's state text (C-070); the readings the plan leaves are `docs/questions.md` Q228.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-22): refuted, and two defects
Every mutant asked for was killed (the program starting or acknowledging, the switch skipped, a stop or
a start allowed in FAULT, default outputs in STOP, the program's clock handed to the plant, the old
program kept across a restart, a power-cycled start-up returned as STOP, each condition dropped, a crash
as STOP, the first scan a cycle late). Two probes showed the controller itself breaking the clause:
- **The exchange while no program runs repeated itself**: after an acknowledge at 10 ms of a fault that
  had exchanged at 0 and 10, and after a start and a stop at 20 ms, the next exchange ran again at the
  instant just served, because a stop or an acknowledge set the next exchange from the last cycle's end
  alone.
- **A stop between periods** (13 ms) made the next exchange run at 13 ms, off the target's periods.

The controller now places that exchange on the target's periods alone, at most once in each: the first
period not before the step and after the last exchange, the program's or its own. A stop right after a
scan at 10 ms therefore brings the safe outputs at 20 ms, not a second time at 10 (the first test said
"at once" and now says the next period).

Test gaps, each now pinned: a start refused in RUN; an acknowledge refused in STARTUP and in RUN; the
program stopping itself from RUN; a cold start refused from the program and with the switch off; refusals
compared against the whole state; `sim` and `no_realtime` each alone; a power-cycled start-up without real
time, and stepped to RUN with it; the watchdog's task name; a stop keeping the overruns; the exchange's
grid after a program that started off it. Wording: the clause said every refusal changes nothing, which
the real-time refusal does not; it now names the exception. Equivalent: removing the reset of `overruns`
at start-up (the first scan in the same cycle sets it).

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-22): refuted, no defect
The controller held every case probed, among them 3000 random sequences of 40 steps, commands, clock
jumps and slow reads checked against the exchange rule after every step. Round 1's mutants killed but
the equivalents: the reset of `overruns`, and four that drop one of the two trackers of the next
exchange (`idle_cycle` and `last_exchange` each suffice). Test gaps, now pinned:
- **No idle step ever came late** (high): the rule "not before the step" could be dropped, or rounded
  down, and every test passed, a late step then exchanging at the instant it came. Four late cases
  are pinned: a stop handled late, a first step late, a slow idle read, a slow scan before a stop.
- **A program started on a period other than zero** (moderate): its last exchange counted without its
  start's offset passed; a start at 20 ms then a stop at 30 now shows one exchange at 40.
- **The warm path** (moderate): every warm start the tests followed with a scan began at time zero; a
  warm start that kept the old clock or resumed the old task passed. Warm starts after a stop and after
  an acknowledge are now pinned.
- Low: a count of overruns past one; a start from every kind of STOP; the crash's FAULT refusing a stop
  and a start.

Outside the clause: the instant the last exchange is measured from (its read or its write); a start's first
scan at the instant of the idle exchange just made (the once-per-period rule is for the time no program runs).

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-22): refuted, a defect
Every earlier mutant killed but the equivalents the note names; round 2's exchange fuzz again found
nothing. **The defect:** the count of overruns was reset after start-up, where the first scan of the
same cycle overwrote it at once (which is why removing that reset looked equivalent in round 1). So a
start showed the previous run's count in STARTUP, and a start stopped from STARTUP kept it in STOP for
good, though it ran no scan: `STOP command(id=101, source="local") overruns(count=1)`. A model of the
controller over 4000 random sequences found 569 such states and nothing else. The count is now reset
when a start begins, and both cases are pinned.

Test gaps, now pinned: refusals in the watchdog's FAULT leaving the faulted program and its exchange
(a refused stop that dropped the task first passed); on a production target without real time, the
person and the switch asked before real time; an acknowledge refused in RUN restarting nothing; a stop
from `local`; the program's clock from zero after an acknowledge. Outside: whether the watchdog's FAULT
keeps the faulted program visible.

## Refute round 7 (2026-11-22): refuted, low weight only, no defect
The critic found no misreading the model shares with the controller, every earlier mutant killed but the
equivalents, and the controller agreeing with the model over 30 000 sequences of 60 operations, 20 000 of 200,
2000 of 2000 and a mix that seldom stops. It measured how far the draws reached: runs short (85% of stops within
four scans), the count of overruns never past four, no idle exchange past 4.29 s of the target's clock, and a

Answered in the model: the host now comes back on a period of the target's clock half the time it jumps, and

## Refute round 8 (2026-11-22): holds
The critic read round 7's additions against the clause and found them right. Each counter reaches what it
names: every one of the 182 idle exchanges held back by a late scan, in the long mix, has that shape. The
never-faulting stalls fall within twice the period: no watchdog fault in 1000 sequences. A jump of seconds over
a running task is a late start, worked by hand and passed. How far the draws reach, short mix and long mix:
- the count of overruns at most 5 and 63, the longest run 21 and 238 scans;
- the task's cycle up to 13 481 and 27 883, the program's clock up to 18 and 39 s;
- the target's clock up to 75 s, up to 11 watchdog faults in one sequence;
- every transition between the states in both halves of the long mix.

Beyond the test's own seeds, the controller agreed with the model in 122 000 more sequences, 2000 of them of
3000 operations. Every earlier mutant is killed but the six equivalents, round 7's survivors among the killed.
Of 19 fresh mutants (32-bit times, the grid in floating-point seconds, the last exchange misplaced or
forgotten, the count capped at eight or at 50 or one step behind), 17 are killed. Two survive, contrived: the
count capped at 255, or kept in a byte, which no run of the draws can reach (a run ends on a stop drawn one
operation in 40) and which the controller could only get by a narrowing written on purpose; the engine's count
reaches core's through a saturating conversion. Left as is, the clause holding.

## The engine's program fault (2026-11-25)
The engine (C-151) now runs a program whose scan may end in an error. The controller is still generic over native
programs alone, so `Fault::Program` cannot reach it, and its tests ran unchanged against the new engine; the clause now
says so. Running an interpreted program, with its fault as `FAULT program(at)` (502, core's `FaultReason::Program`),
its restart from a remade machine and its refusals at making, is its next change. Its run is recorded again on the
engine as it now stands.

## An interpreted program on the controller (2026-11-25)
The controller now runs a `TaskProgram` made by a `Source`: `Native<P>` makes a native program from its initial
values (the default type of `Controller<P>`, so every native use and test is as it was), and `Loaded<I, O>` makes an
interpreted one (C-155) from what its host hands in: the loaded program, its scan entry, its four regions' types, its
first state and its safe outputs (Q231, taken meanwhile). The source makes the program at power-on, the one whose
safe outputs apply while no program runs, once more there to check the task's configuration, and again at every
start, so every start runs it from its first state. Making is deterministic, so a program made at power-on is made
alike at a start; a program that cannot be made is refused when the controller comes up
(`PowerOnError::Program`, the adapter's reason), as a zero period is (`PowerOnError::Task`).

The fault: the engine's `Fault::Program` is mapped by the source. An interpreted program's own error,
`ScanError::Program`, is `FAULT program(at)`, 502, `at` the file and line its location names (`count.rs:2`, as the
plan writes a breakpoint's location); `ScanError::Host` and `ScanError::Outputs`, an image the machine refuses or
outputs read back as none, are the adapter's or the host's failures and the runtime's, `FAULT internal`, 507. A
native program's error is `Infallible`. The watchdog's fault is 501 for both.

How the tests stand (`tests/interpreted.rs`): a hand-lowered program that counts its scans from a first state of 40,
writing the count times a hundred plus its input divided by its divisor behind the division's assertion, beside its
native twin whose default is that count, 300 scans each with equal writes, exact; the divisor of zero on the sixth
scan giving `FAULT program(id=502, at="count.rs:2")`, the safe outputs handed in from that scan on, the exchange on
every period, an acknowledge and a warm start running it from its first state; an input image the machine refuses
and outputs that read back as none each giving `FAULT internal(id=507)`; the watchdog for the interpreted program;
the safe outputs while stopped; the refusals at power-on; a stop and a start running it again from its first state.
The program's location is in the second of its two files, so a fault names the file its location names. Four
mistakes tried by hand, each killed: the program's error as 507, the host's as 502, a stop keeping the program
where it stood, and the location always naming the first file.

The claim changed, so the clause returns to pending until its next refute round.
