# C-044 evidence - the server

## The design (2026-11-07)

Veredas 1's `veredas-server` as it was (its router, its SSE feed, its windowed REST with the
largest-triangle-three-buckets downsampler), over the session's `Live` instead of a feed the
desktop published into: the `serve` feature, on by default, needing the store it serves. Not
carried: the embedded dashboard and its layout endpoints (the apps own their screens, the plan),
and the command sequencer with its handshake wait (a plant-specific procedure; the apps compose it
from the write endpoint when they need it, under the same gate).

The write surface is the handles' own gates seen over HTTP: the switch is checked early for a
better answer (a 403 before anything is coerced), the whitelist by the handle, and no endpoint arms
the switch, which the shell or the project does in process. A write the plant never saw and one
it may have seen answer different codes (503, 504) because the operator must know which.

The test client is raw TCP, not an HTTP crate: what reaches a browser is what is asserted. The
stream test flaked once at ten seconds with the hello received and no data frame; five reruns
passed in under a second each and the frames are the same path the session test proves, so the
flake is recorded here and watched, not explained.

## Found by CI (2026-11-09): the listing read before the channel's series reached it

On Linux (ac62e83) the server test asserted the listing held the channel's `item` series right
after a wait that covered the health, the vitals and the counters alone: the machine's first
announcement landed after the refresh that satisfied the wait, and the listing (the ingest's
cache, refreshed every 500 ms) did not show it yet. The wait now covers every snapshot the test
reads next, the two series it names included. The same trap as the session's counters: a
published snapshot is read by waiting for what is asserted of it.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-09): refuted, six gaps on the write surface and the stream

The critic found: a channel send to an unknown source answered 404 where the clause (and the
OPC route) say 403 by name; a send with nobody connected answered 409, a code the clause does
not have, where a write the plant never saw is 503; the OPC write's 503 and 504 were asserted by
no test; a disarmed write or send with a body that did not parse got the framework's 422 or 400
in plain text, before the gate, where the clause says 403 before anything is coerced; the point
budget's clamp and default were never exercised (the one budget tested was the minimum itself);
the stream's lag event and keep-alive were claimed and never asserted. Fixes: the unknown channel
403 by name; a peer not there, or a worker gone or silent, 503 (a channel's wire carries no
confirmation of its own, so no send of it is ever "maybe seen": the clause says so, and keeps 504
for an OPC write awaiting the server's status); the body read as bytes after the switch and
parsed into the same error form, 400 when it is not the shape; the keep-alive every 5 seconds so
a test can see it; the session's end published on the live state so the snapshot's running flag
is real (it was a constant, which the Verification asserted). One test asserts the lot over the
socket, with the budget's clamp and the refusal codes pinned purely. Held by the critic: the
snapshot never querying the store, the listing from the cache sorted, the health labels for all
six states, the largest-triangle sampler's edges, the page's limits and offsets, the on-demand
read's 404s, the channel's order on the wire, no endpoint setting the switch, no state per client.
The keep-alive is asserted after the plant has stopped: while the OPC source polls it, a frame
every 60 ms never lets the stream fall idle, and the comment would never come; retrying, the
source sends none.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-09): refuted, two narrow points

The critic found the keep-alive asserted loosely (one comment within twelve seconds: an interval
of eleven passed the test, probed by mutation; the code's cadence was five) and a body over the
framework's 2 MB limit answered 413 in plain text by the `Bytes` extractor before the switch was
consulted, disarmed or armed. Fixes: the handlers take the request and read the body themselves
after the switch, under their own limit of 64 KiB (a tag's value or a message's fields never fill
it), a body over it 413 in the error form with the limit named, so a disarmed request of any
size is 403 and nothing of its body is read. The test opens a fresh stream on the idle session
and times the comments from the hello: the first four to seven seconds after it and the next
four to seven after that, which an eleven-second cadence fails; and sends three mebibytes to
each route, disarmed and armed. Held: every finding of round 1, the unknown OPC source and channel
403 by name, the 503 of every channel refusal, the 504 pinned purely, the lag once and the
stream going on, the end published by the pump, the snapshot never querying the store.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-09): holds

The critic probed the new reader over raw TCP: disarmed, a header announcing 3 MiB with no byte
sent is answered 403 in 2 ms; armed, a body of exactly 65536 bytes is parsed (the limit is
inclusive) and 65537 is 413 with the limit named, with a Content-Length and chunked alike; a body
cut short by a half-close and a malformed chunk framing are 400 in the error form. The filler
helper cannot pass on a closed or partial answer (it expects a header block and compares the
status and the parsed body exactly); three back-to-back runs of the test file were green. The
cadence pin rejects an eleven-second interval and tolerates four to seven, as a socket timing
test must. Noted outside the clause: armed, a header announcing a large body whose bytes never
come holds the handler until the client gives up; the limit trips on bytes received, not on the
declared length. That is a slow-client exposure, not a clause gap; it goes to the hand-over
note as a follow-up (a read timeout on the body, with the session's idle window as its measure).
Verdict: holds, after three rounds.
