# C-036 evidence - log families

## The design (2026-11-07)

Veredas 1's `family.rs` and `tail/family.rs` as they were, with their three invariants (a family
born after attach is all new content; never read an adopted active while older retirees are
unconsumed; a sweep skips the files a live cursor reads, by identity). The unit tests of the
family spec moved from the module into the clause's test file, so the clause links one file.

The plant's writer is two things here. The bench's emitter (C-025) writes the files a plant
writes, rotating cleanly on a line count, and its `family_shape` gives the spec that reads them:
the test attaches before the first file, lets the emitter rotate several times in UTF-8 and in
UTF-16, and requires the received lines to equal every line of every file on disk in family
order, read back independently of the tail. The rotation races a plant logger shows (a straggler
landing late in the retired file, a torn write, a fixed-name rename) the emitter never produces
on purpose (C-025: never append to an existing file), so a writer in `tests/common/mod.rs` plays
them, as Veredas 1's test kit did. That writer is the plant, not a stand-in for the tail: what
is under test reads real files on a real disk either way.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-08): refuted

Two gaps. A keyless family (no `date`, no `idx` group) was listed by modification time but
followed by name: the tail ranked members without the time, so a newer file with a smaller name
was never the newest, history leaked and lines were lost. No rank holds for a keyless family
against a straggler (a late line into a retired file moves its time), so following is refused by
text and the listing keeps its time order, now asserted exactly. And the emitter accepts an hour
in a file name; the reader read the date back without the minutes it had no format for, so every
hour of a day shared one key and the next hour was never followed: an hour without minutes is
now the start of the hour, and a pattern whose date specifiers are split by other text (which
the reader could never read back whole) has no shape. Both pinned. The same round brought the
attach guarantee of C-035 to families: the start returns once the folder is listed or the backfill
located.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-08): refuted

Four gaps. The tests shared module pulled the plant helper, which needs the session, so the
linked test no longer built without the store feature: the helper is gated to it. A fixed-name
family whose active file was absent at the start waited the whole attach bound and then opened
the active at its end, losing the first line: a listing with no active signals the attach, and
the active, when it comes, is new content read from its start. The keyed check was syntactic: a
date token that parses on no member left every key empty and the tail followed by name while the
listing ordered by time; a pre-existing family on which no key parses is refused by text, and the
clause now states the tie rule (followed by name, the lower name history: the keys must separate
generations). A twelve-hour hour got no minutes filled: it does now.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-08): refuted

One real loss and one minor gap. A fixed-name active born after the start could retire before the
worker opened it, and the generations renamed into the pattern meanwhile were marked known on the
open and never read (nine rounds of twenty). The family is now followed from that moment as one
born after the start, with the retirees listed at the start as the history, so born mode sweeps
every generation born since before it opens the active. A padding modifier on the hour (`%-H`)
defeated the minute fill: a specifier is now found with or without one. Seen once under load and
handed to round 4 unexplained: in born mode with a fixed name, the first generation read after
the second (L45 before L0), one run of about eight of the whole binary.

## Found by the suite (2026-11-08): a listing between two looks at the path

Two order inversions in fixed-name mode under the whole suite in parallel (one run in three): the
born-after-attach scenario read its second generation before its first, the clean rotation read
L501 before L334 with nothing missing. The live loop, finding the fixed path unchanged, listed the
folder and marked every member known (dropped in from outside, not a rotation); a burst of
rotations between that check and the listing showed its generations in the listing, and they were
taken as known without ever being read. The path is now checked again after the listing, and the
members are marked known only when it is still the cursor's own file on both sides. Handed to the
critic for round 4 with the two observations, since no deterministic test can hold a rotation
inside that window.

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-08): holds

No gap survived. The round-3 loss held fixed over twenty rounds; the listing race the suite exposed
held over 48 rounds of a writer rotating in bursts under sixteen busy threads, in both modes; the
re-check after the listing is sound by identity (a rotation renames the cursor's file away and the
path never points at it again). One wording the critic weighed: a straggler written after the
start into a retiree the first listing seeded as history is history too, as the clause says.

## Found by CI, refute round 5 (2026-11-09): a second rotation inside the pre-read sweep

The Linux runner inverted two generations once more at 94f112b (lines 90 to 134 after 135 on,
nothing missing or duplicated), the shape round 4 had closed from another door. The critic
reproduced it on Windows six times of six and named the mechanism: the pre-read sweep of a
freshly adopted active skipped the cursor's own file by identity when it retired during the
sweep (invariant 3, against duplicates) but went on reading the retirees newer than it, so a
generation that retired after the cursor's was emitted before the cursor had read its own; the
drain grace inside `read_full` is the window, and a slow runner's writer rotates twice in it. The
sweep now stops at the cursor's own retiree: everything newer waits for the live loop's next
sweep, after the cursor has read its file, so the order holds. Pinned without a hook: a fixed
family with a 1.5 s drain grace, a generation born and retired between two looks so the adopt's
pre-read sweep holds its grace on it, and two rotations inside that grace; the test fails six of
six at c34d8bd and passes with the stop. The born branch's pre-read sweep had the same shape and
takes the same stop.

## Refute round 6 (2026-11-09): holds

No gap at 77e68a6. Checked: the stop after older retirees were read in the same pass (walked

five times unloaded and four of five under thirty-two CPU burners, the fifth failing a bench

predecessor, which no test folder reaches. Verified after six rounds; the clause grew at rounds
1 to 4 and at the CI finding.

## The family's follower shares the file tail's watcher (2026-11-26)

The spin on a quiet log (C-035's evidence, "The watcher woke the loop on its own opens") held the
family's follower too, a full core for a family of one dated member on the reference plant. Pinned
here as well, both modes (`following_a_quiet_family_costs_almost_no_processor_time`).

A newest-by-order family rescans every `rescan_interval`, but its loop waited its whole poll after
each pass: a member born while a rescan was not yet due (its create waking the loop early) waited a
poll, five seconds in the test. The wait now ends by the next rescan (no change at the default
100 ms poll, under the 250 ms rescan). The quiet family test asserts a rotation in either mode read
within a second and a half; the mutant that waits the poll misses the new dated member, and the
fixed-name case catches a watcher that never wakes (the dated one is rescanned regardless).
