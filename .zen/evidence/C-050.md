# C-050 evidence - the object feed

## The design (2026-11-09)

The feed is Veredas 1's `live.ts` moved into Rust, where its outputs can be asserted exactly: the
page receives a state, a mark, a word, a level's fill and a marker's place, and maps them onto the
drawing. Numbers come from portal's data frames (one sample each, with its key); text comes only
through the ingest's series cache, because portal broadcasts numeric samples alone; the cache also
seeds a series no frame has carried yet and heals one whose frames a lag dropped (a newer index
only).

The fold is pure (samples in, views out, the clock a parameter), so its rules are tested without
timing; the live test proves the same views reach the snapshot route and the stream.

## Found while writing the live test

- **An arrival is not a change.** Comparing whole views published every polled object every tick,
  since each poll refreshes its arrival time: a change is now any field but the arrival, which
  rides the next change and the snapshot.
- **The health lags the first value.** Right after the start, values arrived a refresh (500 ms)
  before the source's health read live, and every object read stale for that long. A source still
  waiting has delivered nothing an object could show; staleness now reads a source as away when it
  is idle, retrying, ended or failed, and an object with no value is stale on its own.

## Found in the browser (2026-11-09): waiting is also a reconnect

Driving the page against the demo plant, the bench was stopped to see objects go stale: they
stayed normal. Portal reads an OPC source reconnecting (`ConnState::Connecting`) and a channel
whose peer left as **waiting**, the same state as before a first delivery, and the feed had taken
waiting for "not away" (the startup lag above). Away is now a pure function of the health and the
count delivered (`feed::away`): idle, retrying, ended or failed, or waiting once the source has
delivered. The fold test pins all six states before and after a first delivery; the live test now
holds the plant's objects stale for four more seconds while the source tries again, and both fail
with the old rule (the mutation run on the day).

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-10, at bfaeaad): refuted, seven gaps

Two of the critic's mutations failed the test (the arrival counted as a change; waiting never
away). Its findings, and what changed (some had already been closed by b77957b, after the commit
the critic read):
1. **A startup race (CI would have flaked):** until the feed's first tick, the snapshot route
   served no objects and no status, and a test closure read an object that was not there (2 of 20
   clean runs). The feed now evaluates once before it is served; six parallel runs green.
2. **The stream's status unasserted** (a payload of `{}` stayed green): the first snapshot's and a
   status event's running, switch and health are asserted, and the source away rides the stream.
3. **A change pinned one way only** (a `same` reading state and word alone stayed green): a level
   moving inside its state, a marker moving and a marker added are changes in the fold, and a
   level's move reaches the stream live.
4. **"A status every second" unpinned** (4 s stayed green): three status events in a row, each
   within 2.5 s of the last.
5. **The health labels unpinned:** `health_label` is public and its six labels and details are
   pinned.
6. **Keyed series:** the default 10 s forget bound, one key read (closed by b77957b), the latest
   sample whatever its key are now asserted; and a real gap: a series whose keys only the ingest's
   cache had seen showed one place for ever. The fold now takes the cache's key count, and a keyed
   series never shows one place.
7. **NaN republished every tick:** a float stuck at NaN never equalled itself. Views are compared
   as the page receives them (NaN reads `null`), so it is the same as itself.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-10, at 20a5c34): refuted, two defects and seven gaps

Two defects in the code. A rule over a text series that no object reads was compared as a number:
`compile_rule` dropped the series' type and the fold watched the series untyped, so the cache's
"0" became the number 0 (a rule `equals = "0"` never held, one `equals = 0` did). And a time series
read two ways: the cache gives it as the ingest renders it ("2026-11-10 12:00:00"), but a frame
carries its epoch milliseconds, which the fold took as the value. Now a rule carries its series'
type and the fold watches it with it, and a series typed text or time is taken from the cache
alone; both cases are pinned (and the text one fails on the old code exactly as the critic's probe
read it).

Seven gaps in the test, each a mutation left green: a change compared without its state (going
stale with the value unchanged never had to ride the stream; C-051's page test caught it, C-050's
own did not); the first rule against the weightiest; `below` at its bound; ranges not starting at
0 for a level and a place; a rule's own key; a key's sample at an equal index; and a status every
two seconds passing a test meant for one. Each is pinned now: a state alone changing is a change
in the fold (source away, past the bound, flipped by another series), and live, the rate going
stale past its bound and outlet 1 going stale with the plant away each ride the stream with their
mark, word and value; a lighter rule first holds first; `below` is strict; 50..150 and -50..50 at
and past their ends; a rule bound to B2 after A1 moved; an equal-index key sample replacing with
its new arrival; at least five statuses in six seconds.

Argued from reading, not claimed: key-bound objects and keyed markers have no per-key cache to
heal from after a lag; a source coming back has no live test (the view is pure over its health).

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-10, at 77180a4): refuted, three defects and one gap

Three defects, each the unchanged code failing a probe of what the clause says. An empty text was
never taken (`cached` read "" as "nothing yet"), so a tag cleared to "" kept its old text and state.
A text or time sample under the same stamp as the one held was never taken (the cache was taken
only when strictly newer, to keep an unchanged entry's arrival), though log stamps to the second
make that common. And an object or a rule bound to a key of a text or time series never got a
value: text and time come from the cache alone, which holds no value per key.

The answers. The fold now reads the cache's sample count: no sample stored is no value, an empty
text is one, and a count that moved since the last poll is a new sample, taken at an equal index
too, while an entry polled again unchanged keeps its arrival (the staleness pins still hold). A
`key` on a series that is not numeric is refused at load, for an object's value and for a rule,
naming the series and its type (the reference says a key is a numeric series'). The gap: a stream
client lagging past the buffer was never tested to get a fresh snapshot; the stream is now one
function (`feed::stream`) the host serves, and a test sends five events into a channel of two and
reads the snapshot, the fresh snapshot where three were lost, then the rest.

Argued, not claimed: key-bound numeric objects and keyed markers have no per-key cache to heal from
after a lag or a backfill read before the feed subscribed.

## A key's own value (2026-11-10, from C-051's second refute round)

A keyed marker's side panel showed the object's value, the latest sample of any key, whichever key
was selected. Each item of a keyed marker now carries its key's own value as the object shows a
number (`Item.text`, its decimals applied), and the fold's tests assert it beside each place.

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-10, at c6b5d4f): refuted, one defect and two gaps

The defect: portal sends frames on every pump pass and refreshes its cache every 500 ms, so the
cache trails the frames; the fold took any poll whose count had moved as a new sample at an equal
stamp. Two lines under one stamp (97.5, then 99) could have the older brought back over the newer
by a poll still holding the first (the critic saw the newer never reach the stream in 2 to 5 pairs
of 50, the older read as alarm meanwhile), and a sample already framed had its arrival stamped
again at the poll (staleness up to 0.7 s late). The fold now counts the samples the frames brought
that the cache's count has not yet accounted for: at an equal stamp the cache is taken only when its
count moved past them (a lag, a backfill, a text series), so the frames' own samples stand with their
arrivals. The fold test frames 97.5 and 99 under one stamp, polls the cache trailing at 97.5 and then
at 99, and reads 99 with the frame's arrival, stale past the bound from it; a third sample the frames
missed is taken.

The gaps: a keyed marker's items were never read at their decimals or with a place to clamp (every
keyed value an integer inside the range), and a rule over another series was never read before that
series had a value (falling back to the object's own value passed). A keyed marker to a tenth reads
A1 at 10.25 as "10.2" and B2 at 130 clamped to the path's end as "130.0"; and a rule over B2 holds
nothing while B2 has no sample, the object normal though its own value would trip it.

## Refute round 5 (2026-11-10, at 672362b): refuted, one defect and one gap

The defect: round 4 counted the samples the frames brought against the cache's count, which places
the cache's sample among the frames only when no frame is lost and the cache always trails. A lag
broke the first (two frames lost, the next pass's 99 received, the cache refreshed after the lost
pass still at 97.5: the count moved past what the frames brought, so 97.5 came back over 99, read as
alarm until the next refresh), and the stream's unbiased select broke the second (a cache polled
before its own frame was read counted that frame twice, so a later line under the stamp whose frame
a lag lost was never taken, a false alarm held until the plant wrote a newer stamp). The gap: no
test counted keyed frames against the cache (a mutation counting unkeyed frames alone stayed green).

The answer is an order, not a count: portal's frames carry each sample's `n`, the series' count with
it, the count the cache shows once it holds that sample (C-043). The fold keeps `n` with what it
holds and takes a sample, from a frame or from the cache (its count its `n`), when it comes after:
a newer stamp, or the same stamp with a higher `n`. The same sample read again is no arrival, an
earlier line under the stamp never comes back, and a frame sample without its count is no sample.
The fold test drives the critic's three sequences (a lag, the cache read before its frame, two keys
under one stamp), the same sample again for the series and for a key, and an earlier line under the
stamp. Six mutants each turn a test red: the same sample taken again, an equal stamp taken whatever
its count, the cache's count ignored, a sample without its count taken, a key ordered by stamp
alone, and portal's count off by one (portal's session test).

The wording: "a key read on numeric series alone" left C-050's description; it is the binding's
refusal, stated and pinned by C-048 (`files.rs`).

## Refute round 6 (2026-11-11, at 511d8a1): refuted, three gaps and a wording overclaim

Round 5's order was never checked end to end: the fold test fed `n` and the cache's count by hand,
so portal's frame count one past its cache's, or the feed's poll passing the count one too high,
both kept the recorded test green (portal's own session test catches the first, but C-050's record
does not run it). Nothing made the feed's frame receiver lag, so a feed that ended at its first lag
stayed green. The status went out every 1.2 s about half the time (its cadence counted from an
instant read after the evaluation, a hair short of the second on the fifth tick), while the
Verification said "a status within the second" and the test waited 5 s for it. And the Description
claimed a cache heal that a key's own value and place never get (the cache carries the series' last
sample alone).

The answers. The tick's cache poll is `Fold::poll(&Live, now)`, and the live test frames two rate
lines under one stamp, reads their frames from the session (counts one apart), waits for the cache
to hold the later and asserts its count is the later frame's; a fresh fold fed those frames and a
poll of the session reads the later with its frame's arrival, and a second poll leaves it. The feed
loop is `Feed::spawn_with(live, binding, frames)` (`spawn` subscribes the session's own); the test
hands it a receiver of capacity one with three frames already sent, and the third, then one sent
after, land while the statuses keep coming. The cadence counts from the instant the tick was due,
so five ticks make the second; the test reads each status's own clock (`now_ms`, taken at its
evaluation, free of the reader's delays) and asserts every interval but one under 1.1 s, one late
tick forgiven, beside the bounds it had (a median of the intervals as read failed once under the
full suite's load, and caught the old cadence one run in three). The Description now says the
cache heals the series' value, a key's own value and place coming from frames alone.

Three mutants turn the recorded test red every time: portal's count one past, the poll's count one
past, the feed ending at its first lag. The old cadence turns it red five runs in six (its intervals
as sent mixing 1000 and 1200 ms; the fixed cadence measures 1000 to 1010 ms): the cadence check is
statistical by nature, the fix exact by construction.

## Refute round 7 (2026-11-11, at b820e69): refuted, two gaps

The cadence drifted on a busy runtime: the ticks were set to `MissedTickBehavior::Delay`, which in
tokio moves the next deadline to now plus a period whenever a tick is polled more than 5 ms late, so
the lateness of the five ticks added up inside each status interval. Round 6's "exact by
construction" held only while no tick was late. The critic's probe (a task blocking the feed's
thread 0 to 80 ms every 30 ms) measured a mean of 1055 ms with two intervals past 1.1 s, which the
recorded test's bound refuses: a flake waiting for a loaded runner. And the cache's key count
crossed `Fold::poll` unchecked (zeroed, the test stayed green), though a keyed series ingested
before the feed subscribed is known as keyed through that count alone.

The answers. The ticks skip a missed one and keep their 200 ms grid (`MissedTickBehavior::Skip`), so a
late tick costs its own lateness; the clause says so. The live test reads, from a fold that saw no
frame, the keyed markers' series as keyed through a poll of the session (no single place, the
cache's two keys), and runs the lagged feed under the critic's blocking task for 8 s, asserting the
mean interval by the statuses' clocks under 1.02 s. The key count zeroed turns it red; the old
`Delay` turned it red in three runs of three (means 1041 to 1075 ms). The load check is statistical
and the clause says so.

## Refute round 8 (2026-11-11, at 6f72af5): refuted, one gap and a word

The cache's key count was tried at 0 and 2 alone, so a fold taking a series as keyed only from its
second key stayed green, though a keyed series whose one key the ingest saw before the feed
subscribed then shows one place for ever (round 1's defect, narrowed to one key). The answer: the
fold test reads the series keyed from the cache's count at one key and at two, no single place
however long; the mutant turns it red. The word: "a tick later than its period skipped" named a
mechanism no test checks (a burst of catch-up ticks behaves the same under the test's load); the
clause now says a late tick never moves the grid, which round 7's check pins.

## Refute round 9 (2026-11-11, at 4a41059): holds

The critic matched every phrase of the Description to the assertion that pins it (values from
frames and cache, text and time from the cache alone, the order under one stamp end to end, the
state and its marks, staleness by value, source and bound, the markers per key, the change rule,
the switch and health, the cadence as stated, reading on past a lag) and ran a mutation of the away
rule (a source waiting again after it delivered never away), which turned the recorded test red.
Leads it dropped as outside the clause: a source's health `detail` (the page never reads it), the
store's now on a UTC runner (proven where the run is recorded, an hour apart here), a rule over a
numeric series no object reads (shared code, its watch pinned by the text rule). Verified.

## After verification (2026-11-11): the load check flaked, a limit stated

The full suite went red once on round 7's load check: under the machine's own load (a virtual
machine running beside it) the kept grid read a mean of 1035 ms against its 1.02 s bound, while a
moved grid had read 1041 to 1075 ms. No bound separates the two once the machine itself is loaded:
a heavier blocking task (0 to 120 ms) and a 1.15 s bound let the moved grid pass three runs of
three. So the kept grid is stated as the code's choice (`MissedTickBehavior::Skip`), which no test
here measures apart from a loaded machine's lateness; the load check stays, worded as what it
proves (the statuses still about a second apart under load, mean under 1.15 s).
