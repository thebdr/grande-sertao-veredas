# C-009 evidence - one stop/join handle for every source worker

Ported from Veredas 1's `veredas-core` `source.rs` and `error.rs`. Veredas 1 tested the handle
through its `SourceSet`, which stays with the data plane (portal); `tests/source_handle.rs` drives
the handle directly on real threads.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-05): holds, two edges hardened

Verdict: holds. The critic ran a 6-test probe: 3 000 workers (panicking, failing, ending cleanly)
polled 0-39 times while finishing with `request_stop` interleaved, no outcome lost or changed, no
double join; 1 000 polls of a live worker, the slowest 200 ns; a drop in 19 us against a worker
lingering 3 s. `take_if` behaves as Veredas 1's `unwrap_or_else(unreachable!)` did.

Hardened on the critic's two notes, though neither counted against the clause:
- A panic whose payload panics again when dropped: `poll_outcome` dropped the payload inside its
  `map_err`, after the join handle was spent, so the second panic unwound into the poller and a
  later `join` read `Ok(())`, a panicked worker reported as clean. Veredas 1 has the same line.
  `outcome_of` now drops the payload under `catch_unwind`; the test panics with such a payload and
  expects `Panicked` from the poll and from `join`, and from `join` alone.
- `is_finished` on a worker that ended but was never polled was untested (a mutant reading only
  `join.is_none()` would have passed); now pinned, with a live worker reading unfinished beside it.

Held: timing bounds that only a broken handle could blow (1 s for 100 polls, 200 ms for a drop),
`request_stop` racing a finishing worker (one atomic store), the path in every I/O error (no
path-less conversion exists).

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-05)

Refuted: dropping the payload under `catch_unwind` caught only the first panic. When the payload's
drop panics with another payload whose drop panics too, `let _ =` dropped the second outside any
guard: the poll unwound into the caller, a later poll read `None` while `is_finished` read true,
and `join` returned `Ok(())`; `join` alone unwound. Fixed: the payload is forgotten, never dropped
(one leaked box per panicked worker), which also ends a slow payload drop blocking a poll of an
ended worker and the panic hook firing a second time in the polling thread. The test drives
payloads nested once and three times through poll then join and through join alone.

Noted, not the clause's: dropping the handle of a worker that panicked with such a payload, never
polled, aborts the process ("thread result panicked on drop"): Rust's own rule for an unjoined
thread, unavoidable without blocking in drop. `cargo test` always unwinds; a profile with
`panic = "abort"` would kill the process on a worker panic before any handle code ran, and none
sets it.
