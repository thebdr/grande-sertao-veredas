# C-072 evidence - the EtherNet/IP scanner

## The design (2026-11-22)

The briefing names a young Rust crate for the scanner. Two were read. rseip 0.3.1 (MIT) holds
explicit messaging and no implicit I/O, with about fifty asserts and unwraps, some on its encoding
paths. `ethernetip-core` and `ethernetip-connections` 0.1.1 (Apache-2.0), which the bench's adapter
test (C-111) already drives the adapter with, hold the encapsulation, the Common Packet Format, the
Connection Manager's requests and Class 1 framing, their decoders checking every length before they
read. Their own scanner does not fit a controller: no bound on connecting or on an answer, the
16-bit sequence count left out of a connection's sizes (the bench refuses it 0x0127), no watch on a
silent adapter, and the Sockaddr Info items numbered the other way round from the standard (the
crate's `SOCKADDR_INFO_T_TO_O` is 0x8000 and carries the originator's address). So core takes their
codecs, pinned exactly as the workspace already pins them, and writes the scanner itself
(`docs/questions.md` Q102, option (a) taken): the items by the standard's numbers, every step
bounded, the connection's timeout watched.

The scanner runs on a thread and a current-thread runtime of its own: the open (connect,
RegisterSession, Forward_Open) reports back through a channel, then one `select!` produces the
outputs at the granted O→T interval, consumes the inputs, and watches the deadline (the granted
T→O interval × 4 << multiplier from the last input of this connection). Lost, it produces no more
and closes its session: per the standard the connection is dead at both ends, and the adapter times
its own end out. The handle is blocking; a close or a drop sends the Forward_Close and
UnRegisterSession, each bounded by the response bound.

## Reality

Three readings of the standard meet in the two tests, none the other's copy. Core's test holds the
scanner's requests to the standard's layout written out by hand (the Forward_Open field by field
with its connection parameters, the Forward_Close, the encapsulation headers, the Class 1 items),
plays every refusal and failure from a scripted adapter, and hands the whole conversation to
Wireshark's dissectors (tshark 4.6.6, `~/.local/opt/wireshark`, as maquinar's adapter test finds
it), which read it with no complaint, the sizes, intervals and serial as sent and item 0x8001 in
the request and 0x8000 in the reply each with its port. The bench test meets maquinar's adapter, its
encapsulation, Connection Manager and Class 1 code its own: the outputs land, the inputs arrive, the
adapter's record of the connection is what the scanner asked, and its refusals come back by status.

Met on the way, in the test's own peer: the Forward_Open's two RPIs sit at offsets 22 and 28 of its
body (after the 6 bytes of service and path); a first draft of the peer read them at 26 and 32 and
granted nonsense, which the scanner took as given. A granted interval of 0 is refused by the
scanner; any other the adapter's to choose.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-22, run on 9f58c67): refuted, two defects and seven gaps, closed

Defects in the scanner, shown by probes:
1. Inputs were taken by the address the reply's item 0x8000 named, not by the adapter's: with
   0x8000 naming 127.0.0.2, a right frame from the adapter's own 127.0.0.1 was stray and one from
   127.0.0.2 taken. Now the adapter is the address of its session or the one its reply named (a
   device with an I/O interface of its own), and nothing else.
2. A Forward_Close unanswered, or answered wrongly, returned before the UnRegisterSession was
   sent. Now the session is unregistered whatever the close's answer.

Gaps in the tests, each a mutant that passed both files, each now caught: any datagram resetting
the watchdog (a stray spray kept a silent adapter's connection alive); the two granted intervals
swapped three ways (every test granted equal ones; now 10 ms out and 100 ms in, a 400 ms timeout
held while inputs come every 100 ms); the watchdog armed only after a first input (an adapter that
never sends is now lost at the timeout from the open); a run/idle header on every output and every
input read as run (modeless outputs and idle inputs now pinned); only the T→O granted interval
checked for 0 (the O→T one, 0, made `interval(0)` panic the thread after a successful open; now
refused and pinned); an unspecified address in the reply taken as given (now pinned as the
session's). The harness read the adapter's log before its thread had logged the UnRegisterSession
(red in 6 of 8 runs under the lock); every close now waits for that frame, and six runs under the
lock were green.

Held: the Forward_Open, Forward_Close, RegisterSession, UnRegisterSession and Class 1 frames against
CIP Volume 1 and Volume 2 as the critic knows them; every size edge each way (505 run/idle and 509
modeless accepted, 506 and 510 refused, `u16::MAX` refused without overflow); the bounds; no panic
under 60 session answers, 400 Forward_Open answers and 20 000 datagrams fuzzed; an item order
swapped or the sequenced address missing refused by the codec; the feature gating.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-23, run on 440d945): refuted, two gaps in the test, closed

Round 1's two defects reverted and its nine mutants each fail the core file, and four new ones
too (inputs from the session's address alone, any datagram from the adapter resetting the
watchdog, outputs to the session's address at the named port, an unanswered close returning Ok);
the round-1 race is gone (54 full runs under the lock, every mutant run).

The gaps: a frame of this connection's id at another size, sprayed at a silent adapter's
connection, was never tried, so a watchdog reset by any frame of the right id passed (Running
after 2 s, the controller seeing a healthy link with stale inputs); and the Forward_Open's
multiplier was checked on the wire only at 0, so a scanner sending 0 while timing out by 7
passed (the adapter would drop its outputs at 80 ms of jitter while the scanner believes it has
10.24 s). Closed: the spray holds every kind of stray (another host, another id, this id at
another size, a datagram that is no Class 1 frame), and the tests opening with multiplier 1 and 7
read it from the Forward_Open's byte 18; both mutants now fail.

A rare flake the critic saw once in about 235 runs ("a lost connection produces nothing", one frame
too many): the timeout and an output tick fell in the same millisecond and the unbiased `select!`
sent the tick first, or the adapter's recorder was still holding the last frame. The scanner's
`select!` is now biased (a close, then the timeout, then inputs, then outputs), so nothing goes
out once the timeout has passed, and the test counts on the scanner's side.

Noted outside the clause: the scanner reads the 16-bit count and not the 32-bit encapsulation
sequence, so a reordered older datagram with another count replaces newer inputs. The clause
claims the 16-bit count only (as Class 1 consumers do); a reordering on a controller's own
segment is rare, and the question is left for the controller's own I/O rules.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-23, run on a8f2ad0): refuted, one defect and one gap, closed

Every one of round 2's seventeen mutants fails the core file, each on its intended assertion; the
round-2 flake did not come back in 300 runs of its test and 72 of the file.

1. **A defect round 2's fix brought in.** With the `select!` biased inputs before outputs, the
   output tick never won while the I/O socket held a datagram, so a flood of stray datagrams (from
   two threads on another host, as fast as they go) stopped every output while the link said
   Running: 0 outputs in a flood second, 372 650 strays. The order is now a close, the timeout, the
   next output when due, then inputs; the critic's probe gave 49 to 51 outputs a second with this
   order up to 1.24 million datagrams a second sent. Pinned by a flood test (multiplier 7 so the
   timeout stays out of the way): produced and received at the adapter both 40 to 60 in the flood
   second, the link up; round 2's order fails it.
2. **A gap.** A first input with count 0 taken as a repeat (`last_count` starting at `Some(0)`, the
   common `u16 = 0` form) passed both files, since the scripted adapter starts at count 0 with the
   zeros the scanner starts with; such an adapter would leave the controller reading zeros until
   its count moved. Pinned: a first frame with count 0 and data `5A`s is taken, one update.

Two flakes, test reliability rather than a false green: `the_timeout_runs_from_the_open` measured
its upper bound from before the open, so a stall of the machine during the open turned it red under
a load of 27 to 30 (the open itself took 0 to 12 ms); the lower bound is still from before the open,
the upper one now from after it. And once in 60 runs the bench adapter could not bind its UDP
encapsulation socket on the TCP listener's ephemeral port (`AddrInUse`): maquinar's code (C-111),
carried to maquinar through the orchestrator.

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-23, run on 8ce94c9): refuted, two gaps in the test, closed, and the first-input window

Round 3's answers held: its order (inputs before the tick) failed the flood test 6 of 6 and P1 the
first-input test 3 of 3; the seventeen earlier mutants still fail; the flood test was green in 150
runs alone and 40 of the file under the lock.

The gaps: the watch counted from before the connect passed (Q7), since the scripted adapter answers
at once and the test's upper bound ran from after the open; and outputs held back until a first
input passed (Q6), since every test that counted outputs had inputs first. The critic also noted
that the bench's adapter gives a first frame 10 s, as the standard's initial watchdog does, where
the scanner gave the plain timeout. So the scanner now awaits the first input the longer of
`Spec::first_input` (default `FIRST_INPUT`, 10 s) and the timeout, from the end of the open, then
the timeout after each input; `Opened::first_input` says which, and `Lost` names the window that
ran out. Pinned: an open held 300 ms against an 80 ms timeout and a 200 ms window runs, the adapter
producing; the same open with a silent adapter is lost 150 to 450 ms after the open returned (Q7
lost it at once); an adapter that never sends, its window shortened to the timeout, gets its
outputs from the open on (Q6 sent none). Both mutants now fail. The flood test's "link up" holds at
its multiplier 7; at 0 the kernel drops the adapter's own inputs with the strays, which no scanner
can help, and the clause now says so.

## Refute round 5 (2026-11-23, run on 7c64985): refuted, two gaps in the test and one defect, closed

Q6 and Q7 now fail; every earlier mutant still fails (those of the watchdog adapted to the new
window), and five new ones are caught (the window never reset after an input, no max, `Lost`
naming the timeout, the loop using `spec.first_input`, the window ending at the first output).

The gaps, both in the new first-input window: a stray before the first input ending the window
(W1, W1b) passed, since every stray test let inputs flow first; and the window's "longer of the
two" was pinned only where several wrong formulas agree (the max taken with the requested interval
W5, without the multiplier W6, capped at 10 s W9). Pinned now: a slower grant at multiplier 1 gives
an 800 ms window over a 100 ms one, lost no sooner than 750 ms after the open; multiplier 7 gives
10.24 s over the standard's 10 s; strays of every kind for 200 ms end no 400 ms window and an input
inside it is taken, the timeout applying from then. All four mutants fail.

The defect: `Spec::first_input = Duration::MAX` (a caller waiting "forever") overflowed the deadline
and panicked the scanner's thread after the open went out, the handle then saying Running forever
and `close()` sending nothing. The deadline now saturates (a year away); pinned by a window without
end that produces, runs and closes with its Forward_Close and unregister; the old line fails it.

Seen once, not counted: under a load of 40 the bench's silent-session test measured 3.2 s against
its 900 ms ceiling, green when rerun (a stall of the machine, as round 3 saw).

## Refute round 6 (2026-11-23, run on 37ce529e): refuted, one gap in the test, closed

Round 5's mutants (W1, W1b, W5, W6, W9) and the saturation's revert fail; all 38 earlier mutants
fail one at a time under the lock (the unbiased `select!`, R0, still passes a single run: its fault
is round 2's flake, about one run in 235). Nothing new elsewhere: no overflow up to `u32::MAX` µs
times 512, receive errors, the lost path, a close or drop after a loss, the filters.

The gap: the window-without-end test checked Running 60 ms after the open, before the 80 ms
timeout, so a saturation falling back to the timeout (S3, lost at 83 ms) or to the standard's 10 s
(S2) passed. The test now holds the adapter silent past 300 ms and past `FIRST_INPUT` + 500 ms and
asserts the link Running and still producing at each; both fail it. The file now takes 10.5 s.

## Refute round 7 (2026-11-23, run on bbc14258): refuted, one defect, closed

S2 and S3 now fail the window-without-end test; S0 and S1 too; every one of the 33 older mutants
fails the core file but R0, as before.

The defect, where six rounds had not looked: `Spec::io_bind` took an IPv6 address. Bound to `[::]`
(dual-stack, `net.ipv6.bindv6only` 0 here), the socket sees an IPv4 sender as `::ffff:127.0.0.1`,
which is neither the session's address nor the one the reply named, so every input of a healthy
adapter was stray: against the bench's adapter, 33 inputs produced, none taken, the link lost at its
first-input window while 30 outputs went. EtherNet/IP's Sockaddr Info items carry IPv4 alone, as
the bench's adapter says of its own bind (C-111), so the scanner now refuses an IPv6 address for
the I/O socket or for the adapter before anything is sent, each by its reason; both refusals are
pinned in the bounds test with no connection made.

## Refute round 8 (2026-11-23, run on b143a0b2): refuted, one defect and one gap, closed

Round 7's refusals hold: removing either fails the bounds test, and the critic's IPv6 adapter
listening on `[::1]` was refused with no connection.

The defect: "the address of its session" was the spec's address, not the session's. An adapter
named `0.0.0.0` is reached at the local host (127.0.0.1 on Linux), so with the reply naming
`0.0.0.0` too, every one of its inputs was stray and the link lost at its first-input window while
the adapter saw a healthy connection. The scanner now takes the session's address from the stream's
peer (`Opened::session`) for the input filter and for both fallbacks (no 0x8000 item, or an
unspecified address in it). Pinned on Linux: an adapter named by the unspecified address runs,
judged by the peer its session reached, the reply's `0.0.0.0` read as that peer; the old filter
fails it.

The gap: the IPv6 adapter's refusal was checked against a port nothing listened on, so a refusal
made after the connect passed. The bounds test now points it at a listening `[::1]` and asserts no
connection reached it.
