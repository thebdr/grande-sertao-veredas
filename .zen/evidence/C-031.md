# C-031 evidence - the simulated controller

## The design (2026-11-06)

The engine is pure: `tick(elapsed_ms, peers)` and `on_message(name, fields, peer)` are the whole
interface, with a status to read and knobs to turn. No socket, no sleep, no wall clock but the
one a `now` field asks for. A test drives it at chosen instants and pins every output exactly;
the bench channel (C-029) drives it from the wire with real elapsed time. This is the
generalisation of Veredas 1's `mscsim` (a hard-coded conversation of one vendor's messages) and
of `veredas-trajetorias`' automated replies (random or rule-based destinations): the items, the
reply, the outcome, the destinations' state bits, the answers, the commands and the cyclic
messages are all the rules file's (C-030), and no vendor, device or product is named anywhere.

Scheduling: every due time (the next item, each cyclic message) is the last due time plus the
interval, stepped past the clock when a stall missed some (`step_past`), so a late tick never
stretches an interval and a stall keeps the phase; pinned on ticks of 10, 30 and 70 ms giving
the same count over 10 s, and on a stall of ten intervals announcing one.

The conversation over a socket is the worked example of the reference: the machine listening,
the unit dialling, both on the channel schema's worked file; items, answers, outcomes and
heartbeats flow, the monitors show every message's fields, and the knobs are turned live (every
chute full and back, the register stopping the announcements and a command from the unit
starting them, the no-read fault turned to all).

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-06): refuted

The mix was never shown to draw by weight (every mixed outcome was asserted as one of two
values; a draw always taking the first entry would have passed), and a reply with no
destination field, when the file has destinations and no outcome, was counted as an invalid
destination. Now a thousand deliveries pin 850 to 950 sorted and 50 to 150 lost with the ended
counts agreeing, a weight of 0 in front is never drawn with the shares 3 to 1 holding, and a
reply with no destination field names none (the note `1 to -`, nothing counted). Held:
`step_past` at its edges, the first item after a long wait, a stall, `tick(0)` repeated, the
interval raised or lowered, the bench's seed over the file's, delayed answers, the history cap,
the socket test's tolerances and its latencies.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-07): refuted on the sticky reject

The round-1 gaps held closed, and the burst, the resume, the gate, `announce_now` with no peer,
the history cap, the channel's millisecond carry and the socket tolerances were found sound. A
decision remembered its reject: a code rejected while every chute was full kept going to 9
after the chutes came back (probed on the worked unit, seed 11). A reject is never remembered
now; the reference and the clause say so, and the engine test pins the code rejected while full
drawing again and the code remembered before the full period keeping its chute.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-07): holds

The reject is never stored and the old entry kept; a code rejected while full draws again and a
code remembered before keeps its chute, both pinned; a blocked memory re-decided by a rule or
the spread replaces its entry in place. Noted, not a gap: the sticky memory's cap evicts by
when a key was first remembered, which is what "the newest 10000 keys" means here.

## Found by CI (2026-11-08): one stream, four purposes

The bench file's seed test failed on the Linux runner (f3934b9) and once in eight local runs: the
controller drew its items, its outcomes, its cyclic messages and its answers from one stream, so
the order in which a tick carried them (an outcome landing before or after the next announce,
which a long tick on a starved host reorders) shifted every later item's weight, and two runs
under one seed disagreed. The draws are now a stream per purpose (Pcg32 streams 9 to 12), so an
item's fields depend on its place alone; pinned without a clock by ten ticks of 100 ms against
one of 1000 ms rendering the same eleven weights, which the old code failed. The bench file test
also read the items from the status at a moment they could be travelling (the status lists the
awaiting and the ended, not the travelling), and counted a peer's recent lines before the four it
wanted had been kept; both reads now wait for what they assert (C-033).
