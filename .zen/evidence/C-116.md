# C-116: the PROFINET device's record read

## Why now, and why on the raw link

Aranha's plan reads the bench device's port data by the implicit read in its phase 6 ("fixture
first"), and its own codecs for the read (`profinet::rpc`, C-137) and the records
(`profinet::record`, C-138) were on `main`: the device answers with them, so the scanner and the
device cannot drift apart. A real device has no host stack beside it: the bench device answers ARP
for the address it holds and reads IPv4 and UDP itself, on the same raw link as its DCP and LLDP,
at the address DCP gave it.

## The client is the kernel

The test gives the far end of the veth pair an address and talks through a plain UDP socket. The
kernel resolves the device by ARP (its neighbour table then holds the device's MAC) and drops any
datagram whose IPv4 or UDP checksum is wrong, so both are proven by code the bench did not write.
Aranha's codecs build the request and read the answer; tshark is the second reading, every
checksum checked good.

## Found on the way

- **A veth leaves the UDP checksum to an offload that never happens.** The device, checking the
  checksum as a device does, dropped every read: a raw socket on the device's end reads the partial
  sum the sender left. The test turns transmit offload off on the client's end (`ethtool -K a0 tx
  off`), as a real card writes the whole checksum on the wire. ARP worked from the start.
- **Wireshark's checksum status** reads 1 for good (0 bad, 2 unverified); a PNIORW error's code 1
  sits under another field name, so the test checks the error code (0xDE) and its decode (0x80).

## What it holds back

No connection (Connect, the parameters, PrmEnd, ApplicationReady) and no cyclic data: those come
with core's IO controller. No I&M1 to I&M4, no record write. One port, one neighbour.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-23): refuted, answered

Sixteen of the critic's eighteen mutants passed the test; four probes showed real faults, all in
how the neighbour was written:
- **A neighbour id that was not short visible ASCII made the device refuse its own port data**
  with DE 80 B0 00, "invalid index" (a chassis "schalter-ü", or 100 bytes of another subtype): an
  encoding failure was mapped to that status. Ids are now written to fit (visible ASCII kept, other
  bytes as \xHH or hex, at most 255 characters), and a record that still will not encode is a read
  error (DE 80 A0 00), never an index the device lacks.
- **Port ids other than a local one** (an interface name, what most switches send; a MAC) and
  network or other chassis ids were written as Rust's debug text: now as their text by subtype.
- **Any station's shutdown frame cleared the neighbour**: now only the neighbour's own (802.1AB
  names a neighbour by its chassis and port).
- **An API other than 0** is refused DE 80 B4 00 (invalid area/API), not B2; the record is cut to
  what the request's ArgsMaximum leaves after the 64-byte header too; a muted device mutes its DCP
  alone (the clause says so).
- **Pinned now** (the probes, the test's `round_1` module): the cut, another slot, another
  operation, interface or packet type, the activity in either byte order, a last fragment, the
  unspecified address, a computed-zero checksum, the whole ARP reply, one ARP answer exactly.
