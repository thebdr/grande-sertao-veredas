# C-116: the PROFINET device's record read

## Why now, and why on the raw link

Aranha's plan reads the bench device's port data by the implicit read in its phase 6 ("fixture
first"), and its own codecs for the read (`profinet::rpc`, C-137) and the records
(`profinet::record`, C-138) were on `main`: the device answers with them, so the scanner and the
device cannot drift apart. A real device has no host stack beside it: the bench device answers ARP
for the address it holds and reads IPv4 and UDP itself, on the same raw link as its DCP and LLDP,
at the address DCP gave it.

## The client is the kernel

The test gives the far end of the veth pair an address and talks through a plain UDP socket. The
kernel resolves the device by ARP (its neighbour table then holds the device's MAC) and drops any
datagram whose IPv4 or UDP checksum is wrong, so both are proven by code the bench did not write.
Aranha's codecs build the request and read the answer; tshark is the second reading, every
checksum checked good.

## Found on the way

- **A veth leaves the UDP checksum to an offload that never happens.** The device, checking the
  checksum as a device does, dropped every read: a raw socket on the device's end reads the partial
  sum the sender left. The test turns transmit offload off on the client's end (`ethtool -K a0 tx
  off`), as a real card writes the whole checksum on the wire. ARP worked from the start.
- **Wireshark's checksum status** reads 1 for good (0 bad, 2 unverified); a PNIORW error's code 1
  sits under another field name, so the test checks the error code (0xDE) and its decode (0x80).

## What it holds back

No connection (Connect, the parameters, PrmEnd, ApplicationReady) and no cyclic data: those come
with core's IO controller. No I&M1 to I&M4, no record write. One port, one neighbour.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-23): refuted, answered

Sixteen of the critic's eighteen mutants passed the test; four probes showed real faults, all in
how the neighbour was written:
- **A neighbour id that was not short visible ASCII made the device refuse its own port data**
  with DE 80 B0 00, "invalid index" (a chassis "schalter-ü", or 100 bytes of another subtype): an
  encoding failure was mapped to that status. Ids are now written to fit (visible ASCII kept, other
  bytes as \xHH or hex, at most 255 characters), and a record that still will not encode is a read
  error (DE 80 A0 00), never an index the device lacks.
- **Port ids other than a local one** (an interface name, what most switches send; a MAC) and
  network or other chassis ids were written as Rust's debug text: now as their text by subtype.
- **Any station's shutdown frame cleared the neighbour**: now only the neighbour's own (802.1AB
  names a neighbour by its chassis and port).
- **An API other than 0** is refused DE 80 B4 00 (invalid area/API), not B2; the record is cut to
  what the request's ArgsMaximum leaves after the 64-byte header too; a muted device mutes its DCP
  alone (the clause says so).
- **Pinned now** (the probes, the test's `round_1` module): the cut, another slot, another
  operation, interface or packet type, the activity in either byte order, a last fragment, the
  unspecified address, a computed-zero checksum, the whole ARP reply, one ARP answer exactly.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-23): refuted, answered

Round 1's answers held, five of them unpinned. The critic's probes and mutants are in
`~/.cache/veredas/critic-c116-r2/probes`; each finding is a pin in the test's `round_2` module.
- **A name not UTF-8 came out as hex**: aranha files a local chassis or an interface name whose
  bytes are not UTF-8 under another subtype, and the device wrote the whole id as hex. Those name
  subtypes (chassis 6 and 7, port 1, 5 and 7) keep their bytes, escaped `\xHH`.
- **The neighbour never aged out**: 802.1AB drops a remote's information when its TTL runs out;
  the device now keeps the deadline its last frame gave and states no peer past it.
- **B2 at subslots it has**: an index the subslot does not carry is DE 80 B0 00 ("invalid index"),
  B2 only a slot or subslot the device lacks (IEC 61158-6-10's PNIORW codes); the API is checked
  first, for any index.
- **An ArgsMaximum under 64 got 64 bytes back**, past its MaximumCount: it is now refused DE 80 A0 00
  with no data at all, which also gives the read error a case the wire reaches.
- **A fragment of several was answered as whole**, and **a frame to another MAC** carrying a read to
  its address: neither is now (the device reassembles nothing; DCP already checked the MAC).
- **The cuts** fall at a whole escape or byte (a cut had split `\xc3` or left half a byte), and a
  backslash is written `\x5c`, now stated, so an escape reads one way.
- **Unpinned answers of round 1**, now pinned: the 255-character cut, network addresses and other
  subtypes as text, another neighbour's TTL-0 whatever it shares, odd-length checksums, the cut
  past the 64-byte header.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-23): refuted, answered

Round 2's answers held, four of them pinned on one case where the clause's words cover more. The
critic's probes and mutants are in `~/.cache/veredas/critic-c116-r3/probes`; each finding is a pin
in the test's `round_3` module.
- **Another station's TTL-0 cleared the neighbour when its ids only read the same**: the device
  compared the ids as written for the port data (a local chassis and an interface name of one text,
  two names alike in their first 255 characters, bytes written as text and other bytes whose hex
  reads the same). 802.1AB names a neighbour by its chassis and port TLVs; the device now keeps the
  ids its frame carried (aranha decodes a subtype and its bytes one way) and compares those.
- **A chassis or port component or an interface alias came out as hex** past visible ASCII where a
  port's interface alias was escaped: 802.1AB's text subtypes are a chassis's 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7 and
  a port's 1, 2, 5 and 7, now every one a name. Round 1's and round 2's hex pins moved to a reserved
  chassis subtype (8); a network address of another family as hex with its family byte, now said.
- **An ARP request in a frame to another MAC was answered and counted**, where a card drops it: the
  device answers one broadcast or to its own MAC, as round 2 drew the line for datagrams.
- **Unpinned claims, each with a surviving mutant**: a neighbour heard again keeping its first
  deadline (now kept to its last frame's TTL, on the machine's lock); the A0 answer carrying a cut
  header ("no data at all" was asserted as "no more than asked"); the slot checked before the API
  where the slot is missing; chassis 6 and port 1 and 7 not UTF-8 written as hex; LLDP ignored while
  muted; ARP requests for other addresses counted.

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-25): refuted, answered

Round 3's answers held, and rounds 1 and 2's, each reverted as a mutant and caught by its pin. The
critic's probes and mutants are in `~/.cache/veredas/critic-c116-r4/probes`; each finding is a pin
in the test's `round_4` module.
- **LLDP was taken whatever its destination**: a frame to another station's MAC, broadcast, any
  group (the link joins every one) or the device's own MAC became the neighbour. LLDP is taken now
  only to 802.1AB's nearest-bridge group address, 01:80:C2:00:00:0E, as PROFINET sends it.
- **Unpinned claims, each with a surviving mutant**: a frame heard again with a shorter TTL (the
  later deadline kept passed), the same ids' TTL-0 from another source MAC, bytes at the edges of
  visible ASCII (DEL and control bytes kept raw made the record fail to encode), unanswered
  datagrams counted refused, the read error's activity and sequence, a header with IPv4 options.
- **Wording**: "outside visible ASCII (under 0x20, 0x7F and up)", not "past"; a serial number "not
  up to 16 visible characters", as the reference has it; another interface judged by its UUID, its
  version and the object UUID not judged (the critic saw them answered; IEC 61158-6-10 was not
  checked to ground a refusal).
