# C-151 evidence - the engine of a scan

Reworked from Veredas 1's `orcl-runtime` (`task.rs`, `clock.rs`; its real-time setup not carried):
`docs/orcl/README.md`, "What it absorbs from Veredas 1", says what changed and why.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-22): refuted
The critic ran 31 mutants against the recorded command, in a scratch copy. Four gaps, each with a
surviving mutant:
- **Lateness was never anything but zero.** On virtual time every scan started on its deadline, so
  lateness recorded as zero, reversed, or its maximum taken as the last value all passed; so did a
  program handed its deadline in place of its start, inputs read at the deadline, and a fault dated
  at the deadline. Now a host comes back late (13 ms for a scan due at 10): the scan is handed 13 ms,
  reads its inputs then, and lateness reads 3 ms maximum, 3 total, 1 mean over three scans; a fault
  that starts late is dated at its start (14 ms).
- **A scan's longest length and its mean** were pinned only where every scan took 3 ms. Now pinned
  over 1, 1, 15, 1, 20, 1, 1, 1 ms: 20 ms the longest, 41 in all, 5.125 the mean.
- **Safe outputs could not be told from default ones**: the test program's safe value was 0, its
  type's default, so an engine writing defaults passed. Its safe value is now 7.
- **What reached the plant after the fault was not read**: an engine that stopped writing once
  faulted, or wrote twice, passed. Every write the plant received is now listed, one per scan at its
  end.

Held, killed by the tests as they stood: the time error at `>=`, the fault at three periods or at
`>=`, the scan's own outputs written before the fault check, catching up instead of skipping, the
exact-end boundary, virtual time going back, the fault dated at the scan's end, a faulted task
still scanning, inputs read twice, a real clock without its spin, skips off by one, faulted scans
left out of the statistics, the refusal's text, the fault naming the period, overruns uncounted, a
zero period accepted, clones of the virtual clock not sharing its time. The real-clock test was run
15 times on one CPU shared with three busy loops: green each time; it asserts only what holds under
any load.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-22): refuted
Every survivor of round 1 was killed. Fresh mutants found three more gaps:
- **No test program took time itself**: the clock moved only in the plant's read, so an engine
  that measured a scan before the program ran passed, blind to the very thing its watchdog guards.
  Programs that move the virtual clock in their own scan now run 15 ms (a time error) and 25 ms (the
  fault), the outputs written after them.
- **Nothing told a scan's length from its lateness**: an engine measuring from the deadline passed.
  A scan started 8 ms late running 5 ms is now no time error, and one started 9 ms late running 15 ms
  a time error and no fault.
- **Faulted scans never started late**: one now does, reading at its start, its lateness counted.

Three survivors on points the clause did not state, now stated and pinned: the faulting scan is a
time error; once faulted, no scan is a time error or an overrun and the fault keeps its first cause
and date; a program is handed its outputs as the last scan left them. Equivalent mutants left:
`first_due >= next`, the faulted branch leaving the outputs as they are, writing at `clock.now()`.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-22): refuted, and a defect in the engine
Every mutant of rounds 1 and 2 was killed but three equivalents. Fresh survivors, each now caught:
a host back more than a period late (an engine also measuring lateness, or capping it, passed);
the time error's own limit (a tolerance of a few milliseconds passed; now 10 ms and 1 ns); the fault's
length (a later faulted scan rewriting it passed: now 30 ms after 25); the statistics across a fault;
`VirtualClock` really sleeping (now a 10 s wait timed on the wall); a deadline computed in 32 bits
(cycles past 2^32); outputs left by a scan that was a time error; a refusal always naming `main`.

**The defect.** The engine judged a scan's length and the time-error rule when the outputs were handed
over, but the skipped deadlines after the plant's write returned. Every test plant wrote at once, so
nothing showed it; on a real clock every write takes time, and with a plant taking 1 ms to write, a scan
of exactly one period skipped the deadline at its end. The engine now judges all three at the one
moment the outputs are handed over: the plant's own time to take them is not the scan's, and a
deadline passing meanwhile runs late, its lateness counted. The clause says so; a plant whose write
takes 1 ms pins it, after a scan of exactly 10 ms and after one of 9.5 ms.

Considered: counting the write in the scan, as an S7 counts its output transfer in the cycle time,
would leave the fault (decided before the write, so that a faulting scan never writes its own outputs)
measured on another length than the time error. On a target the write is a copy into the bus thread's
buffer (`docs/controlador/execution-model.md`, point 6).

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-22): refuted
Every earlier mutant killed but the equivalents (`first_due >= next`, the faulted branch leaving the
outputs, writing at `clock.now()`, wrapping deadlines) and one the clause does not cover (a faulted
scan's report keeping stale inputs; the plant's read itself is logged and asserted). Six mutants on
the new "a scan ends when its outputs are handed over" were killed; five survived, now caught:
- **The statistics and the overrun count after a slow write** were never read: a length or an overrun
  taken after the write passed. Now read after a 10 ms scan and a 1 ms write.
- **The watchdog with a slow write**: a second check after the write, or a fault length taken after it,
  passed. A 19.5 ms scan with a 1 ms write is now no fault, and a 25 ms one names 25 ms.
- **Lateness was never late twice**, so a maximum taken as the total passed. Now 3 ms then 2 ms.
- **The clause's sentence was wrong for a write spanning two deadlines**: the engine runs the first late
  and skips the second at that scan's end, never catching up, while the sentence said every such
  deadline runs late. The sentence now says what the engine does, for a slow write and for a host away
  alike, and both are pinned with their skip counts (the host's were never read on virtual time).

## Refute round 9 (2026-11-22): refuted, no defect, and a change of method
Resumed after the machine's cut of 20:25 from its own results. 144 of 158 earlier mutants killed, the rest
those set aside and one (`P7`, a faulted late scan's skips judged at its start) the note had missed. Fresh
survivors: lateness under a millisecond never read (moderate: on a target lateness is jitter, almost always
under a millisecond), the skip rules on faulting and faulted scans (low), safe outputs taken once when the
task is made (low), the program handed a time rounded down (low).

Nine rounds showed the pattern: the engine right since round 3, and each round finding a dimension the
examples had not tried, because the clause states universal sentences (to the nanosecond whatever the period,
every cycle run or counted, maxima and means) that examples prove one point at a time. So the clause's
command now holds a model of the clause (`tests/model.rs`), written apart from the engine in nanoseconds and
run in lockstep with it over 4000 random sequences drawn to land on deadlines and a nanosecond either side,
every observable compared after every step, and a count that the draws reach each case the clause names. Its
life was checked on round 9's survivors in a copy: lateness kept in whole milliseconds, lateness under a
millisecond as zero, a faulted or a faulting scan skipping the deadline at its end, a faulted scan's skips
judged at its start, the program handed its deadline or a time cut to the millisecond; each compiled and
failed on the model's assertions.

## Refute round 12 (2026-11-23): refuted, two low gaps in the draws' reach, no engine defect
The critic read the model against every sentence of the clause and found no misreading shared with the engine.
Round 11's additions hold. 217 of 228 earlier mutants are killed, the 11 left in the classes set aside. Replaying
the draws showed where they still stop. No watchdog fault was ever drawn after scan 49, after more than 17 time
errors, after more than 6 in a row, or after more than 34 skipped deadlines. No scan was ever more than 6 periods
late. And the long-absence test read only the skip counts, never the late scan itself. So two classes passed (low):
- the watchdog switched off late in a run (after 64 or 1000 scans, past cycle 1000, after 20 time errors, after 8
  in a row, after 100 skips);
- a runtime that resynchronises after a long absence (running the latest passed deadline), or one that counts no
  lateness for it, caps the lateness, resets the output image, exempts the late scan from the rules, or faults on
  the absence.

Now answered in the model, its draws shared by its runs and by its count of what they reach:
- one step in twenty, the host is away for up to a million periods, back on a deadline, a nanosecond past one,
  or anywhere in a period;
- a third mix of 100 runs of 2000 scans holds every scan to twice the period until a step drawn between the 60th
  and the 1960th, after a drawn streak of up to 40 time errors, where the task faults;
- the long-absence test reads the late scan after 300 periods and after 2^33: its cycle, its time, no time
  error, still running, its lateness counted.

The draws are counted to reach a fault past the 1000th scan (49 times), after more than 20 time errors (100),
after more than 10 in a row (82), after 100 skips, and a running and a faulted scan late by 1000 periods or more,
each at least 20 times.

Outside the clause as written, now added to it: a `MonoClock` that runs slow, reads behind, or truncates to whole
milliseconds or microseconds passed, every real-clock check being a lower bound. The clause now says the clock
keeps the wall's pace. The check, which holds under any load, reads the wall and then the clock, and requires the
clock plus the wall's head start to reach the wall.

All 243 mutants of rounds 2 to 12 (round 12's controls and edges among them) were run again: 231 killed with the
new check, all of round 12's W, A and K survivors among them. The 12 that pass are the classes set aside: `M5a`,
`N25`, `N26`, `F8b`, `I6`, `H19x`, `G12` to `G14`, `R4` and `S13`, and `N4b`, a clock waking 100 ns early, set

deadline and the early wake is not seen.

## Refute round 13 (2026-11-23): refuted, three low gaps in what the tests see, no engine defect
The critic found no misreading the model shares with the engine. Round 12's draws do what the clause says, and
every earlier mutant is killed but the classes set aside (232 of 243; `N4b` was killed on that run). Its measure
of where the tests stop showed three low gaps:
- **Counts past 16 bits.** No test ran past 2200 scans or 778 time errors, so scans or overruns counted in 16 bits
  (wrapping or saturating) passed, the class of round 11's `S2` with its edge moved.
- **Long waits.** The longest wait on a target's clock was 20 ms, so a clock that, for longer waits, slept and
  returned without its spin (up to 300 µs early) passed, round 5's `H24` with its edge moved. A 50 or 100 ms
  task waits that long every cycle.
- **A clock frozen between waits.** The pace check read the clock right after a wait, where a clock frozen
  between waits is right, and the target-clock task's program took no time. Such a clock measures a 5 ms scan as
  nothing, so on a target the time-error rule and the watchdog would go blind.

Now caught:
- 70 000 scans of one and a half periods are each counted (scans, time errors, skips) past 16 bits, the mean and
  longest length exact, the task still running;
- waits of 25, 50 and 100 ms are judged on the clock and on the wall;
- the pace check reads after 100 µs of spinning on the wall;
- a program spinning 5 ms on a 2 ms period, on a target's clock, is measured at 5 ms or more, a time error and a
  fault, which load only makes longer.

The critic's probes, taken in as they stood, were green on the engine. The reach count now counts running scans
late by 1000 periods apart from faulted ones, as the clause names them.

All 278 mutants of rounds 2 to 13 were run on the new tests: 260 killed, every low survivor of round 13 among
them. The 18 that pass are the 12 set aside and six the critic named contrived:
- thresholds placed just past the draws: the output image reset after 30 days or 2^20 periods away, and the
  watchdog off for a scan 2^20 periods late;
- a clock that wraps at 2^32 ns, one kept in `f32` seconds, and one 1 ppm slow, each within the tests' window
  or the check's margin.

## Refute round 14 (2026-11-23): refuted, one low gap, no engine defect
The critic re-read the clause sentence by sentence and found every verification item realised by a test that runs
its case. Round 13's additions hold, the model shares no misreading with the engine, and every earlier mutant is
killed but the classes set aside (261 of 278).

One low gap: nothing pinned that `MonoClock` only counts forward. Swapped for `SystemTime`, the system's settable
clock, it passed every test, because the wall the tests judge against runs at the same rate and no test can step
the system's clock without privileges. On a controller the steps happen: NTP's first sync after a dead battery,
or systemd's rewind (`docs/controlador/linux-study.md`). A forward step during a scan would read as a watchdog
fault and turn the controller to FAULT (C-152); a backward one would move the reading back.

Since no behaviour can show it, the promise is read in the source. Every file of `src/` is read, with comment
lines aside and whitespace removed: none names `SystemTime` or `UNIX_EPOCH`, `clock.rs` imports `Instant` from
`std::time`, and `MonoClock` is started at `Instant::now()` and read as `self.start.elapsed()`. The clause says
so, and why the check is in the source.

The check also kills the contrived clocks of round 13 whose reading changes the source (wrapping at 2^32 ns, kept
in `f32`, 1 ppm slow) and round 14's 1 ppm fast one.

Contrived, not counted:
- `U5`: a mean lateness over a 16-bit count while the count itself is right, a narrowing placed just past the draws;
- `U12`, `U13`: where the faulting scan's end is read relative to its safe outputs. No test gives the safe outputs
  any time: ORCL's are a declared value, and the rule is judged before they are computed;
- `V1`: a wait over 100 ms returning without its spin, round 13's edge moved again.

All 299 mutants of rounds 2 to 14 were run on the new tests: 280 killed. The 19 that pass are the 12 set aside,
round 13's three threshold edges, and round 14's four contrived ones.
