# C-151 evidence - the engine of a scan

Reworked from Veredas 1's `orcl-runtime` (`task.rs`, `clock.rs`; its real-time setup not carried):
`docs/orcl/README.md`, "What it absorbs from Veredas 1", says what changed and why.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-22): refuted
The critic ran 31 mutants against the recorded command, in a scratch copy. Four gaps, each with a
surviving mutant:
- **Lateness was never anything but zero.** On virtual time every scan started on its deadline, so
  lateness recorded as zero, reversed, or its maximum taken as the last value all passed; so did a
  program handed its deadline in place of its start, inputs read at the deadline, and a fault dated
  at the deadline. Now a host comes back late (13 ms for a scan due at 10): the scan is handed 13 ms,
  reads its inputs then, and lateness reads 3 ms maximum, 3 total, 1 mean over three scans; a fault
  that starts late is dated at its start (14 ms).
- **A scan's longest length and its mean** were pinned only where every scan took 3 ms. Now pinned
  over 1, 1, 15, 1, 20, 1, 1, 1 ms: 20 ms the longest, 41 in all, 5.125 the mean.
- **Safe outputs could not be told from default ones**: the test program's safe value was 0, its
  type's default, so an engine writing defaults passed. Its safe value is now 7.
- **What reached the plant after the fault was not read**: an engine that stopped writing once
  faulted, or wrote twice, passed. Every write the plant received is now listed, one per scan at its
  end.

Held, killed by the tests as they stood: the time error at `>=`, the fault at three periods or at
`>=`, the scan's own outputs written before the fault check, catching up instead of skipping, the
exact-end boundary, virtual time going back, the fault dated at the scan's end, a faulted task
still scanning, inputs read twice, a real clock without its spin, skips off by one, faulted scans
left out of the statistics, the refusal's text, the fault naming the period, overruns uncounted, a
zero period accepted, clones of the virtual clock not sharing its time. The real-clock test was run
15 times on one CPU shared with three busy loops: green each time; it asserts only what holds under
any load.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-22): refuted
Every survivor of round 1 was killed. Fresh mutants found three more gaps:
- **No test program took time itself**: the clock moved only in the plant's read, so an engine
  that measured a scan before the program ran passed, blind to the very thing its watchdog guards.
  Programs that move the virtual clock in their own scan now run 15 ms (a time error) and 25 ms (the
  fault), the outputs written after them.
- **Nothing told a scan's length from its lateness**: an engine measuring from the deadline passed.
  A scan started 8 ms late running 5 ms is now no time error, and one started 9 ms late running 15 ms
  a time error and no fault.
- **Faulted scans never started late**: one now does, reading at its start, its lateness counted.

Three survivors on points the clause did not state, now stated and pinned: the faulting scan is a
time error; once faulted, no scan is a time error or an overrun and the fault keeps its first cause
and date; a program is handed its outputs as the last scan left them. Equivalent mutants left:
`first_due >= next`, the faulted branch leaving the outputs as they are, writing at `clock.now()`.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-22): refuted, and a defect in the engine
Every mutant of rounds 1 and 2 was killed but three equivalents. Fresh survivors, each now caught:
a host back more than a period late (an engine also measuring lateness, or capping it, passed);
the time error's own limit (a tolerance of a few milliseconds passed; now 10 ms and 1 ns); the fault's
length (a later faulted scan rewriting it passed: now 30 ms after 25); the statistics across a fault;
`VirtualClock` really sleeping (now a 10 s wait timed on the wall); a deadline computed in 32 bits
(cycles past 2^32); outputs left by a scan that was a time error; a refusal always naming `main`.

**The defect.** The engine judged a scan's length and the time-error rule when the outputs were handed
over, but the skipped deadlines after the plant's write returned. Every test plant wrote at once, so
nothing showed it; on a real clock every write takes time, and with a plant taking 1 ms to write, a scan
of exactly one period skipped the deadline at its end. The engine now judges all three at the one
moment the outputs are handed over: the plant's own time to take them is not the scan's, and a
deadline passing meanwhile runs late, its lateness counted. The clause says so; a plant whose write
takes 1 ms pins it, after a scan of exactly 10 ms and after one of 9.5 ms.

Considered: counting the write in the scan, as an S7 counts its output transfer in the cycle time,
would leave the fault (decided before the write, so that a faulting scan never writes its own outputs)
measured on another length than the time error. On a target the write is a copy into the bus thread's
buffer (`docs/controlador/execution-model.md`, point 6).

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-22): refuted
Every earlier mutant killed but the equivalents (`first_due >= next`, the faulted branch leaving the
outputs, writing at `clock.now()`, wrapping deadlines) and one the clause does not cover (a faulted
scan's report keeping stale inputs; the plant's read itself is logged and asserted). Six mutants on
the new "a scan ends when its outputs are handed over" were killed; five survived, now caught:
- **The statistics and the overrun count after a slow write** were never read: a length or an overrun
  taken after the write passed. Now read after a 10 ms scan and a 1 ms write.
- **The watchdog with a slow write**: a second check after the write, or a fault length taken after it,
  passed. A 19.5 ms scan with a 1 ms write is now no fault, and a 25 ms one names 25 ms.
- **Lateness was never late twice**, so a maximum taken as the total passed. Now 3 ms then 2 ms.
- **The clause's sentence was wrong for a write spanning two deadlines**: the engine runs the first late
  and skips the second at that scan's end, never catching up, while the sentence said every such
  deadline runs late. The sentence now says what the engine does, for a slow write and for a host away
  alike, and both are pinned with their skip counts (the host's were never read on virtual time).

## Refute round 5 (2026-11-22): refuted
Every earlier mutant killed but the equivalents and those outside the clause; the critic adds as
contrived a `MonoClock` waking 100 ns or 20 ns early, below what a debug build can observe. Fresh
survivors, now caught:
- **The faulted scans' lengths in the maximum** (moderate, the verification line claimed it): the only
  test with a faulted scan longer than the faulting one never read `max_exec`. It now reads 30 ms.
- **Periods that are no whole number of milliseconds** (moderate): deadlines built on a period cut to whole
  milliseconds or microseconds passed, every test period being whole. Periods of 2.5 ms, 500 µs and
  333 333 ns now keep their deadlines.
- A schedule anchored at the first step instead of the clock's zero (low): the clause now says "from the
  clock's zero", and a first step at 5 ms runs scan 0 late and scan 1 at 10 ms.
- A wait over 5 ms returning early on a real clock (low): waits of 6, 10 and 20 ms are now tried.
Also pinned: a mean kept to the nanosecond. Outside: the output image's state before scan 0; the mean's
precision below the nanosecond.

## Refute round 6 (2026-11-22): refuted, no defect
101 of 112 earlier mutants killed, the 11 left equivalent, outside or contrived (the note's, plus a
`MonoClock` sleeping the whole wait then spinning, never early; `advance` not saturating at
`Duration::MAX`; a no-op). Fresh survivors, now caught:
- **What the program is handed** (moderate): the late-start test read the engine's report, not the scan
  the program received, so a program handed its deadline while the report showed the start passed. The
  program's scan is now compared with the report's on late and skipped scans.
- **Periods of a second and more** (moderate): a schedule built on the period's sub-second nanoseconds
  alone passed, every period tried being under 10 ms. Periods of 1, 1.5 and 5 s now keep their deadlines.
- A skip judged on a period cut to whole microseconds (low), visible only after about a thousand scans:
  2000 scans of 333 333 ns now skip nothing.
- A first step more than a period late (low): at 25 ms it runs scan 0 late and counts two skipped.
Outside: the means before any scan; the period handed to the program, which the clause does not name.

## Refute round 7 (2026-11-22): refuted, an error of round 6
Every earlier mutant killed but those set aside, and one: round 6 above says the program's scan "is now
compared with the report's on late and skipped scans", and the test it added ran no late scan: its host
came back at 29 ms, before the deadline at 30, so nothing started late and a program handed its deadline
in place of its start still passed (moderate). The host now comes back at 34 ms, the scan is handed 34 ms,
and the test asserts a lateness of 4 ms so it cannot go vacuous again. Also pinned (low): the overrun of a
time error that started late counted; the faulting scan's own lateness; a faulted late scan's lateness in
the total and the mean as well as the maximum.

## Refute round 8 (2026-11-22): refuted, no defect
135 of 147 earlier mutants killed, the rest those set aside. Every test added for a past gap was checked
against its own arithmetic and runs the case it names. Fresh survivors, now caught:
- **The target's clock judged only against its own reading** (moderate): a `MonoClock` running a thousand
  times fast, or twice, or a millisecond ahead, passed, every wait being measured by the clock itself.
  Both real-clock tests now also judge against a wall clock started before it, which holds under any load.
- A late scan's skips judged at its start (low): a scan from 19 to 34 ms now skips the deadlines at 20 and
  30 ms, and the next runs at 40; its 9 ms of lateness are counted.
- A late faulting scan's time error and overrun (low), now read.
Contrived and left: a program handed default inputs on its first scan alone (every test plant's first
snapshot is the default).
