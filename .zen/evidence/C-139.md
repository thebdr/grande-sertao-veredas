# C-139 evidence - the wire seam

## The seam, the capture file and the hub (2026-11-23)

Phase 4's first piece (`docs/aranha-plan.md`): the one seam the listener, fusion and asking will
sit on, with its three kinds. Absorbed from Veredas 1's `wire.rs` and `pcap.rs`
(`/home/thebdr/code/Veredas`, branch `supervisao-plan`), changed on the way:
- **Stamps in nanoseconds**, not milliseconds: a raw link's clock and a nanosecond capture keep
  their precision, and the writer writes nanosecond pcap (Veredas 1 wrote microseconds).
- **A frame keeps its length on the wire**, so a capture cut by its snapshot length says so
  (`Frame::is_cut`), and writing it back keeps it.
- **A capture cut short keeps the frames before the damage**, named, where Veredas 1 refused the
  whole file: a capture still being written is read up to where it stops, as tshark reads it.
- **The memory hub waits** up to its timeout (Veredas 1's returned at once, which a listener's
  loop would spin on), bounds each port's queue and counts what it lets go, and a dropped port
  leaves the segment.
- **The seam never hands back a frame this host sent**: the raw link behind it passes them over
  within the same wait, so a listener's own sends stay apart from what it heard.
- Typed errors with messages, the pcapng refusal named apart from another magic number.

Spiked against tshark 4.6.6 before the tests were written (seventeen hand-made files, and
tshark's own rewrites with `-F pcap`, `nsecpcap` and `pcapng`):
- It reads major version 2 and 3, refuses 1 ("major version 1 unsupported"); ignores the
  snapshot length (a 60-byte frame under a snapshot length of 0 or 20 read whole); reads a
  fraction past a second ("Fractional second 1500000000 is invalid") and a length on the wire
  under the bytes kept ("Frame length is less than captured length") with a complaint each; reads
  the frames before a record cut in its header or its bytes and says the file "appears to have
  been cut short in the middle of a packet"; refuses another link type's frames as Ethernet;
  takes a link type with the check sequence's bits set as Ethernet, the frames as recorded.
- **Its writer counts seconds as signed**: a stamp past 2038 (2 147 483 648 s) fails its rewrite
  ("An error occurred while writing to the file ... Success.") though its reader reads stamps to
  2106 (4 294 967 295.999999999 read). The conversion test keeps its stamps inside 2038; the read
  test holds the last second a capture counts.

Before the first commit, 35 mutants of `pcap.rs`, `hub.rs` and `mod.rs` (each bound, each
byte-order and resolution choice, each header field's place, each refusal, the replay's end,
the hub's own-frame rule, queue bound, stamp, wake-up and leaving, the raw link's pass over its
host's frames and the seam's length check before the socket) were run against the tests: all
35 killed, one only after a test was added (a last record keeping no bytes, read by tshark too).

The clause shares the word "capture" with portal's capture rules (C-038, named regex groups read
from text lines): a different capability, cross-referenced in the clause.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-23): refuted, the reader wrong in two of tshark's rules

The critic compared the reader with tshark on hand-made files and 350 generated ones. It found
the cuts, the hub, the replay and the raw link sound (no panic, locks in one order, no wake-up
lost), and found, now answered:
- **Versions before 2.4 lay a record out otherwise** (the defect): before 2.3 the length on the
  wire comes first, at 2.3 the smaller of the two is the bytes kept, and the nanosecond magic
  counts microseconds before 2.4. The reader read every version as 2.4 and so named damage in a
  2.2 file tshark reads whole. Measured again here (7 versions, both magics, both orders of the
  lengths) and followed; a test now builds sixty files (seven versions, three magics, both byte
  orders at 2.2 to 2.4, both length orders) and holds the reader to tshark's reading of each,
  frame for frame and damage for damage.
- **The link type's high bits** (the defect): tshark refuses only bits 16 to 25 and reads bits 26
  to 31 freely, so the reader refused 0x24000001, which is how libpcap writes Ethernet with a
  check sequence, and the test asserted the refusal of 0x08000001, which tshark reads. The test's
  check-sequence value never set the flag. The reader now follows tshark; each link-type case is
  shown to tshark (ten read, four reserved, six others).
- **Formats tshark reads that the reader refused**, measured here and followed: AIX's (2.2, the
  microsecond magic, link type 6: Ethernet in nanoseconds) and the modified magic a1b2cd34 (record
  headers 24 bytes). tshark guesses no other variant under the standard magic (a file of 24-byte
  records under it is damage to tshark too).
- **Two places where tshark's arithmetic wraps**, now named departures in the clause and pinned:
  tshark keeps a fraction past a second as an invalid fraction (it prints "1.1500000000") and
  wraps it past 2^31 nanoseconds, where the reader carries it; tshark shows every length on the
  wire past 2^31 − 1 as 2^31 − 1, where the reader keeps it. (The critic put the first at
  4 294 968 microseconds; tshark's product is signed, so it wraps from 2 147 484.)
- **Waits with no upper bound** in the tests: a hub port waiting twenty times its timeout and a
  raw link restarting its timeout for each frame of this host's passed over both passed. The hub's
  50 ms wait is now held under 500 ms, and a raw-link wait of 300 ms under a stream of this host's
  frames every 20 ms to between 300 ms and 1.5 s. The deadline check after a passed-over frame was
  redundant with the remaining wait computed from the one deadline, and is gone, so the stream
  test kills a restarted timeout.
- **Smaller**: the MTU's refusal is named (`RawError::TooLong(9000)` and its message), the
  replay's "at once" is held to a second for five waits of a minute, and `stamp`'s two edges are
  pinned (before the epoch 0, past 64 bits the largest).
Before this commit, 27 mutants of the three files (the critic's survivors and each new rule) were
run: 25 killed, one equivalent (swapping two equal lengths at 2.3), and one killed after a test
was added (a modified capture cut inside its 24-byte record header).
