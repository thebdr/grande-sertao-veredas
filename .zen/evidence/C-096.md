# C-096: A log says when its start's history is in the store

## Why (2026-11-25)

A comissionador replay starts a session from the project file and replays a window of its store,
so it must wait until the start has read the logs' history into the store. It guessed from the
lines holding still for a second, and a family with four or more empty members in a row made the
backfill pause longer than that, the replay starting before the newest member was read
(docs/questions.md Q319, the critic: 5 empty members aborted 2 runs of 3). The architect ruled
that portal says so.

## Choices

- **Counted in lines, judged in the store.** A worker reads its history synchronously but sends
  each line down a channel that the session's pump stores later, so a flag set by the worker
  alone would say "read" while lines still wait in the channel. The worker publishes the number
  of lines it had sent when its read-in reached the end of every file as it stood at the start;
  the session says the history read once its count of that log's lines (counted as the pump
  drains them, stored by the flush that comes before the refresh) reaches that number.
- **Where the read-in ends.** A family's backfill hands the live loop its seam (the active member
  at the offset where the replay stopped); the first read of that cursor reaches the end as it
  stood, and the count is taken there. A start at the end skips the history: the count is taken
  at the active's first read, nothing of the history in it. A file read from its start: after its
  first read, which reads to the length at that moment. Not there, born after the start, or with
  no active member yet: nothing to read, the count zero at once.
- **Core's `SourceHandle` untouched.** The counter is an `Arc<AtomicU64>` the session hands each
  tail source (`with_history_read`), `u64::MAX` until set, set once by a compare-and-swap.
- **Named `history_read`**, not `read_in`: comissionador's `main.rs` has a function `read_in`, and
  a member's policy test may read portal's public names against its tokens.

## Tests and mutants

The test waits on the flag and, at every look, asserts it is not said while fewer rows than the
history are stored. Three mutants: the stored count not asked (only that the worker set its
count), the family read in at once before its backfill, the file read in at its first look.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-25, at c50638f5): refuted, three defects and three promises untested

1. **A store error said read**: the count compared was of lines drained, not stored; a flush
   whose append failed dropped its rows already counted, and the flag said the history read with
   36 053 of 40 000 rows stored. A log's lines are counted stored only when the flush holding
   them landed; a failed flush before the history is read loses it for good, the flag never
   true. Tested in the crate (`session::history_test`), a flush failing being the store's own
   to provoke.
2. **A fixed-name family read from a start in the past, its active not there, never said read**:
   the backfill loops until the fixed path opens, and no mark was reachable there. Once every
   retiree is swept and the fixed path does not open, the history is read.
3. **A held file started at its end never said read**: a start at the end skips the history, so
   it says read at the first look whatever the open does; the family at its end alike.
4. **"Set once" untested**: lines arriving after the flag now never take it back at any look.
5. **A family not there or born after the start** untested: each says read at once, no row.
6. **Sources with no history and the snapshot's field** untested: true for a log, an OPC source
   and a channel; false for a file nobody may read from a start in the past.

Mutants, each red: a failed flush losing nothing, the fixed family's sweep not marked, a held
start at the end not marked, the family at its end not marked (red once a family whose active
nobody may read joined the test: a family with no active is marked on another path), the mark
set more than once (red in a unit test of the mark: lines written in one go left no moment for
the flag to fall back on), a family with nothing to read not marked, a source with no history
unread, the snapshot always read.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-25, at 948090dd): refuted, two defects and two promises untested

1. **A member there and not readable said read with its lines missing**: the backfill skips a
   member that does not open within its patience, and the history was said read without its
   lines (500 of 600 rows stored). A member found there and not readable now marks the history
   lost (`HISTORY_LOST`, set once like the count), and that log never says it read; one gone
   (not found: deleted, or renamed by a rotation and read under its new name) leaves nothing
   unread.
2. **A fixed active there and not readable said read**: the fixed-name sweep took any failure to
   open the fixed path for its absence. Only "not found" says the history read; a fixed active
   there and not readable holds history and is waited for.
3. **A family folder not there read from a start in the past**: its worker fails (C-037, the
   locate needs a listing) and the history is never said read; the clause says so now.
4. **A file not there read from a start in the past** untested: said read at once.

The unreadable members are made so by `chmod 000` once the start has returned (the start waits
for the locate, which peeks every member) and while the first member's 20 000 lines replay, so
the replay reaches them shut; a chmod landing late fails the test (every row stored), never
passes it.

Mutants, each red at the assertion meant for it: a member there and not readable not marking the
history lost (said read with 20 400 of 20 500 rows), the fixed sweep saying read on any failure
to open its active (said read with 20 300 of 20 400), a member gone taken for one held (never
said read with every row stored), a file not there from a start in the past not marked (never
said read). A first run of the first mutant failed before the session's first refresh, the
health not yet there; the looks while the history is read assert "never said read" since, the
exact "not read" asserted after the waits.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-25, at 4bb2e2ad): refuted, three defects and one limit unstated

1. **A fixed active renamed between the sweep's listing and its open said read too early**: the
   open found nothing and the history was marked at the count sent, before the retiree the
   rename made was read (20 444 of 60 000 rows stored when said). A not-found open now lists the
   family again and says read only when no unread retiree is there; a rename is atomic, so the
   listing taken after the open that found nothing shows the new name. Tested in the crate
   (`tail::family::history_test`), a hook before the open (compiled for the crate's tests alone)
   holding the window.
2. **A last line with no ending left out of a history said read**: C-035 holds a partial line
   until its newline, and the clause now says the history is its ended lines, the unended one
   stored once it ends, never waited for. Sending it would split a line its writer is still
   writing; waiting for it would hold the flag for good on a file that ends unended.
3. **A newest-by-order family whose newest member is deleted never said read**: the backfill
   converged on a newer member that never came, with no mark on that path. Gone (names are
   stable in this mode) or read to its end, the planned active now says the history read, as the
   fixed sweep does.
4. **A family started at its end whose active is held at the first look skipped the lines
   written while it was held**: each retry opened the active at its end then. Its name, length
   and identity at the first look are kept, as the single file keeps them, and the open that
   succeeds reads from there (a replacement whole); a held active that retires unopened is read
   from where it stood in the member of its identity, awaited while it does not open, then every
   generation born after it and retired since whole, in order, and the newer active whole.

Mutants, each red at the assertion meant for it: the fixed sweep's not-found open saying read at
once (said at 20 000 of 60 000 lines sent), the newest member gone unmarked (never said read with
every row stored), the held active's look not kept (0 of 50 rows), the retired held active's
remnant unread (20 rows), the generation between unread (30), the retired held active not
awaited (20 rows while it was held), the new active held in turn opened at its end (50 of 55).

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-25, at 87550be6): refuted, three defects

1. **A file held at its first look from a start in the past, rotated away before it opens, said
   read with none of its history stored**: the retry opened whatever was at the path. The open
   that succeeds now has to be the file the look recorded (its identity): another one means the
   held file was renamed away, which a file's tail does not follow, and the history is lost,
   never said read; an identity the look could not read cannot tell, and counts as another.
2. **The same file deleted while held never said read**: a not-found retry made no mark. Gone
   while it held the start's history, it is deleted (said read, nothing of it left), unless its
   identity is still in its folder under another name (renamed away: lost). One moved out of its
   folder reads as deleted: the limit, as a family member gone reads as gone.
3. **A stop during the backfill's read of its planned active said the history read**: the read's
   outcome took a stop for "gone or read to its end". A stop has an outcome of its own now
   (`Unread::Stopped`, also when it cuts a drain short), which never says read. Tested in the
   crate: the hook before an open, now run before a replay's open of a member too (`BEFORE_OPEN`),
   holds the replay at its active until the stop is asked.

Mutants, each red at the assertion meant for it: another file at the held file's path not
marking the history lost (said read with the new file's 10 rows), a not-found retry making no
mark (the renamed case said read once the new file came), a file renamed within its folder taken
for one deleted (said read while it was away), a stop taken for a read to its end (said read at
the 20 000 lines sent).

## Refute round 5 (2026-11-25, at ca29cc0e): refuted, the flag holding, new content lost after a held active

The critic traced every way the flag is set and found none said before the history is stored.
The gaps were in what round 3 had promised of a family's held active started at its end, and in
one "not there":

1. **A fixed name's held active, retired unopened, lost its lines since the look**: a fixed
   active keeps its name, so only the newest-by-order mode ever reached the retired branch.
2. **The newer active, held in turn, retiring before it opened, was skipped**: after the retired
   branch the next round opened whatever the active was then.
3. **A generation between held past the 500 ms patience was skipped**: read like a vanished
   retiree.
4. **A file under a folder that is a regular file never said read from a start in the past**:
   the open fails "not a directory", which was taken for a file there and held.

The held active is known by its identity now, whatever its name: still the active, read from
where it stood; a member that is not the active any more, retired unopened, read from where it
stood there (also when no active follows it yet, before the family is followed as born), then
every younger generation whole, each awaited while it is there and not readable, and the active
after them held in turn, from its start, if it does not open. A family whose active is held at
the first look lets its start return then, as a file does, instead of at the five-second bound.
"Not a directory" and an invalid file name read as not there.

Mutants, each red at the assertion meant for it: a held active retired with no active after it
followed as born at once (the fixed case read the new active, 5 rows, while the retiree was
held), the active after a retired one not held in turn (50 of 60), a held younger generation not
awaited (read past while held), "not a directory" taken for a file there (never said read), the
held active never found by its identity (0 rows), the start waiting its five-second bound on a
held active (5.0 s).

## Refute round 6 (2026-11-25, at 4aff54f8): refuted in the held active's new content, the flag holding

The critic traced every write of the mark again, the worker's and the session's, and found none
said before the history is stored, for the second round in a row. Both gaps were in what C-096
had promised since round 3 of a family's held active at a start at its end: a fixed name
rotating again while a held generation was awaited, and a rotation between a round's listing and
the held open. That is new content, not history (a start at the end skips the history), so the
promise moved to a clause of its own, C-098, which takes those two findings as its first and
answers them; C-096 now points there for what a held active reads once it opens.
