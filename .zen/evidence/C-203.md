# C-203 evidence - replay

## The design (2026-11-22)

A replay is `run::run` with `RunOptions::replay(from, to)`: the same engine as a live run (C-202),
its clock started at the window's start and moved only by rows' stamps, a bound or a wait for a
duration, so it waits for nothing; the window's end is its horizon, and a step it ends inside is
cut short, the run aborted with the window's end named. A write and a send are noted "not sent:
replay" (Q315 taken) and the switch is not consulted.

A live run's own window replays to the same run: the same verdict at the same step for the same
reason, the same reported values and criteria, the steps alike once the waits' lengths are set
aside, because a live write moves the clock to the plant's answer while a replayed one does not,
so a wait after a write reads a few milliseconds longer replayed. The function's last outcome, the
reaction a write caused: met at the same stamps. Two replays of one window are equal in every
field.

Later, named in the plan: a replay over a window a session no longer holds (a snapshot opened read
only, which needs portal), and from a run's kept evidence (phase 4's record).

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-23): refuted, two findings, both closed

1. **A live run's own window did not replay to its verdict when a tight wait followed a write**:
   after a step that read the plant, the live clock sits at least the read's settle (250 ms)
   behind the machine, and a write moves it to the plant's answer; a replay's write moves nothing,
   so the wait after it starts at least 250 ms earlier with the same bound. A live run passing a
   300 ms wait 154 ms after its write aborted replayed. Exact replay needs each write's moment in
   the store, portal's to keep: question Q318, recommending (a), with (c) taken meanwhile, the
   claim narrowed to tests whose waits after a write leave that room, the limit stated in the
   reference and pinned by a test (live moving the clock by at least the settle, the replay
   moving nothing and timing out).
2. **The clock moved past the window**: a wait for 5 s under a run's bound of 2 s over a window of
   1 s ended at the bound, past the window, with the bound named. A wait for a duration now meets
   the window's end first whenever the window ends before both the duration and the bound, in a
   step and in a function.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-24): refuted, one finding, the claim narrowed

1. **The narrowing named only a wait's timeout, but every plant read right after a write sees
   the plant before it in a replay.** An await right after a write with a bound of 5 s took
   announcement 26 replayed where the live run took 53: the replay's clock, not moved by the
   write, stood before the write went out. The claim now excludes any step reading plant data (a
   read, a check, a report, a wait, an await) right after a write, before the live run's lag has
   passed; the reference's "Time" says so, and a test pins it: replayed, an await after the write
   takes a row stamped before the plant's answer and a read after it finds `run` 0. Q318 (a),
   portal storing each write's answered moment, would close it.

C-202's round 2 finding 5 (the run's bound inside a wait) touched replay too: its own window now
replays to the same bound.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-25): refuted, two findings, closed

1. **A live run's bound did not replay** (C-202's finding 1): judged on the machine's clock live and
   the run's clock replayed. Both are the run's clock now; three runs the critic aborted live and
   passed replayed now do the same in both.
2. **A replay ignored the tester's stop but in script boxes**: a step routed back to itself ran on
   after the stop (seven minutes, killed), a retry of 1000 ran out. The stop is now looked at in
   every step, every replayed wait and read, every write; both cases end "stopped by the tester".
   The cost the critic measured for a long keyed window (a store query per column at a tied stamp,
   linear) stands, now stoppable.
