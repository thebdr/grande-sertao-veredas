# C-203 evidence - replay

## The design (2026-11-22)

A replay is `run::run` with `RunOptions::replay(from, to)`: the same engine as a live run (C-202),
its clock started at the window's start and moved only by rows' stamps, a bound or a wait for a
duration, so it waits for nothing; the window's end is its horizon, and a step it ends inside is
cut short, the run aborted with the window's end named. A write and a send are noted "not sent:
replay" (Q315 taken) and the switch is not consulted.

A live run's own window replays to the same run: the same verdict at the same step for the same
reason, the same reported values and criteria, the steps alike once the waits' lengths are set
aside, because a live write moves the clock to the plant's answer while a replayed one does not,
so a wait after a write reads a few milliseconds longer replayed. The function's last outcome, the
reaction a write caused: met at the same stamps. Two replays of one window are equal in every
field.

Later, named in the plan: a replay over a window a session no longer holds (a snapshot opened read
only, which needs portal), and from a run's kept evidence (phase 4's record).

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-23): refuted, two findings, both closed

1. **A live run's own window did not replay to its verdict when a tight wait followed a write**:
   after a step that read the plant, the live clock sits at least the read's settle (250 ms)
   behind the machine, and a write moves it to the plant's answer; a replay's write moves nothing,
   so the wait after it starts at least 250 ms earlier with the same bound. A live run passing a
   300 ms wait 154 ms after its write aborted replayed. Exact replay needs each write's moment in
   the store, portal's to keep: question Q318, recommending (a), with (c) taken meanwhile, the
   claim narrowed to tests whose waits after a write leave that room, the limit stated in the
   reference and pinned by a test (live moving the clock by at least the settle, the replay
   moving nothing and timing out).
2. **The clock moved past the window**: a wait for 5 s under a run's bound of 2 s over a window of
   1 s ended at the bound, past the window, with the bound named. A wait for a duration now meets
   the window's end first whenever the window ends before both the duration and the bound, in a
   step and in a function.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-24): refuted, one finding, the claim narrowed

1. **The narrowing named only a wait's timeout, but every plant read right after a write sees
   the plant before it in a replay.** An await right after a write with a bound of 5 s took
   announcement 26 replayed where the live run took 53: the replay's clock, not moved by the
   write, stood before the write went out. The claim now excludes any step reading plant data (a
   read, a check, a report, a wait, an await) right after a write, before the live run's lag has
   passed; the reference's "Time" says so, and a test pins it: replayed, an await after the write
   takes a row stamped before the plant's answer and a read after it finds `run` 0. Q318 (a),
   portal storing each write's answered moment, would close it.

C-202's round 2 finding 5 (the run's bound inside a wait) touched replay too: its own window now
replays to the same bound.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-25): refuted, two findings, closed

1. **A live run's bound did not replay** (C-202's finding 1): judged on the machine's clock live and
   the run's clock replayed. Both are the run's clock now; three runs the critic aborted live and
   passed replayed now do the same in both.
2. **A replay ignored the tester's stop but in script boxes**: a step routed back to itself ran on
   after the stop (seven minutes, killed), a retry of 1000 ran out. The stop is now looked at in
   every step, every replayed wait and read, every write; both cases end "stopped by the tester".
   The cost the critic measured for a long keyed window (a store query per column at a tied stamp,
   linear) stands, now stoppable.

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-26): refuted, two findings, one closed, one narrowed

1. **The tester's stop was not looked at while a replayed wait or await walked its window**: a
   replay takes its whole window in one look, then walks it (a store query at every tied stamp),
   so 1 500 stamps took 12 s and 6 000 a minute, the stop ignored. The stop is looked at on every
   row; the critic's window stops 200 ms in, a wait and an await alike.
2. **A run's bound passed by a write's answer replayed to another verdict**, with no plant data
   read after the write: live the answer moved the clock past the bound and the next step ended
   the run, replayed the write moved nothing and the run passed. The narrowed claim now names it
   (Q318 (a), ruled, closes it once portal stores each write's answer), the reference's "Time"
   says so, and a test pins it.

## Refute round 5 (2026-11-27): refuted, two findings, closed (one as a limit)

1. **The lag a replayed write leaves carries on past the step right after it**: the claim was
   narrowed to plant data read right after a write and a write whose answer passes the bound, but
   the replay's clock stays the write's lag behind for every later step until a wait or an await
   meets a row. A duration wait of 800 ms after a write ends a run of 1 s at its bound live and
   passes replayed; a check after a wait of 350 ms passes live and fails replayed. The claim now
   says so and `replay.rs` pins both; the fix is Q318 (a), portal's table of writes with the moment
   each was answered.
2. **The tester's stop was not heard while a replayed wait or await read its window**: the
   reading took every page of portal's before the first row was walked (200 000 rows, 3 s;
   600 000, 12 s). `Plant::rows_until` now looks at the stop before each page, and the runner reads
   every window through it. The test reads a window of 200 000 rows once, then stops a replayed
   wait and an await 200 ms in: each ends in under a quarter of that reading (the old reading
   stopped after 6.8 s of a reading of 8.6 s).

## C-205's refute round 6 (2026-11-27): a window before a table's rows

The binding refuses a replay whose window opens before the moment from which the plant says the
store holds a table the test reads (`Plant::rows_from`, by default nothing said; the executable
tells `LivePlant` through `with_rows_from`), the table named. `replay.rs` holds it: refused for
`plc_main` held from a second ago, nothing refused for a table held from before the window.

## Refute round 6 (2026-11-27): refuted, two findings, closed (one as a limit, with Q322)

1. **A write that failed live replays to a pass**: a replayed write is not sent and its step passes
   (Q315 (a)), so a live run aborted at a write the plant refused (or portal refused before sending)
   replays its own window to a pass. The claim is narrowed to live runs whose every write and send
   was taken, `replay.rs` pins the difference, and Q322 asks whether a replay should take the live
   answer from portal's table of writes once it is built (Q318 (a)).
2. **The tester's stop was not asked between the pages of a value read back**: closed with C-202's
   round 6 (`Plant::latest_until`), the stop looked at in replay as live.

## Refute round 7 (2026-11-27): refuted, one finding as a limit, with C-202's two closed

1. **A live run the tester stopped replays to another verdict**: a stop is no row of the window, so
   a live run stopped after its first step (aborted at the next) replays to the test's own pass;
   the same holds for a live run a store fault ended. The claim is narrowed to live runs that ended
   on their own verdict, and `replay.rs` pins the difference.

C-202's two findings of the round (a value the stop left unread judged and reported; the stop's
handing to the reading untested) hold in replay as live, closed with them.

## C-205's refute round 8 (2026-11-27): the refusal per value read

The binding judges a replay's window per value the test reads (`table.column`, against
`Plant::values_from` and the table's `Plant::rows_from`, the later of the two) and per table it
awaits (against `Plant::rows_from`), naming each as the test reads it; `replay.rs` now expects
`plc_main.speed`.
