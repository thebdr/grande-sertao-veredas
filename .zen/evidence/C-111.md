# C-111 evidence - the EtherNet/IP adapter

## Built (2026-11-22)

The second bench device of P-020, `src/ethernetip/` (`config.rs` the bench file's table and its
check, `wire.rs` the protocol's frames, `mod.rs` the adapter) and `tests/ethernetip_adapter.rs`.
Nothing is carried from Veredas 1, which had no EtherNet/IP device (its scanner named the protocol
as a probe to come).

How it is built, and why:
- **The bench's own code**, as the Modbus server is: each fault a scanner must meet (every
  Forward_Open refusal's extended status, a stray frame, a silent originator's timeout, production
  stalled) is the adapter's behaviour, which a library's adapter would decide for it.
- **The threading of the bench's other servers**: a thread, a current-thread runtime, the runtime's
  drop as the teardown; one lock over the assemblies and the connections, held for one request or
  one frame at a time. The Class 1 socket's loop wakes at the earliest due production or deadline,
  each production due an RPI after the last was due (a stall skips what it missed, as the OPC UA
  server's draws do).
- **Sizes as the protocol counts them**: a direction's connection size is the data, the 16-bit
  sequence count, and the 32-bit run/idle header when the direction carries one (OpENer checks the
  same). A refusal for a size carries the size wanted in its additional status.
- **Socket-address items as the protocol numbers them**: 0x8001 (T→O) in a request is where the
  originator wants its inputs; 0x8000 (O→T) in a reply is where the adapter consumes, sent when its
  I/O port is not 2222 (with port 0 in tests, the originator's only way to find it).
- **The timeout from the first frame**: a connection's first frame may come up to 10 s after the
  open; after it, a silence past the RPI times 4 shifted by the multiplier closes it.
- **No multicast T→O, no connected explicit messaging, no change-of-state trigger**: each refused
  with the protocol's status, the first thing to add when a consumer needs one.

The readers the bench did not write:
- `ethernetip-connections` 0.1.1: `ForwardOpenRequest::encode`, `ForwardOpenResponse::decode`,
  `ForwardCloseRequest::encode`, the Class 1 frame's `encode_frame` and `decode_frame`;
- `ethernetip-core` 0.1.1: the encapsulation header, the common packet format, the socket-address
  item's bytes;
- Wireshark's dissectors (tshark 4.6.6 in the home folder), over a capture of a whole conversation
  built from the bytes the test sent and received.

Found in the young crate on the way, which matters to core's EtherNet/IP scanner if it forks it:
its scanner states a connection's size without the 16-bit sequence count (refused 0x0127 here, and
by any adapter that checks), and its socket-address item codes are swapped (its originator's
endpoint goes out as 0x8000, the O→T item). The test uses its codecs with the protocol's sizes and
codes, and shows its own scanner refused.
