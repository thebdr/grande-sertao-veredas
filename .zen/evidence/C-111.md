# C-111 evidence - the EtherNet/IP adapter

## Built (2026-11-22)

The second bench device of P-020, `src/ethernetip/` (`config.rs` the bench file's table and its
check, `wire.rs` the protocol's frames, `mod.rs` the adapter) and `tests/ethernetip_adapter.rs`.
Nothing is carried from Veredas 1, which had no EtherNet/IP device (its scanner named the protocol
as a probe to come).

How it is built, and why:
- **The bench's own code**, as the Modbus server is: each fault a scanner must meet (every
  Forward_Open refusal's extended status, a stray frame, a silent originator's timeout, production
  stalled) is the adapter's behaviour, which a library's adapter would decide for it.
- **The threading of the bench's other servers**: a thread, a current-thread runtime, the runtime's
  drop as the teardown; one lock over the assemblies and the connections, held for one request or
  one frame at a time. The Class 1 socket's loop wakes at the earliest due production or deadline,
  each production due an RPI after the last was due (a stall skips what it missed, as the OPC UA
  server's draws do).
- **Sizes as the protocol counts them**: a direction's connection size is the data, the 16-bit
  sequence count, and the 32-bit run/idle header when the direction carries one (OpENer checks the
  same). A refusal for a size carries the size wanted in its additional status.
- **Socket-address items as the protocol numbers them**: 0x8001 (T→O) in a request is where the
  originator wants its inputs; 0x8000 (O→T) in a reply is where the adapter consumes, sent when its
  I/O port is not 2222 (with port 0 in tests, the originator's only way to find it).
- **The timeout from the first frame**: a connection's first frame may come up to 10 s after the
  open; after it, a silence past the RPI times 4 shifted by the multiplier closes it.
- **No multicast T→O, no connected explicit messaging, no change-of-state trigger**: each refused
  with the protocol's status, the first thing to add when a consumer needs one.

The readers the bench did not write:
- `ethernetip-connections` 0.1.1: `ForwardOpenRequest::encode`, `ForwardOpenResponse::decode`,
  `ForwardCloseRequest::encode`, the Class 1 frame's `encode_frame` and `decode_frame`;
- `ethernetip-core` 0.1.1: the encapsulation header, the common packet format, the socket-address
  item's bytes;
- Wireshark's dissectors (tshark 4.6.6 in the home folder), over a capture of a whole conversation
  built from the bytes the test sent and received.

Found in the young crate on the way, which matters to core's EtherNet/IP scanner if it forks it:
its scanner states a connection's size without the 16-bit sequence count (refused 0x0127 here, and
by any adapter that checks), and its socket-address item codes are swapped (its originator's
endpoint goes out as 0x8000, the O→T item). The test uses its codecs with the protocol's sizes and
codes, and shows its own scanner refused.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-22): refuted, answered

The critic ran 23 mutants, each gated behind a variable of one build, and every one kept the test
green; each was caught by a probe of its own. What it found, and what changed:
- **An Unconnected_Send nested a thousand deep overflowed the adapter thread's stack** and aborted
  the whole bench (`cip` and `unconnected_send` recursing without bound): any TCP client could kill
  every part a bench hosts. Now an Unconnected_Send inside another is refused 0x08: the adapter
  routes one level and no more. Pinned at two levels and at a thousand, the adapter answering after.
- **The Class 1 loop was not woken when a connection opened**: a new connection's first frame waited
  for the loop's next due time (4.75 s behind a connection at a 5 s RPI). The open now wakes the
  loop. Pinned.
- **A late frame overwrote the outputs**: the 32-bit sequence number was ignored. A frame no newer
  than the last taken (read across the wrap, as OpENer does) is now dropped and counted stale.
  Pinned, the wrap included.
- **A T→O item naming another host was obeyed.** Now the frames go to the session's own host at the
  item's port (OpENer takes the port alone too); the clause says so. Pinned.
- **ListIdentity on UDP**: an adapter bound to 127.0.0.1 never hears a broadcast (Linux delivers one
  only to a socket on the any-address), and one bound to 0.0.0.0 stated 0.0.0.0 in its item. Now the
  item names the address the reply leaves from; the clause says a broadcast reaches an adapter on
  the any-address. Pinned with a broadcast to 127.255.255.255.
- **Unpinned claims, now pinned**: a Large_Forward_Open taken and refused; unequal RPIs (the reply's
  two, production at the T→O one, the T→O one range-checked); the timeout's multiplier and its upper
  bound, and the first frame's 10 s; the non-default headers carrying frames both ways; the
  standard port 2222 with no item, and no O→T item on it; 0x0315 for a segment and for one connection
  point; a frame from another host; a Forward_Open inside an Unconnected_Send keeping its items;
  five configuration refusals; a taken Class 1 port; the plant side by bytes; the Identity class's
  revision; UDP ListIdentity while silent.

Checked and holding by the critic: who chooses each connection id, the size arithmetic, the
electronic key, the session and lock handling, the refusal layouts as tshark reads them.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-23): refuted, answered

Round 1's answers held, each pinned. Seven mutants kept the test green, each caught by a probe;
and one behaviour was wrong:
- **An IPv6 bind was accepted** and its Class 1 I/O failed in silence: no O→T item, frames sent
  to 0.0.0.0, the status counting them produced. A bind that is an IPv6 address is now refused by
  name: EtherNet/IP's socket-address items carry IPv4 alone.
- **A port, key or data segment in a request's own path** was read as if it were not there (a
  Get_Attribute_Single through port 1 answered as the adapter's own): now refused 0x04 at its word,
  as OpENer does. A Forward_Open's connection path, read apart, keeps its keys, data and ports.
- **Pinned now** (the critic's probes, the test's `round_2` module): the multiplier as 4 << m at 2
  (a linear 4 × (m + 1) passed before), a Large_Forward_Open past 511 bytes, the O→T RPI alone out
  of range, an equal sequence stale, the compatibility bit's major, the first frame at a 1 s RPI,
  stale frames not feeding the watchdog, nested misspellings, refusals read by tshark.
- **The timeout test measured from 10 ms after the last frame**, so a slow sleep under the whole
  suite's load fell short of its floor (once, in a reachable run); it measures from the last frame
  sent now, and every timed test of the bench runs with the machine to itself.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-23): refuted, answered

Round 2's answers held, each pinned, but one could be gone around. The critic's probes and mutants
are in `~/.cache/veredas/critic-c111-r3`; each finding is a pin in the test's `round_3` module.
- **The IPv6 refusal was bypassed**: `check` parsed the bind untrimmed and only refused what parsed
  as IPv6, while `start` bound the trimmed text; ` ::1` and a host name (`ip6-localhost`) bound on
  `[::1]` and produced frames to 0.0.0.0, round 2's failure again. The bind, trimmed, must now parse
  as a dotted quad: an IPv6 address and any host name are refused by name, as the reference says.
- **A restart did not close a session whose answers waited unread**: the session's write was not
  raced against the restart, so `restart()` waited out its 5 s and the old session went on
  answering. The write now ends with the restart.
- **The routing refusal carried no remaining path size**, which a routing error's Unconnected_Send
  reply has; tshark reads it when it is there and says nothing when it is not. The refusal now ends
  with the route's size in words and a reserved byte (one byte alone tshark reads malformed), pinned
  as tshark reads it.
- **Unpinned claims**, each with a surviving mutant, now pinned: the triple's vendor and originator
  serial (a 0x0100 or a close by connection serial alone passed), a key and an extended port
  segment in a request's path, the multiplier above 2, a port segment in a connection path, a taken
  encapsulation port over UDP. The multiplier at 3 and the restart join the timed list.

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-23): refuted, answered

Round 3's answers held, each pinned. The critic's probes and mutants are in
`~/.cache/veredas/critic-c111-r4`; each finding is a pin in the test's `round_4` module.
- **A restart left a Class 1 connection open**: `restart()` clears the connections and moves the
  generation on under the lock, but a session of the old generation goes on serving the request it
  is in and what is buffered till it sees the restart; a Forward_Open served in that gap opened a
  connection after the clear (15 to 21 rounds of 40), which then produced to a gone originator and
  owned the output, so a scanner reconnecting was refused 0x0106. The session's generation now
  travels down to the Forward_Open, which refuses (0x0113) when the restart came first.
- **Unpinned claims, each with a surviving mutant**: the routing refusal's size past one word, a
  reply's items in their order, ListIdentity's status while owned, the Assembly's other services.
- **Wording**: slot 0 is the port segment 01 00; its long form (an extended port segment) is another
  route, refused 0x0312.

## Port 0 met a UDP port taken (2026-11-23)

C-110's seventh round's crate run went red on `round_3::a_bind_that_is_not_a_dotted_quad_is_refused_however_written`:
an adapter on port 0 bound its encapsulation UDP socket to the number the machine chose for its TCP
listener, which another socket held for UDP (`AddrInUse`). The reference promises "the same port
for both"; the adapter now chooses again (up to 64 times) till one number is free for both. Pinned
by 60 starts beside 2000 UDP sockets holding ports on the adapter's address: before, about one start
in fourteen failed, and the test failed every run.

## Refute round 5 (2026-11-24): refuted, answered

Round 4's answers held, and port 0's second choice was pinned. The critic's probes and mutants are
in `~/.cache/veredas/critic-c111-r5/probes`; each finding is a pin in the test's `round_5` module.
- **A restart left a just-connected client open**: a connection still in the kernel's listen queue
  when `restart()` moved the generation on was accepted after it, under the new one, and served (198
  to 200 of 200 rounds). The restart now goes through the accept loop, which takes every connection
  waiting in the listen queue straight from the socket and closes it before moving the generation
  on, as the Modbus server's does.
- **A T->O item naming port 0 sent to nothing**: every send to port 0 fails, while the status
  counted the frames produced. Port 0 now names the standard 2222, as a scanner reads a 0 in the
  reply's item; a frame whose send fails is taken back from the count (pinned in round 6, in a
  private network that drops one port).
- **Unpinned claims, each with a surviving mutant**: 16-bit logical segments (assemblies past 255),
  two connections' O->T ids, an exact electronic key, an embedded request's pad, round 4's
  generation through an Unconnected_Send, a dropped handle freeing its ports, transports
  application-triggered and Class 0.

## Refute round 6 (2026-11-25): refuted, answered

Round 5's answers held (the listen-queue drain under a connect storm and four restarts at once, port
0 as 2222). The critic's probes and mutants are in `~/.cache/veredas/critic-c111-r6/probes`; each
finding is a pin in the test's `round_6` module.
- **A failed send was taken back from the wrong connection**: the take-back found its connection
  by the T->O id, which the originator chooses and two may share (round 5's own pin opens such a
  pair): one connection received 51 frames and counted none, the other sent none and counted 51.
  It is now keyed by the adapter's own O->T id, one to an open connection.
- **Unpinned claims, each with a surviving mutant**: the produced count (never counted, counted
  twice) and its take-back (removed) were read by no test. Round 5's evidence said no send fails on
  the loopback; one does, in a private network whose output to a port is dropped (EPERM): the
  take-back is pinned there.
