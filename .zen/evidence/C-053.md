# C-053 evidence - tables and series over portal's nested REST

## The design (2026-11-09)

Veredas 1's desktop had a table browser and a series view over the session; Q14 left them to
supervisão. They are views over portal's own REST, nested unchanged at `/s/<name>/portal/`: the
page reads `/api/tables`, `/api/table/data`, `/api/series` and `/api/series/data` and nothing else
of portal's, so what it shows is what portal serves (its paging, newest first; its LTTB windows).

## Verified in the browser (2026-11-09)

Against the demo plant of C-051: the four tables listed with their kinds and counts (the capture
rule's, the OPC rule's, the view, the samples table), a table opened by pointer and paged older and
newer (rows 1–50 of 392, then 51–100 and 101–150 as the log grew), typed columns in the header; the
25 series listed with types and counts, one charted over the last hour (600 of 605 points,
downsampled by portal).

Screenshots: `.zen/evidence/supervisao/tables.jpg` and `series.jpg`.

## The page run by the test (2026-11-10, before the first refute round)

C-051's first round showed that reading the bundle for strings proves nothing about what a page
draws. Before C-053's round, its test was given the same answer: the page run in a headless
Chromium (`tests/common/browser.rs`) against the session, the Tables and Series views read back
from the document against portal's own answers (the catalog, both pages of a table of 53 sorts cell
for cell, the series listing, a window's 53 points drawn in order with their values' ranks kept),
and the paths of every request the page made read from its resource timings. Besides the session's
own paths the browser asks for `/favicon.ico` on its own; nothing else.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-10, at ffe4c85): refuted, eight gaps in what the page run could see

The critic rebuilt the page with eleven mutations that kept the test green: the chart drawing
`v * 2 + 100` (the trend fits its axis to any values, so only the order was checked), the window
cut to 15 minutes (all the points sat in the last ten), the points spaced by index, "Newer" landing
one row off, "Older" left enabled on a full last page, every number to one place, every column
typed " double", the list buttons' names and counts swapped, a fetch to another host and an open
`EventSource` on portal's `/events` (resource timings show neither a request's host nor an open
stream), and both views built on the first session instead of the page's own (shown on a
two-session host: `/s/hall/` listing loop's catalog).

The answers, all in the test: the page now runs on a two-session host and every request it makes
is read from the DevTools protocol's network events (`Browser::watched`), with its origin and its
type, the object stream among them; every point of portal's window is placed where the chart's axes
put its time and value (the frame and the 8 % padding taken from `charts.ts`), and the axis labels
read; the window asked of portal is read from the request (an hour to the session's now, 600
points); the pager goes older, newer and to the last page of exactly a hundred rows; the header's
types, the lists' visible names and counts, the chart's heading and a value to three places are
compared; the bundle may name no `/portal/` path but the five; and `/s/hall/` reads hall's catalog
from `/s/hall/portal/api/` with nothing of `/s/loop/`. The eleven mutations were replayed against
the new test (below).

Replayed with the critic's own script (`apply.mjs`, each by exact replacement, the page rebuilt):
`affine`, `window15`, `xbyindex`, `newer`, `boundary`, `precision`, `headtype`, `labels`,
`foreign`, `events` and `first` each turn the tables test red.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-10, at 32a9590): refuted, one defect and six gaps

The defect: a series window of one point drew an empty chart under a header saying one point (the
trend drew its line from two points up). It is now a dot where the axes put it. The gaps, each a
page mutation the test let through: a table picked after another was paged kept the old offset
(the test picked once, at offset 0); the Series view could read another session (only hall's
Tables view was visited); a WebSocket off the host went unseen (the protocol reports it apart from
other requests); the value axis's labels could sit upside down and the time axis's read a quarter
hour off (their texts were read, not their places or times); and an unknown count could show as 0
(one button's count was checked).

The answers. The browser helper records WebSockets with the other requests, and both sessions'
requests are held to the host and to no WebSocket. The test pages sorts again, picks plc_main
(first page) and sorts again (first page, the body whole); checks every button's count between
portal's before and after the listing and the view's "?"; reads the value axis's labels beside
their heights and the time axis's as the store's clock at a quarter of the window each, "now" last;
and at `/s/hall/` opens the Series view (hall's series alone, as hall's portal lists them), logs
one sort there and reads its one point as a dot at its time and the middle of its padded extent,
asked of `/s/hall/portal/api/`.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-11, at 3389787): refuted, two gaps in the test, the page right

The one-point window's dot was checked for its place, its visibility and its fill, never for an area
painted: a circle with no radius (round 2's defect again, drawing nothing) stayed green, and so would
`display: none` or an opacity of 0. And past the first page the row range the reader sees was never
read: pages were matched by the hidden `data-rows` alone, so the visible "rows A–B of N" built from
a wrong expression ("rows 51–100 of 53" on the older page) stayed green.

The answers, in the test: the dot must paint an area (its bounding box not empty, visible, not
hidden, not transparent, filled), and every page is matched by its rows and by what it says of them.
Both mutants turn the test red (the dot "shown: false"; the older page never saying its rows).
