# C-148 evidence - the implicit read

## First version (2026-11-26)

Phase 6's fourth piece, and deep identity's first: the topology's reported and confirmed links
(C-145) need a device's own word on its ports' neighbours, which is its port data, read with no
connection. Absorbed from Veredas 1's `probes/pnrpc.rs` (an implicit read of I&M0 over an operating
system UDP socket bound to any address, its answer the first datagram the socket received, its
header's integers written in one byte order), changed on the way:
- **Through the listener, not past it** ("Rules": it listens before it asks; the listener is the
  only sender on its interface, so every active step is paced where it is heard). The request is a
  frame aranha writes (`ip`: Ethernet II, IPv4, UDP, both checksums) and hands the listener,
  paced and kept as sent like an Identify (C-144); its answer is the datagram the listener keeps
  from the device, matched by MAC, address, port and the request's activity, so both frames are
  on record and every report names the frame it came from. Veredas 1's socket took whatever
  datagram came first to its port.
- **This machine's own address, and an open port.** The request goes from the interface's own
  IPv4 address (the system's, read with `getifaddrs`; refused when the interface holds none, as a
  device could not answer it) and from a UDP port the process holds open on it: the system then
  answers the device's ARP for that address (a stack that does not take the address from the
  request asks for it) and takes the answer in, rather than refusing it with an ICMP port
  unreachable to the device. The bench device answers to the request's MAC directly, so the test
  does not show the ARP; a real device's may.
- **The device's own object.** The request's object is the device's (its vendor and device ids
  from DCP, its instance, 1 when it states none, as PROFINET's default) rather than the nil one; the
  bench reads any, a device may refuse another.
- **Every port, not the ones heard** ("Traps": the ports a scanner hears are the ones facing it).
  The port data is read at slot 0, subslot 0x8001 and on until the device says a subslot is not
  there (IODReadRes, PNIORW, error code 1 0xB2), at most 16, each port named as its record names
  it. A first version read the subslot `0x8000 + NNN` of each port the device's LLDP named
  `port-NNN`; the bench keeps its one port at 0x8001 whatever its name (the second device's
  `port-002` refused at 0x8002), and a device's port facing another device is never heard here,
  so that version would have missed exactly the links the read is for.
- **Refused, never guessed**: a fault or reject, a status other than success, another record's
  header, a record shorter than its header, frames let go before the answer was read, no answer
  in the wait: each a typed error naming the frame where there is one.

The bench proof needs two devices that hear each other's LLDP, which a Linux bridge does not
forward unless told (`group_fwd_mask 0x4000`, allowed in the user namespace: measured with
`unshare -Urn` before the test was written). On that segment each device keeps the other as its
port's neighbour, and the listener hearing both ports draws no cable from its interface (a segment
of several, C-145), so the confirmed link between the two is the read's alone. The dissectors read
the request and the answer (with IPv4's and UDP's checksums checked, which tshark leaves
unchecked by default): DCE/RPC's packet type and operation, PNIO's index, slot and subslot (twice
in the answer: the read's header and the port data's), the object, the nil AR, the record length
asked (1308, what one frame takes) and answered, and the port data's own port, peer chassis, peer
port and peer MAC, each equal to what the read decoded.

The bench answers only what a read asks, so a scripted peer (raw links on the far ends of two
pairs in a private network, the second interface heard promiscuously as on a mirror port) sends,
for each read, the datagrams a real segment can hold beside its answer: the same answer on the
other interface, kept before it; one from another MAC, another address, another port; one to
another port; one under another activity; a working packet; then the answer. Only the answer is
read. It also answers with a fault, a reject, another index's header, a record shorter and one
longer than its header says, port data of another subslot, a peer of an all-zero MAC and the
status that ends the walk, and floods a listener of eight frames past its bound before answering.

Before the first commit, 37 mutants of `src/read.rs` and `src/ip.rs` (kept as a resumable script
under `~/.cache/veredas/aranha/mutants/c148/`, the pure tests first and the bench's only when they
pass): the refusals before sending each dropped (an ended interface, no MAC, the device not heard
there, 0.0.0.0, the wait unbounded, no address of the interface's own), each part of the answer's
match dropped (its source, its destination, its activity, its interface), a fault taken as no
answer, the status, the header's record and the record's length unchecked, frames let go ignored,
the end of the walk taken for another status or for an error, the walk stopping after one port, a
port named by its subslot, a zero peer MAC kept, another subslot's port data taken, the object nil
or of instance 0; of the datagram, each check dropped (the header's checksum, a fragment, the
protocol, the version, UDP's checksum and its lengths, the total length short of UDP's header, a
tag), no pad, a payload one past taken, a broadcast address taken, no don't-fragment, another time
to live: all killed, eleven only once the scripted peer was written, the interface's check only
once the same answer came first on the other interface, heard promiscuously.

## Its neighbours in the contract (2026-11-26)

The stop hook paired the clause with C-116 (the bench device's record read); the implications check
found them the two ends of one exchange, nothing contradicting: the records at the same slots,
subslots and indexes, the walk ending only on the status C-116 gives a subslot it lacks, no ARP
sent (the device answers to the asker's MAC), the port's subslot independent of its name on both
sides. Against C-137 ("following the port an answer comes from is the caller's"), the clause now
says what this caller does: an answer from another port than 34964 is passed over, not followed
(the scripted peer's case). What the reader relies on that C-116's description does not state but
its test pins (the answer from port 34964, the port data's slot and subslot those asked) is
maquinar's to word, if it wants to; asked through the orchestrator, never edited here.

## The first refute round, answered (2026-11-26)

The critic (its own copy; 31 mutants the first run had not tried; a demonstration file of its own:
a wire stalling under the read's turn, a scripted device recording the request's object and this
machine's ICMP, a checksum of zero, and a fuzz of the datagram reader) found the code doing what
the clause says in every case, and refuted on the test: 30 of the 31 survived it, about 21 of them
breaking words of the clause no assertion held:
- **The turn** ("sent through the listener, taken back unsent past its turn"): no test reached the
  wait, the withdrawal, NotSent, Stalled, Refused or a worker ending while the read waits.
- **The port held open**: nothing checked that the answer reached an open port, so a request from
  another port than the socket's (this machine then sending the device an ICMP port unreachable,
  a frame outside the listener) passed.
- **The frames a refusal names**, **the walk's bound of 16**, **the object's instance**, **another
  subslot's or slot's header**, **the answer's destination address and MAC**, **the other byte
  order**, **a checksum of zero**, **a 24-byte header**, **a UDP length short of its packet**,
  **two peers**, **another slot's port data**, **a wait of exactly 10 s**.

Its demonstrations are taken into the linked test (`round_1`), with a case for each of the rest:
the scripted device of the main case now answers a header of the subslot or slot asked for, and
every subslot to the walk's bound; the refusals' frames are found among the device's datagrams
kept; a worker ending while the read waits, and a read behind thirty frames, join the turn's cases.
The critic's equivalents stand: a cut frame (a live link never cuts, and a read needs a MAC), the
request's own frame (it never matches the device as source), the socket's drain (it closes when
the read returns), the RPC interface's UUID (the clause's match does not name it), and the port's
short name on the names the bench writes.

The critic's 31 mutants replayed against the answer (its runner, pointed at the worktree, results
under `~/.cache/veredas/aranha/mutants/c148r1/`): 26 killed, every survivor it named among them;
the five that survive are the five it found equivalent.
