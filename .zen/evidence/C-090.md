# C-090 evidence - the store kept as a window

## The ruling and what is built (2026-11-22)

The architect's ruling of 2026-11-19 ("Memory window", `docs/robustness.md`, "The store of a
controller's embedded portal"): by default a controller's embedded portal keeps its recorded values
in memory as a fixed window, the last stretch, as long as the project sets, nothing on the drive.
Built: the window, on any session, chosen by the project (`store = { window_s = N }`) or by the
embedding (`SessionConfig::store_window`). Not built here: recording to the drive with a size limit
and the window written at a fault (their own clauses, P-019), and Q41's open points (a ceiling in
bytes beside the span; what gives way at a drive's limit).

## The spike: does an in-memory store hand memory back?

`docs/questions.md` Q41 left "how the window is built" to this plan: DuckDB's manual says a file
hands space back poorly after deletes, and nothing was checked for memory. Probed on the pinned
engine: a table appended in batches of 5 000 rows, every row past the newest 100 000 deleted after
each batch. Over two million rows appended the engine's figure (`duckdb_memory()`) stayed between
5 and 19 MiB and the process at about 70 MiB; the same table deleting nothing grew to 185 MiB and
200 MiB. A periodic CHECKPOINT kept it lower still (1 to 8 MiB) but is not needed. The figure moves
in steps, not row by row (with 50 000 kept, 3.5 to 28 MiB, no trend), so C-042 pins the shape: the
window's figure stays under a million rows kept, and ends under two million kept; the table that
deletes nothing grows. A plain DELETE of the oldest slice is the window; no table per slice of
time, which would have changed the store's surface (a base table as a view over slices).

## Decisions

- **The arrival clock.** The window holds what arrived within the span, never what is stamped
  within it: the stamp is the plant's clock, which may jump or replay (a backfill), and the
  arrival is the clock health is judged on and the order rows are appended in, so the oldest
  arrivals are a prefix of the table and a delete takes a slice. The test writes a batch stamped a
  day earlier after the first and sees the first go. The narrow `samples` table has no arrival;
  its bare samples are let go by their index (no producer in the session writes it). Reversible:
  one column in one statement.
- **Once a second, by the pump.** The pump is the store's one writer and holds its ingest
  connection, so the window takes no lock a reader holds (C-043). A row lives at most the span and
  the period (and a pass) past its arrival.
- **The listing counts what was seen.** The series cache's counts order the live frames (C-043's
  `n`); they stay counts of samples seen, the store holding the window. The counters add `evicted`,
  the snapshot `window_ms`.
- **Names.** The session's field is `store_window`, not `window`: supervisão's policy test refuses
  a public name of portal its own sources also hold, and supervisão names its time windows
  `window` (docs/portal-plan.md, "Traps").

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-22, at 6cdb62e): refuted, five gaps in the test

The critic held the code (a row at the cutoff kept, the views and the narrow surface reading the
window, no overflow in the span, the stop's last passes, the eviction timed on every pass however
busy) and found the test without teeth:
1. **The span's lower bound untested**: a cutoff at a third of the span, and at zero, passed every
   test. The log test now reads the rows' own arrivals and holds all ten 2.8 s after the first of
   them arrived, however loaded the machine (nothing that arrived within the span can go), and
   checks the batch goes no sooner than three seconds after the last of them.
2. **The upper bound loose**: a cutoff a second late passed. Against the bench the oldest poll held
   is watched for four seconds, every phase of the once-a-second pass: never older than the span,
   a pass and 0.7 s; the late mutant reaches about three seconds.
3. **"Through the ingest connection" untested**: an eviction through the query connection brought
   C-043's stall back for windowed sessions (0 passes behind a held export). A window session
   behind a held export now passes at least half its quiet pace, the lines stored.
4. **A window on a disk store accepted through `SessionConfig`** (the project file refused it,
   controlador does not read one): now refused by `Session::start` with its text, nothing written.
5. **The memory pin could not tell slow growth from a bound**: one comparison at two million rows.
   The engine's figures prove the same from run to run (identical over six million rows in two
   runs, a cycle between 3 and 27 MiB); the pin now holds four million rows under a 32 MiB ceiling
   with the later peak no higher than the earlier, beside a table deleting nothing that passes the
   ceiling within a million. It takes some twenty seconds.
The clause no longer claims what no test provokes ("a failed pass is a store error with its text":
the code does so, but a DELETE on the store's own tables cannot be made to fail from outside). Five
mutants each turn the test red: the cutoff at a third, at zero, a second late, the eviction through
the query connection, the disk refusal off. A pass skipped while the pump drains a flood (MAX_DRAIN)
lives: the code times the pass on every round, and no test floods a window for long enough to see.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-22, at b3380d9): refuted on the lower bound

The critic held the upper bound (a cutoff a second late killed) and found the lower one loose: a
window cut 700 or 900 ms short passed. The log test's batch arrived in the session's first passes,
so the pass after a short cutoff fell after the test's checks; and the bench test asserted only that
the oldest poll was at some look at least the span old, which a short window reaches just before
each pass. The bench test now asserts at every look over four seconds that the oldest poll held is
at least half a second old: with a poll every 50 ms a window keeping its span always holds one
that arrived about a span ago, and a loaded machine only makes them older. The 700 ms short mutant
fails it.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-22, at 5ff9097): refuted, two loose bounds

The critic held the upper bound and found two the tests left loose:
1. **The bottom of the window**: a cutoff 350 ms short passed, the bench test only asking that
   the oldest poll held was once at least 500 ms old. The bench test now keeps the arrivals held at
   each look and, for every poll let go since the last, asserts it was at least the span old at
   the look after its going: it went at a pass before that look, whose cutoff was the span before
   the pass, so the correct code always meets it and any short cutoff fails it at every pass.
2. **"Once a second"**: a pass every 1.5 s passed. The oldest poll held moves at a pass alone
   (each lets twenty polls go), so the test counts its moves over six seconds: at least five, never
   1.45 s apart.

Implications (C-091's and C-093's checks, 2026-11-22): the clause's "not built" no longer lists
the record and the copy at a fault, which are C-091's and C-093's (6c7dbbe).

The critic's two mutants, each red in the bench test now: the cutoff 350 ms short, the pass every
1.5 s.

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-23, at e487a86): holds

The critic found no path that passes while the clause fails: both bounds and the once-a-second
