# C-201 evidence - the test file and the function file, read and checked

## The design (2026-11-22)

`docs/test-schema.md` is the reference; Q310 (the syntax), Q311 (the script box's language) and
Q317 (how plant data is named) are taken as the assistant recommends, so the reading is one
front end over a model the engine runs (the orchestrator's word: a ruling for another syntax
rewrites `lex` and `parse` and the examples, not the engine).

- `lex` never fails: what it cannot read is a `Bad` token with its reason, reported where the
  parser meets it. Line ends are tokens; runs of them are one, and the line end before a line
  that opens with `->` is dropped, so a call's outputs may sit on the line after it (the
  design's own layout).
- `parse` cuts the header at the first line opening with the word `test` or `function`, hands it
  to `veredas_core::schema::check` (the suite's texts) and refuses any other key in it; then
  reads the body by recursive descent. Inside parentheses the parser skips line ends itself, so
  a setting that takes the rest of its line (`id`, `area`, `portal`, `use`) is cut from the text
  by its span and cannot leave the lexer counting an open parenthesis.
- `check` resolves every `<name>.<column>`: a variable's row column when the name is a variable
  of the scope (the test's: constants, a script's assignments and loops, `read`, `await` and
  call outputs; a function's: ports, assignments, loops, awaits, call outputs), plant data
  otherwise, rewritten into `ExprKind::Plant` in the model it returns, so the engine never
  guesses. Two functions of one name: the first wins and the second is the problem.

## What the build met

- **`max` was a keyword and a built-in.** `retry a max 1` read it as a word of the language, and
  `max(a, b)` then could not be called. It is no longer reserved: the route reads it by its
  place. The reference's list of words lost it; the test holds the list equal to the reader's.
- **A step named `report`.** The design's mock names a step after its action; the reference's
  worked example did the same and the reader refused it as a word of the language. The step is
  `summary` in the reference: names stay apart from the language's words.
- **A setting after `stop`.** A line `area late` after a step ending in `stop` was reported as
  "nothing follows `stop`" before the setting rule; a setting's key now answers first.
- **The function a duplicate name resolves to.** The table of functions kept the last of two of
  one name, while the problem pointed back at the first; it keeps the first.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-22): refuted, nine findings, all closed

1. **Valid durations refused.** `1.001s` was `1.001 * 1000.0` in floating point, 1000.9999999999999,
   and refused as no whole number of milliseconds (360 of the 20 000 literals `N.ddds`). A
   duration is now computed in exact decimal (mantissa and scale as integers).
2. **Another header key with a value past 64 bits accepted.** Core's reader reads integers to 128
   bits and passed; `toml::Table` then failed and the key check was skipped. The header is now
   scanned line by line: every line that is neither blank nor a comment sets `format` or
   `version`, whatever another key's value. `toml` left the crate's dependencies.
3. **A call in a `let` accepted** (`now()`, `max(1, 2)`). Refused.
4. **A misspelled built-in in a statement** read as a function call statement, refused at its
   `)`. A call statement is now told from an expression by its named inputs (or none); a script
   box's check knows the test's functions, so `y = 1 + F()` is named as a function inside an
   expression.
5. **`#` inside a string's braces** opened a comment in the inner lexer and the rest was dropped.
   The inner lexer reads no comment; `#` there is no part of the language.
6. **Another key named at line 1** for `[extra]` or `meta.x`: closed by the scan of 2.
7. **CRLF moved a line end's column by one**: the line-end token began at `\n`. A carriage return
   right before a line feed is now part of the line end, and a setting's rest of the line stops
   before it.
8. **The language's words accepted as a field's name** in `send` (`timeout: 1`). Refused; a
   field or an input named so is written in backquotes, as a table is.
9. **Deep nesting crashed the process** (3 000 parentheses in release, 600 in debug, 20 000 `not`,
   a 100 000-term sum). The reader now refuses, at their place, an expression nested deeper than
   64 levels or holding more than 1 000 operators, and blocks nested deeper than 64.

What held under the critic: multi-byte characters before an error, an empty `{}` at a line's end,
an unclosed string or brace at the file's end, a header alone, an empty file, an empty layout, a
byte-order mark, CRLF in settings and before `->`, a retry past 32 bits.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-22): refuted, eight findings, all closed

1. **The operator limit skipped in patterns and ranges; the process aborted.** `pattern()` and a
   `for`'s range reset the count and read at the additive level, and every `(` inside reopened an
   outermost expression; a pattern of 50 000 terms overflowed a 2 MB stack. Every expression's
   entry now opens one count (`open`, `sum`), and an inner `(` counts on.
2. **A zero with a huge negative exponent hung the reader** (`0e-2147483648ms`, 23 s in release):
   the scale loop divided zero by ten two billion times. The exact computation now has no loop:
   the digits trimmed of their zeros, the unit as 1, 6 or 36 times a power of ten, and a result
   whole only when at most `10^2` divides it. Trailing zeros no longer count as digits.
3. **A one-output call statement broken inside its parentheses** was refused: the look ahead for
   named inputs did not pass over the line ends inside them. It does.
4. **A byte-order mark refused by the new header scan** (round 1's fix broke what had held): the
   mark is no key.
5. **A comment ate the carriage return** of a CRLF line end, moving the column. A comment stops
   before it.
6. **Both limits started over inside a string's braces.** The inner reading now counts on from
   the expression the string sits in, and hands its count back.
7. **An input in backquotes could never be declared**, so the reference's promise was empty. A
   body could not name such an input either; the reference now says an input is never one of the
   language's words, and backquotes are for tables, channels and fields.
8. **A multi-line string in the stamp** (`format = """` across lines) was refused: a multi-line
   string's lines are its value's now.

## Confronted with the contract (2026-11-22): distinct from C-017, unrelated to C-071

The turn-end gate flagged C-201 as a near-duplicate of C-017 (core's channel file) and of C-071
(core's Modbus client), neither of which it named. The implications check found:
- **C-017: distinct.** The channel file is TOML throughout, every key read by `schema::load`; the
  test file's header alone is TOML, its body a language of its own. They share the stamp, which is
  C-045's. C-201 now names C-045 in its Source and says how it stands beside the suite's TOML
  files (C-017 onward, C-046, C-048), on C-048's pattern.
- **C-071: unrelated.** They share the word "function": a Modbus function code there, a function
  of the test language here. No cross-reference.
- **For others' clauses, carried by the orchestrator:** C-045 (core) speaks of a body as TOML and
  could say a file whose body is not TOML hands it the header's lines; C-046's title (portal)
  could say "every TOML file of the suite"; core's `telegram/schema.rs` and `catalog.rs` say "like
  every plain-text file of the suite"; and the suite plan's "every plain-text config and project
  file is TOML" stands against Q310's option taken meanwhile, which Q310 names. The header scan in
  `parse.rs` is a partial second header reader: core's reader returning the header's other keys
  with their places would let comissionador drop it.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-22): refuted, four findings, all closed

The round was cut by the machine's stop and resumed from the critic's transcript.
1. **A quote run in a header comment hid every key after it.** The scan counted `'''` and `"""`
   on every line, comments included, and took an odd count for an open multi-line string. The
   header is now read line by line as TOML reads it: a comment runs to the line's end, a
   single-line string to its closing quote (a basic string's escapes passed over), and only a
   delimiter outside both opens or closes a multi-line string.
2. **The cycle check was cubic** (2 000 chained functions held the reader 64 s): a breadth-first
   search from every function, copying the path at every node. Now one pass finds the strongly
   connected components (Tarjan's method, without recursion), and only a function on a cycle is
   searched for its way back, inside its component, with parent links; a long way back is named
   by its first five functions and counted. The same round showed the check rebuilding the
   file's list of function names for every statement (a 5 000-function chain: 1.2 s); it is built
   once. Places come from a line index built once per file (ten thousand problems: 1.5 s before).
3. **The two depth limits, each held alone, overflowed a 2 MB stack together** in a debug build
   (64 blocks with 64 levels of parentheses inside). Blocks and expression levels now count
   against one depth of 64.
4. **An unfinished expression in a string's braces was named "the file's end"**: the inner
   reader's end is now named as the `}` that closes it.

What held under the round: 19 208 random durations against exact fractions, operator counts never
carried into another expression over thousands of statements, error paths inside braces, the call
look-ahead, the byte-order mark and the line ends, and 140 000 random mutations of the worked
example without a panic.

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-23): refuted, five findings, all closed

1. **A panic on a character past ASCII in a header's multi-line string** (`note = """olá"""`):
   `toml_line` sliced the line at a byte inside a character. It compares bytes now.
2. **Quadratic time over a used file**: the loop over the used files' functions and the
   duplicate-name problems placed each problem by a scan from the file's start (20 000 functions:
   21.6 s). Both take their places from the line index.
3. **A quoted key holding `=` hid another key** (`"format=x" = 2` read as `format`). A quoted key
   is read to its closing quote, a bare one to a blank, `.` or `=`, a dotted key named whole.
4. **A function named twice broke the cycle search**: its index took the last definition where
   every other check takes the first, so a real cycle went unnamed and a false one was named. The
   index takes the first.
5. **"and 1 others"**: one other, several others. The way back's search uses index arrays and a
   stamp per search instead of a map (a 2 000-function cycle: 4.5 s before, in a debug build).

## Refute round 5 (2026-11-23): refuted, three findings, all closed

1. **An escape in a quoted header key hid another key** (`"\format" = 1`, `"versio\n" = 1`):
   the hand-made key reader kept the letter after a backslash, so a control character's escape
   made `format` or `version`; the reverse, `"form\u0061t"`, was refused as a strange name. In a
   differential of 228 180 random headers against `toml`, 15 170 disagreed, all of this class.
   This was the fourth round to find a hole in a hand-written TOML scan, so the scan is gone: the
   header is read by `toml::de::DeTable` (the `toml` crate's own reader of a document into keys
   with their byte spans, its values kept as text, so no value of any type or width is refused
   for its value), and the first top-level key that is neither `format` nor `version` is named at
   its line, a control character in it by its escape.
2. **A dotted or table key with `=` or `]` inside its quotes was misnamed** (`'a=b'.c` as `'a`):
   the same reader names a dotted key by its first part, the table it makes, and a table by its
   name.
3. **Problems sharing one line were placed in quadratic time** (160 000 layout positions on one
   line: 3.8 s, about 13 s at 2 MB): the line index keeps whether each line is ASCII, where a
   column is a byte's distance from the line's start, and counts on a line past ASCII from the
   last place it counted there.

## Refute round 6 (2026-11-23): refuted, two findings, both closed

What held: `Lines::place` against a count from the line's start on 180 000 random places (ASCII,
`é`, `𝄞`, CR, CRLF, no last line end, places inside characters and past the end), and the header

byte-order mark, dotted keys, tables and arrays of tables with blanks), 150 000 random headers

1. **Four checks were quadratic in the file.** A function's inputs, its `reads` and a test's used
   files were each searched by a scan of their list for every statement, plant read or `use`

   expression scanned it, and every `let` copied the constants above it (20 000 constants: 46 s,
   found while answering). Each is a set built once (per function, per file, per call). The
   tests hold the four cases of the finding and the constants to five seconds of processor time,
   and the used files and the blocks by how their time grows: a check linear in its file takes
   eight to twelve times as long for a file eight times larger; the checks before this round took
   36 and 44 times as long. The time is the thread's own on the processor, as Linux's scheduler

   once: the wall clock had the same check at 2.6 s and 5.8 s minutes apart.
2. **A value core's reader cannot hold hid another key** (`x = 1e400`, an integer past 128 bits):

   text stands, so the stamp is still decided first. A header that is no TOML is now placed where
   TOML found the fault, not at 1:1.


## A red run beside other tests (2026-11-23)

`a_duration_s_exponent_is_read_without_a_loop_over_it` bounded two parses by a second of wall
time and read 2.4 s once in the suite under load. A wall clock measures the machine, not the
loop. The test now compares against a baseline it measures itself: three literals of one length,
read the three ways a duration is (zero, not whole, past the range), with exponents of ten
(`0e0000000010ms`, `1e-0000000010ms`, `1e0000000030ms`) and of a billion (`0e1000000000ms`, ...),
each lexed 5 000 times, best of three rounds, on the thread's CPU time (Linux's scheduler, moving
by a 1 ms tick at most; each side runs about 40 ms). Both sides look at the clock after every
run, so the looking costs them alike; the billion's side must cost within ten times the ten's,
and a round stops as soon as it passes that. Measured: 0.95 to 1.00. A reader that counts the
exponent out (a loop up to its value, capped at two billion) fails the test, "an exponent of a
billion cost over 10 times one of ten in every round", in 38 s; the real reader passes it in
0.4 s.

## The architect's Q312 (e) (2026-11-25): the roles a test names sign its report

Asked who signs an acceptance report, the architect answered "Roles named by the test": anyone
signs by a typed name and a signed report is frozen; besides, a test may name the roles that must
sign (`sign tester, witness`), and the report reads "awaiting witness" until they have. Built so:
the test's `sign` setting (C-201) names `tester`, `witness` or both, each once; the report keeps
them as `test.sign` (C-204), each of them signs once, a role not named is refused, and
`Report::standing` reads `awaiting <roles>` until all have signed, then `signed`; a note is refused
from the first signature; a test naming no role is signed, and frozen, by its first signature, as
before.
