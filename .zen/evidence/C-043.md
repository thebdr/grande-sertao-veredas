# C-043 evidence - the session

## The design (2026-11-07)

Veredas 1's desktop session (its ingest pump, its live caches, its health) moved out of the shell
into the library, so a shell, the server and controlador embed the same thing: the `session`
module behind the `store` feature. What a reader needs every second (the series listing, the
vitals, the health, the counters) is kept by the ingest, never queried from the store: Veredas 1
found at a plant's scale that a listing by store scan ran for minutes holding the store's one
connection, and every endpoint convoyed behind it.

Three protocols feed one store through one pump thread: a captured line, a mapped poll and a read
message each become a wide row of the table their rule or message names, and every value a
sample the frames broadcast. A message table is named by the message alone (C-041), so the
session refuses a name taken twice across rules, OPC rules and channels, naming both owners,
before anything spawns.

Health folds the worker's liveness with the arrival age on the arrival clock. A quiet log and a
share away read alike (idle): a tail cannot tell them apart, and the clause says so rather than
claim it. A server or a peer away is retrying, which is alive and self-healing, never failed; only
a worker that ended in error is failed. The refresh runs every 500 ms, so a test's idle window must
be longer than that or the live state ages into idle before it is seen (the first health test did
that, with a 400 ms window).

The master switch is the one `WriteGate` every OPC handle and channel handle of the session
shares (C-040, C-039): there is no second gate here, so it cannot drift from what the handles
enforce; the session only exposes it, live.

## Reconcile (2026-11-08): the project and the session check alike

The implications check against C-041 and C-046 found the channel clause miscited (C-038 for
C-039), two validators drifting (the project compared table names by case and refused a view
named like another view; the session compared without case and let two views share a name), and
checks the session ran that the project skipped. The two now claim names through one type
(`project::Claims`, case-insensitive, views included), the channel is checked whole before anything
spawns (`channel::check`: framing, reply rules, tables), and the clause names what it refuses.

## Found by CI (2026-11-08): the counters are a snapshot on the vitals' cadence

On Linux (e6d6b7a) the session test read the counters right after the store showed the polls and
found one poll and one pass: the pump's first pass had drained the backfilled lines, the first
poll and the announcement together, published its snapshot, and the next publication was not
due for 500 ms, while the test's other reads (the rows, the series, the health) were satisfied
by the store or by that same first snapshot. The test now waits for the counters as it waits
for everything else the pump publishes; the clause already states the cadence.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-09): refuted, the stop's order

The stop joined the pump before the workers, and the pump's final pass drained at most one cap's
worth: the events the workers sent after the pump's last pass, and any backlog past 20 000,
never reached the store (49 613 of 60 000 rows in the critic's probe, the pump held on its
refresh so the backlog was deterministic); and no refresh ran after the workers ended, so a
reader still holding the session saw a stopped source live, ageing into idle, never ended, and
counters up to one refresh old. The stop now ends the workers first, then the pump, whose last
passes drain the channels dry, cap or no cap, and publish a last refresh, every source ended
and the counters final; the clause says so, and one test holds all three. Held by the critic: the
validation through one claim set across the three kinds, a rule's scope against log ids alone,
the table names case-insensitive across four owners naming both, the channel's check and the
store's open before anything spawns, a spawn failure stopping what was spawned, the drain cap
never sleeping on a full pass, the keys capped at 64 and flagged, the frames numeric alone with a
time as epoch milliseconds through a broadcast of 256 that lags a slow listener, a worker's end
winning over every other health state, one gate cloned into every handle.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-09): refuted, a failed worker ended clean by the stop

The stop's last refresh read every worker as ended: the workers' outcomes went to the caller
alone, the runtimes they were read from were taken by then, and a source whose worker had
failed (a family on which no key parses, failed at once with its text) read `Ended` after the
stop, its text gone from the health a shell or the server still holds. Each worker's outcome is
kept at the stop and the last refresh reads it: `Failed` with the text for an error, `Ended` for
a clean end; the clause says "every source ended, or failed with its text". One test holds it.
Held by the critic: a hung worker hangs the stop in either order (no join has a timeout, as
before), the unbounded channels and the dropping broadcast against the inner lock held while
joining, the senders all dropped but the pump's own clones so the drain-dry tail cannot miss a
late event, the double stop idempotent, the frames' sender outliving the session (no clause
claims a listener sees the end).

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-09): holds

No gap at 7c91035: the outcome map keyed as the stats and the reports are for all three kinds,
filled once at the stop and read after the live reports, a worker ended or failed before the
stop the same through it, the drop path the same map, every stats entry owning a worker. Three
claims of the clause had no test, right in the code and shown by the critic's probe: the key cap
at 64 with its flag, the retrying states for a server and a peer away, a slow listener's lag on
the broadcast of 256 while the plant flows on; one test now asserts them (against the plant
stopped before the session starts, and a listener that never reads). Verified after three
rounds; the clause grew at rounds 1 and 2.

## The switch reached only through the session (2026-11-10, from supervisão's C-054)

Supervisão's refute round found that a component serving a session could arm the switch by reaching
into `Live` (`pub gate`). `Live`'s `gate`, `opc` and `channels` are now crate-private: outside portal
the switch is read with `Live::writes_armed` and set with `Session::set_writes_armed`, the handles
reached through `opc_handle` and `channel_handle`. No behaviour changed; the session's and the
server's tests are green unchanged.

## 2026-11-11: the frames carry the series' count

Supervisão's feed (C-050, refute round 5) could not order two samples under one stamp when a frame
was lost to a lag or read after the cache's refresh. Each frame sample now carries `n`, the series'
count with that sample, the count the cache shows once it holds it. The session test reads the ten
appended lines' frames with their exact stamps, values and counts (21 to 30, after the 20 captured);
a mutation sending the count off by one turns it red. The rest of the frame is unchanged.

## Refute of the frames' count (2026-11-11, at 99f66f0): refuted on the test, the code right

The critic traced `note_sample` and found `n` right for every kind of source, a text sample, two
rules on one series, a backfill, a frame split into chunks, a store error and a type change. The
test was the gap: it pinned `n` only on the log's temperature, where the series' count is the line
count, so three mutants stayed green: `n` read from the lines counter, a sample that is not numeric
left uncounted (a text series' count stuck at 0, which supervisão orders cache samples by), and
every OPC sample noted twice (frames carrying counts the cache never shows). It also found the
Verification's "the series cache listing the three sources with the exact type, count, keys, last
index and last value" asserted for the log alone.

The answer is in the test alone. A line no rule reads goes among the ten appended, so the
temperature's 21 to 30 are the series' counts and not the lines read. Every frame sample, from every
source, is kept through the stop: each series framed counts one up from frame to frame, and its
last count is the cache's after the stop's last refresh. The cache's type, count, keys, last index
and last value are asserted as the store holds them for the log's temperature, the server's speed
and word and the machine's item, and the text state's count is 20, then 30. Four mutants each turn
the test red: `n` from the lines counter (the counts jump 25 to 27 across the unread line), a text
sample uncounted, every OPC sample noted twice, every telegram sample noted twice.

## Refute of the frames, round 2 (2026-11-11, at 7488bb3): refuted on the test, the code right

Two words of the frames stayed unpinned. The frame's key was never read (every key dropped, the test
stayed green), though supervisão places keyed samples by it. And "every numeric sample" was checked
only for the series that happened to be framed: framing floats alone (the server's integers never
framed) stayed green. Round 1's wording "every series framed from every source" also overstated:
the machine announces before the test subscribes, so no telegram frame is ever observed.

The answers, in the test: the log's frames carry their exact keys (the line's item), every keyless
sample frames no key, and the log's temperature and the server's speed, run and word must each be
framed, so their count chains are checked too. The Verification now says the log and the server, and
that the machine's item is checked through the cache against the store, not through a frame. Both
mutants turn the test red.
