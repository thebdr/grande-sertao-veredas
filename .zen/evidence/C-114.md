# C-114: `fisica`, a plant's scene at a fixed step

## The engine (docs/questions.md Q160, taken meanwhile)

Rapier 0.36.1 (`rapier3d`, Apache-2.0), pinned, with `enhanced-determinism`. Avian would bring
Bevy's ECS into a bench that has none; Parry alone resolves no contact (a part pushed off a belt,
a chute); no engine at all is enough for a conveyor line, not for a scene. Only
`src/fisica/world.rs` names Rapier, so a change of engine is that module.

## The spike, before the code (2026-11-22)

A scratch crate outside the repository drove Rapier's own `PhysicsWorld`: a 0.3 m carton on a
belt (a fixed box whose contacts get a tangent velocity in `modify_solver_contacts`) and a 1 cm
light barrier as a sensor collider with collision events.
- The carton reached the belt's 0.5 m/s within 0.05 s and kept it.
- The barrier's events came at x = 1.849 (front edge at the beam) and x = 2.157 (back edge past
  it), to the step.
- Two runs agreed bit for bit.
- The tangent velocity is the second collider's relative to the first: with the belt first, +v
  carries the other along +x; the contact normal points from the first to the second (up, on
  the belt's top). With the opposite sign the carton ran off the belt's back end and fell.

## What the test taught

- **A sensor sees a body one step late.** Rapier's step detects contacts first, then solves and
  moves the bodies, so the events of step N describe the poses after step N-1. The reference test
  pins it exactly: at the event's step, the pose one step earlier overlaps the beam and the pose
  two steps earlier does not, entering and leaving. The clause states it rather than running a
  second detection after each step: 4 ms at the default step, under a real sensor's own delay.
- **The pair order.** The world inserts the file's bodies before the belts, so they meet a belt
  as the pair's first collider, and a body added later as its second; the test carries one of
  each at the belt's speed, which proves the hook's sign both ways.
- **A belt's sides stay still.** The hook gives the tangent velocity only to contacts whose normal
  is within 45° of the belt's up; a body standing on the floor against the belt's side does not
  move while the belt runs.
- **A sleeping body on a stopped belt.** Rapier puts a body at rest to sleep; a belt started
  again would leave it there. A speed change wakes every body.
- **Penetration** (corrected by refute round 2). The world once set Rapier's
  `normalized_allowed_linear_error` to 1 mm, taking it for an allowed penetration; in Rapier 0.36.1
  only the CCD solver reads it, and penetrations are corrected all the way to zero, so the setting
  was inert and is gone. What holds, measured: a box rests 0.07 mm into a fixed floor whatever its
  mass and size; a mover pushing a tonne at 1.5 m/s holds it 2.04 mm into its face (one step's
  travel is 6 mm); a body on a lighter one sinks into it (2.6 mm at ten times its mass, 26 mm at a
  hundred, 157 mm at a thousand), the solver's iterations shared along the stack.
- **Numbers worked out by hand.** The free fall's velocity is g t to f32 precision; its drop is
  g t²/2 within g dt t/2 (an integrator that moves after it accelerates). On a 40° slope at
  friction 0.6 a body slid 1.093 m between 0.1 s and 1.1 s against 1.078 m worked out from
  g (sin 40° − 0.6 cos 40°); on 20° it held under 2 mm.
- **A test's own errors, found on the way**: a yawed belt's `size` is along its own axes (the
  first scene gave the cross belt 0.6 m of running length and the body fell past it); a slide
  measured from 0.1 s carries the speed it had gained by then.

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-23): refuted, answered

The critic found four code defects and twelve claims no assertion held:
- **A slow belt stalled what lay on it**: Rapier 0.36.1 puts a body to sleep once it has moved
  slower than about 0.1 m/s for half a second, and a sleeping body's contacts are not solved. A
  carton on a belt at 0.01 m/s moved 3 mm in 5 s instead of 50. Every body touching a running belt
  is now woken before each step; belts from 0.01 to 0.5 m/s carry within 2 %.
- **The belt's underside carried too** (an absolute value let its own -z face count): the normal is
  now turned out of the belt and must point along its up.
- **A huge finite angle made the pose NaN** (yaw 1e300) or a wrong one (1e10 read as 80.46°):
  angles are reduced to a turn in double precision before use.
- **Time and the runner wrapped past 2^32 steps** (`steps as u32`, a saturating u32 count): both
  count in 64 bits now; reflexo's own runner likewise.
- **Pinned now** (the critic's probes, kept as the test's `round_1` module): the event cap and its
  order, late steps counted, drop ending the thread, hooks before the step, friction's mean, mass,
  a belt's side and underside, slow belts, movers and lifts, the angle order and its read-back
  ranges, a removal's leavings in order, sleep inside a sensor, a refused place, two defaults.
- **Wording**: a removal's leavings follow its step's events; angles read back within their
  ranges; only a step more than one step late counts late; a value of another kind refused as not
  TOML (the reference says so).

## The calm second under the whole suite (2026-11-25)

The orchestrator's whole-suite run at 71ca704 had the runner test red once: "6 of 274 steps counted
late in a calm second", against a bound of five. The test already ran with the suite's own tests
held off (`threads-required = 'num-cpus'`); a step of the reference scene costs 0.46 ms on average

and in round 4's at 10 and 20 ms. A runner counting every step late fails both (250 of 250 against
0); five runs under the lock and one beside eight busy loops passed.
