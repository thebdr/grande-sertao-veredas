# C-052 evidence - the dashboards

## The design (2026-11-09)

Veredas 1's dashboard posted a layout to the gateway and computed nothing: the page charted raw
windows with uPlot. Here a panel is TOML in the binding and every number it shows is computed in
Rust from the session's store (`panel.rs`), so the test asserts each bucket, breach, run, median
and bin exactly from lines it writes with chosen stamps; the page draws plain SVG. A window's right
edge is a parameter (`to`, the store's now by default), which is what makes the numbers exact in a
test. Bars are aligned on the clock and the bucket holding `to` is the running one. State over time
reads each sample of the object's own series by the rules over its own value (a rule over another
series has no history to read here). Charts in cells read a table's rows over the window (at most
the newest 50 000, said when capped), group by a key column, and give per key the median, σ
(population), n, the median per bucket and twenty bins from median − spread to median + spread.

## Verified in the browser (2026-11-09)

Against the demo plant of C-051: the eleven panels drawn and refreshed every five seconds, the four
tiles (normal against a target with its sub-line, "slow", the infeed's code), the trend with its
dashed limit and breach dots and a hover value, the bars per minute with the running one outlined
and the dashed target, the infeed's states over time, the grid, the levels with their ticks, the
table sorted by count with inline bars and "■ slow" words, and the charts in cells per outlet.

Screenshots: `.zen/evidence/supervisao/dashboard.jpg` (tiles in alarm, normal, warn and alarm, the

## Refute round 1 (2026-11-10, at 6e5c215): refuted, eight gaps in the test, none in the code

Nine one-line mutants of `panel.rs` and one that ignored three windows stayed green. Each gap is now
pinned: the table sorted by a column whose order differs from the view's (travel, descending: 3, 2,
1); the windows' edges read with two more right edges and no new data (the trend's totals, the
timeline's runs cut at the window, the cells' counts, the bars' first bucket whole from its clock

at 0 (50 to 150); a limit above with a point equal to it (not a breach); the stale tile's age
between 1 s and the time since the lines were written; a value below its target ("-1%"); and the
cells' cap, now parameters of `panel::cells` (the route keeps the store's pages and 50 000): the
newest five of nine kept across pages of two and reported capped, all nine across five pages not.

## Refute round 2 (2026-11-10, at 9ef85df): refuted, one defect and six gaps

One defect: the timeline read only the samples inside its window, so a state that began before the
window and still held was not drawn (to=at(67) began its runs at at(10), not at the window's start
at(7); a window with no change in it was empty while the line ran the whole hour). Now each row
opens with the state holding at the window's start, from its last sample within the day before
(`TIMELINE_LOOKBACK_MS`); the reference says so.

Six gaps in the test. The page's chart geometry was never read (sparklines drawn flat, the trend
spaced by index and upside down, breach dots on the floor, the limit at the top, bars from counts
and shifted, runs at equal widths, a distribution upside down: all green); the reader now reads
every vertex, dot, bar, run and line and places each by the chart frame `charts.ts` states,
compared within the page's rounding. Breaches and the total were only shown at nine points: a dense
series of 400 with five neighbouring breaches is now charted. Only five-minute buckets were tried:
hourly and daily buckets from a window off that grid, and the cells' sparkline by the quarter hour.
The table's sort with numbers of different lengths and nulls (`sort_rows`, unit-tested). A level
past its range (`over`, 50..85 at 100, a tick at 90). The default window's edge now lies between
two readings of the store's now.

Replayed one by one against the new test with the critic's patterns: M1, M2, M4, M5 (bars by
count), M5b (target on the floor), M6, M7, R1 to R4, T1 (breaches from the downsampled line), B1,
B2 and the seed removed: every one red. M3 (breach dots on the floor) did not build (the critic's
own pattern fails `tsc`, as their report says), so it was not run.

## Refute round 3 (2026-11-10, at 32a9590): refuted, one defect and five gaps

The defect: the cells panel paged the window by offset, newest first, so a row stored between two
page reads (stamped inside the window, newer than the rows already read) shifted the order by one:
one row read twice, the oldest missed, the counts wrong under a heading saying nothing was capped
(23 of 53 reads in the critic's probe while rows arrived fast; at a plant's pace, a row now and
then, cleared at the next refresh). It now pages by stamp: each page reads the rows at or before
the last stamp taken, past those already taken at that stamp, so a row stamped after it never
shifts a page; a table without `index_ms` keeps the offset. A test reads three hundred sorts two
rows a page, over and over, while a hundred and fifty arrive at the newest end: each counted once
every time (offset paging put back turns it red).

The gaps, each a mutation that stayed green: which points a downsample kept (the first 300, or the
first 60 of a sparkline, passed the count); the day's lookback (seven minutes passed); a second
object on a timeline (one row passed); every mark's extent on the page (a limit or target line of
no length, a breach of no radius, runs of no height passed: only their places were read); and the
cap's note, never drawn on the page (its panel was never capped there). The answers: the dense
trend's line and a value tile over the dense series equal portal's own downsampling of the
window's samples, ends and peak named; the line held from 22 hours before a window, and nothing from
25; travel's six runs beside the line's; lines read end to end across the plot, dots by their
radius, runs by their height; and the page's fetch hands the cells panel over past its cap, its
note read in the heading.

## Refute round 4 (2026-11-11, at f164a98): refuted, two gaps in the test, the code right

No rows shared a stamp, so the code that skips the rows already taken at a page's bound stamp never
ran: offsetting by one instead stayed green, though a log stamped to the second makes ties and the
route pages by 2 000 rows (a probe read six rows a hundred times under a heading saying nothing was
capped). And the page's running bar was compared only when its bucket held a value: the latest
sample sits 30.5 s into its five minutes, so about one run in ten drew no running bar and a page
never marking it stayed green.

The answers, in the test: after the arrivals, six sorts with four at one stamp between two others,
read two rows a page, each read once (every key's count exact, read equal to total, not capped);
before the page opens, a sort stamped at the store's now (outlet 2, leaving every drawn figure as it
stood; within 20 s of a bucket's end the next bucket is waited for), and the page's bars must carry
exactly one marked running. Both mutants turn the test red.

## Refute round 5 (2026-11-11, at 8b48c81): refuted, one defect and one gap

The defect: a row stored at the very stamp a page ended on sorts ahead of the rows already read
there (the store breaks a tie by every column, newest arrival first), so the next page, skipping by
the count read at that stamp, started that many rows early and read them again (the critic counted
outlet 7 fifteen times for its ten rows, read 315 of a total of 310). The gap: no level or tick was
ever below its range's start, so a fraction held at the end alone (a level under its range drawn as
a full bar) stayed green.

The answers. The paging keeps its offset (a newcomer only moves the rows read later, so a page never
starts past a row not yet read) and takes each row once by its source and sequence, which every base
table carries; rows read again are skipped. The test reads two hundred rows of outlet 7 at the
window's newest stamp again and again while a hundred and fifty arrive at that stamp ten
milliseconds apart: outlet 7 counted exactly two hundred in every read, no key past the rows stored
for it (the old paging counted 350 in both runs of the mutant). A level `under` (35 against 85..120)
reads 0 with its 80 tick held at 0 and its 90 at 5/35; dropping the lower clamp turns it red.

Also learned: a writer that keeps appending at the stamp being paged until the reads are done
starves the reads (a page of two cannot outrun a row every 5 ms); the test bounds the writer.
